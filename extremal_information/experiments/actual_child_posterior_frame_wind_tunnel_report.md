# Actual-child posterior-frame wind tunnel

## Question and finite scope

This audit asks whether the near-rank-one posterior matrices arising from the
**actual contracted-temperature minimizing children** possess a reusable
low-information frame.  It tests three concrete candidates:

1. a frame fixed by the two child spectral decompositions;
2. a frame generated by the elementary bridge fields `B 1` and `B^T 1`;
3. a bounded orbit under the natural signed symmetries of the children.

The test uses `beta=4`, `lambda=1`, splits `3+3`, `4+4`, and `3+7`, and the
complete bridge cube in every reported case.  Both orientations are evaluated
through `N=8`; the larger `N=10` test uses orientation `+1`.  All child
signings are selected by exhaustive signing and exact energy-histogram
enumeration.  The nontrivial pressure comparisons are interval separated at
80 decimal digits.  The SVD-derived conclusions are numerical finite
evidence, not interval-certified or asymptotic statements.

## Exact posterior reconstruction

Write `L(B)` for bridge log pressure, `t=beta/sqrt(N)`, and
`rho=tanh(t)`.  For edge `e`, bridge sign `b_e`, and edge-flipped bridge
`B^e`, the deleted-edge cavity response and full posterior mean are

```text
r_e(B_-e) = b_e tanh((L(B)-L(B^e))/2) / rho,
M_e(B)    = (r_e(B_-e)+rho b_e)/(1+rho b_e r_e(B_-e)).
```

The program verifies on the complete cube that `r_e` is unchanged after the
deleted bit is flipped and that `|M_e| <= 1` to numerical tolerance.  On three
deterministic bridges per record it also recomputes every posterior coordinate
by a direct Gibbs sum; the largest reconstruction error is `1.7e-15`.  All
statistics below are weighted by

```text
q_lambda(B) = exp(-lambda L(B)) / sum_C exp(-lambda L(C)).
```

## Result 1: low rank is present

Let `sigma_1 >= sigma_2 >= ...` be the singular values of `M(B)`.  The
posterior matrices are often strongly rank one:

| `N`, split, orientation | median `sigma_1/sqrt(mn)` | median `sigma_1^2/||M||_F^2` |
|---|---:|---:|
| `6`, `3+3`, `-` | 0.446789 | 0.742065 |
| `6`, `3+3`, `+` | 0.383550 | 0.587745 |
| `8`, `4+4`, either | 0.575973 | 0.873324 |
| `10`, `3+7`, `+` | 0.556166 | 0.865065 |

Thus the following failures are failures of **frame synchronization**, not a
claim that each posterior matrix has high numerical rank.

## Result 2: the child spectral frame fails

For `G_L=MM^T` and `G_R=M^TM`, define the basis-independent commutator ratios

```text
c_L = ||A G_L-G_L A||_F / (2 ||A||_op ||G_L||_F),
c_R = ||D G_R-G_R D||_F / (2 ||D||_op ||G_R||_F).
```

Zero is necessary and sufficient for the corresponding posterior Gram matrix
to preserve the child's spectral eigenspaces.  The negative escort places the
following mass on `c>0.1`:

| `N`, split, orientation | `q(c_L>0.1)` | `q(c_R>0.1)` |
|---|---:|---:|
| `6`, `3+3`, `-` | 0.523041 | 0.523041 |
| `6`, `3+3`, `+` | 0.841978 | 0.841978 |
| `8`, `4+4`, `-` | 0.919640 | 0.919640 |
| `8`, `4+4`, `+` | 0.919640 | 0.919640 |
| `10`, `3+7`, `+` | 0.822697 | 0.994321 |

In particular, at `N=10` the right fixed-child frame fails at normalized
commutator level `0.1` on more than `99.4%` of the actual escort mass.  This is
a decisive finite falsifier of the fixed child spectral-frame candidate.

## Result 3: elementary bridge fields do not generate the frame

The naive field candidate predicts left and right leading singular vectors
from `B1` and `B^T1`.  At `N=10`, the escort masses with alignment below
`0.5` are respectively

```text
q(|<u_1,B1>|/||B1|| < 0.5)       = 0.417845,
q(|<v_1,B^T1>|/||B^T1|| < 0.5)   = 0.436099.
```

This is not a tie artifact.  After also requiring the relative leading gap
`(sigma_1-sigma_2)/sigma_1 > 0.05`, the corresponding unconditional masses
remain `0.411551` and `0.428074`.  At `N=8` the gap-filtered masses are both
`0.308974`.  A large, unambiguous part of the actual escort therefore has a
leading frame not supplied by these elementary fields.

## Result 4: the natural symmetry quotient is already large

The natural group consists of simultaneous signed similarities of the two
children with common multiplier `delta`, acting on `B` with the same
multiplier, together with global bridge negation.  The program counts its
bridge orbits exactly by Burnside's lemma:

| split | bridge dimension | group order | exact natural bridge orbits | rounded singular spectra (7 digits) | `exp(H_q(spectrum))` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `3+3` | 9 | 72 | 18 | 13 | 3.62--4.35 |
| `4+4` | 16 | 64 | 1,438 | 793 | 268.16 |
| `3+7` | 21 | 144 | 16,148 | 16,148 | 6,189.44 |

The `N=10` rounded-spectrum count is stable at 7 and 8 decimal digits; at 6
digits it is 16,147.  Hence, at this finite frontier, even the singular values
numerically distinguish essentially every natural symmetry orbit.  This does
not prove an asymptotic entropy rate, but it rules out the proposed bounded
natural orbit and makes clear that symmetry alone has not synchronized the
adaptive frames.

## Research judgment

The experiment rejects all three concrete low-information frames while
preserving the positive fact that individual posterior matrices are usually
close to rank one.  The narrower unresolved issue is therefore not nuclear
compression by itself.  It is a quantitative **adaptive-frame
synchronization/response-entropy theorem**, followed by a conversion of that
frame information into the directional quantity required by the recurrence.

No asymptotic conclusion is claimed.  A different optimizer-specific frame
could still exist, and the finite orbit growth alone is not a lower bound at
orders tending to infinity.
