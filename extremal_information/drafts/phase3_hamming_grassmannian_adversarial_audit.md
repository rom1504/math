# Adversarial audit: coding barrier and sparse-flat law

**Status.** Independent audit.  HG.1--HG.2, HG.5--HG.6, SF.4, SF.9, and
the rooted-lift examples survive.  One research-level inference in the coding
barrier needs correction: an upper bound on packing cardinality does not by
itself prove that the puncturing quotient is a nonminimal response summary.
The missing conclusion can instead be proved by an explicit probabilistic
covering bound, stated below.

The audit used the two existing exhaustive verifiers and the independent
line/alphabet verifier in
[`verify_phase3_hamming_grassmannian_falsifier.py`](../experiments/verify_phase3_hamming_grassmannian_falsifier.py).

## 1. Coding-barrier audit

### HG.1--HG.2: valid, including the low-weight exception

For binary lines `L_v={0,v}` and `L_w={0,w}`,

```math
d_{\rm Hs}(L_v,L_w)
=\min\{\operatorname{wt}(v+w),
       \max(\operatorname{wt}(v),\operatorname{wt}(w))\}.     \tag{A.1}
```

This is equivalent to HG.1 and HG.2.  It implies both facts used in the
coding sandwich:

* representatives of two packed lines are more than `t` apart;
* at most one representative can have weight at most `t`.

If a light representative exists, replacing it by zero preserves cardinality
and gives a distance-`t+1` code because all remaining representatives are
heavy.  Thus the proof of

```math
A_2(D,t+1)-1\le\Pi_{D,1}(t)\le A_2(D,t+1)
```

is correct.  Independent exact maximum-clique computations through `D=6`
obey the sandwich at every integer threshold.

### HG.3: the numerical gap is correct, but the nonminimality inference is not

The inequalities

```math
\log_2\Pi_{D,1}(\lfloor\delta D\rfloor)
\le(1-H_2(\delta/2)+o(1))D                                  \tag{A.2}
```

and

```math
(D-t)-\log_2\Pi_{D,1}(t)
\ge(H_2(\delta/2)-\delta-o(1))D                             \tag{A.3}
```

are correct.  Puncturing `t` coordinates has exactly `2^(D-t)` possible
zero-or-line images, and the coefficient is strictly positive.

However, (A.3) compares one quotient's number of states with a **maximum
packing number**.  A packing upper bound is not a covering upper bound and
does not prove that a smaller summary exists.  Therefore the sentences

> “the Hamming upper bound proves a strict state-count overestimate”

and

> “the anticode quotient is rigorously nonminimal”

need either to be weakened to “the quotient count strictly exceeds the
maximum packing count” or replaced by the covering theorem below.

### Proposition A.1 (a genuine strict line-response quotient improvement)

Let `N_{D,1}(t)` be the minimum number of closed radius-`t` Hausdorff balls
covering all binary lines.  With

```math
V_D(t)=\sum_{i=0}^t{D\choose i},
```

one has

```math
\boxed{
N_{D,1}(t)
\le
{(2^D-1)(\ln(2^D-1)+1)\over V_D(t)}+1.
}                                                               \tag{A.4}
```

Consequently, for fixed `0<delta<1/2`,

```math
\log_2N_{D,1}(\lfloor\delta D\rfloor)
\le(1-H_2(\delta)+o(1))D,                                    \tag{A.5}
```

so puncturing uses at least

```math
(H_2(\delta)-\delta-o(1))D                                  \tag{A.6}
```

more bits than an explicitly existing radius-`t` metric summary.  This is a
valid proof of nonminimality and is stronger than (A.3).

#### Proof

The exact line-ball formula HG.4 shows that every radius-`t` ball has at
least `V_D(t)` members.  For a heavy center it has exactly that size.  For a
light nonzero center it is the union of two distinct Hamming balls of size
`V_D(t)`, with zero removed, and again has size at least `V_D(t)`.

Independently select each of the `2^D-1` line centers with probability

```math
p={\ln(2^D-1)+1\over V_D(t)}
```

(the trivial all-center cover handles `p>1`).  Symmetry of the metric means
each line belongs to at least `V_D(t)` candidate balls.  Its probability of
remaining uncovered is at most

```math
(1-p)^{V_D(t)}\le e^{-pV_D(t)}<{1\over2^D-1}.
```

Add every uncovered line as a center.  Expected total size is at most the
right side of (A.4), so such a cover exists.  The entropy asymptotic gives
(A.5), and comparison with `D-t` gives (A.6). `square`

If the response query is 1-Lipschitz in carrier Hausdorff distance, storing
the nearest center in this cover is the corresponding approximate response
summary.  This is the missing operational step in the original inference.

### HG.5--HG.6: valid with the stated thresholds

The systematic-chart argument is sound.  For graph carriers generated by
`[I_k | X]` and `[I_k | Y]`, matching the same input row `u` gives

```math
d_{\rm Hs}(C_X,C_Y)
\le\max_u\operatorname{wt}(u(X-Y))
\le |\{j:X_j\ne Y_j\}|.                                    \tag{A.7}
```

Thus a `>t` carrier packing maps to a `2^k`-ary code of symbol distance at
least `t+1`.  Choosing one canonical information set partitions the
Grassmannian, so the factor `binom(D,k)` is legitimate even though a carrier
can possess several information sets.

The threshold restrictions are also correct:

* HG.9 holds for all `t`;
* Singleton gives HG.10 only for `t<=D-k`;
* the sphere radius in HG.11 is `floor(t/2)` because the symbol-code minimum
  distance is `t+1`;
* the Reed--Solomon saturation in HG.6 requires `2^k>=D-k`, and the endpoint
  `t=D-k` is correctly handled by a singleton.

Exhaustive graph-carrier comparisons in the existing verifier find no
counterexample.  The warning after HG.6 is essential: MDS column words need
not be a Hausdorff packing because (A.7) is one-sided.

## 2. Sparse-flat audit

### SF.4: exact

Fix `J=C cap C_0` with dimension `k-ell` and `U=pi(C)` with dimension `ell`.
After choosing a section over `U`, the possible `C/J` are precisely the
graphs of maps

```math
U\longrightarrow C_0/J.
```

Both spaces have dimension `ell`, giving `q^(ell^2)` possibilities.  The
condition `h^to(C,C_0)<=Delta` is exactly `U subseteq L_(C_0)(Delta)`.
This proves every factor in SF.4; exhaustive enumeration at `(D,k)=(4,2)`
and `(5,2)` agrees for every center and integer threshold.

### SF.9: valid, but it is deliberately cardinality-only

Every quotient leader has an ambient representative of norm at most
`Delta`, proving SF.5.  Counting ordered bases gives SF.6, even though the
leader ball need not be a subspace.  The two product estimates

```math
{k\brack\ell}_q\le4q^{\ell(k-\ell)},
\qquad
{q^{\ell^2}\over|\operatorname{GL}(\ell,q)|}<4
```

give SF.7.  A symmetric ball is contained in its directed ball, so greedy
deletion yields SF.9 with the claimed strict `>Delta` packing threshold.
The optimization in SF.10--SF.11 is also correct.  In particular, when
`H_2(delta)<kappa`, direct maximization gives the deficit
`(kappa-H_2(delta))^2/4` relative to the common-host GV exponent.

This validates the theorem but also its limitation: SF.9 throws away the
flat spectrum immediately through `Lambda<=|L|^ell/|GL|`; it cannot by
itself test whether sparse-flat incidence improves the exponent.

### Rooted-lift counterexample: valid with one scope caveat

The quotients by `span(1100)` and `span(1000)` are both isometric to the
three-dimensional Hamming cube.  Their direct sums therefore have identical
quotient normed spaces and all sparse-flat spectra, while their kernel
diameters are respectively `2r` and `r`.  The finite symmetric radius-one
line-ball sizes `5` and `7` were independently reproduced.

Thus the counterexample is a correct scalable obstruction to recovering
**labelled symmetric response geometry** from the unrooted quotient norm.
If a particular interface forbids the zero carrier as a future query or
restricts all queries to the same Grassmannian, the zero-carrier comparison
alone does not apply; the finite same-dimension ball-size witness still proves
two-sided insufficiency, but a same-dimension scalable response witness would
then be an additional theorem target.  The present draft should state this
scope explicitly rather than treating every query model as identical.

## 3. Audit of finite-alphabet amplification

The seven displayed two-planes are pairwise distinct and jointly span a
five-dimensional subspace of `F_2^6`.  Exhaustive comparison gives

```math
\delta^\to(L_i,L_j)=3\qquad(i\ne j)
```

in each orientation.  Therefore for words `u,v in[7]^m`, direct-sum
additivity gives the exact identity

```math
d_{\rm H}(C_u,C_v)=3d_{[7]^m}(u,v),
```

not merely a lower bound.  A seven-letter code of distance
`d_m=ceil(3m/4)` hence gives strict carrier threshold

```math
\Delta_m=3d_m-1,
\qquad
{\Delta_m\over6m}\longrightarrow{3\over8}.
```

The finite certificate proves existence only; it does not prove that the
alphabet is unique or maximal.  Exhaustive length-two products (1,176 pairs)
confirm the direct-sum threshold.

Two scope corrections are essential.

1. Every product carrier contains a nonzero word of weight at most four in a
   single block.  Its exclusion from any minimum-distance-`>Delta_m` host is
   therefore immediate; the Hamming bound separately proves that no
   `2m`-dimensional such host exists at all.
2. Here `k=2m`, so the mixed-profile presentation toll is `2k=4m`, while the
   carrier gap is only about `9m/4`.  The carrier law gives no separated
   response profiles.  The construction falsifies common-host control of the
   **pure Grassmannian Hausdorff packing**, not common-host control of the
   presented multichannel response rate.

The general finite-alphabet response theorem repairs the second issue when a
better local alphabet is available.  If there are `q` local carriers with
both directed gaps at least `d` and presentation radius `p`, then an outer
code of relative distance `rho` has product-response gap

```math
(d-p)\rho m.
```

Indeed, if `omega(i,j)=max_x(f_i(x)-f_j(x))`, exact product factorization gives

```math
\|F_u-F_v\|_\infty
=\max\left\{\sum_t\omega(u_t,v_t),
             \sum_t\omega(v_t,u_t)\right\}.
```

Each directed carrier witness gives the corresponding signed local response
margin `d-p`; the identity then maximizes all local differences with the same
sign before taking the absolute value.  This joint same-sign recoupling
strictly improves the coarse global carrier estimate
`(d rho-p)m` by `p(1-rho)m`.  The positivity window is simply
`d>p` and `0<rho<1-1/q`.

The two proposed witnesses survive exhaustive audit:

* the seven nonzero words of the binary `[7,3,4]` simplex code give Hamming
  lines with `d=4,p=2`, hence residual gap at least `3m/2` at `rho=3/4`;
* the seven nonzero multiplication maps of `F_8` give rank-metric lines with
  `d=3,p=2`, hence residual gap at least `3m/4`.

All 42 directed pairs in each alphabet were checked directly.  These are
genuine presented-response families, unlike the seven-plane construction.

## 4. Audit judgment

The coding sandwich and sparse-flat ball identity are solid.  The most
important correction is logical rather than numerical:

```math
\text{packing upper bound}\not\Longrightarrow
\text{smaller summary exists}.
```

Proposition A.1 repairs the issue by constructing a smaller metric cover.  It
also sharpens the line-slice lesson: at macroscopic distortion, the response
covering exponent is at most the Gilbert exponent `1-H_2(delta)`, while the
puncturing quotient has exponent `1-delta`.  The sparse-flat result remains a
correct exact ball law, and its rooted-lift counterexample correctly shows
that quotient incidence without embedding data is not a reusable symmetric
state.  Finally, finite-alphabet amplification supplies the missing positive
composition law: two-sided directed exposure survives repeated products
whenever its local directed gap exceeds the presentation toll; outer coding
then supplies positive query mass without charging matching coordinates.
