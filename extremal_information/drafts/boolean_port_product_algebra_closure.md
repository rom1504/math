# Odd product-algebra closure for Boolean trust responses

**Status.**  Rigorous sufficient theorem, exact composition laws, a dense
regular-Hadamard family with growing port arity and sublinear exact state,
and a finite verifier.  The theorem identifies an arbitrary-arity class on
which the projective row histogram is an exact labelled Boolean trust
carrier.  It generalizes the automatic two-port and triple-product
three-port formulas and explains what fails in the four-port equal-Gram
collision.

## 1. A selector and its active product algebra

Let `H` be real symmetric with `||H||_op<=r`, and let
`w_1,...,w_p in {+-1}^n`.  For nonempty `S subseteq [p]`, write

```math
w_S=\bigodot_{i\in S}w_i.                           \tag{PC.1}
```

Fix a Boolean selector `tau:{+-1}^p -> {+-1}` which is antipodally odd and
agrees with majority away from ties:

```math
\tau(-a)=-\tau(a),
\qquad
\tau(a)=\operatorname {sgn}(\sum_i a_i)
\quad\hbox{when }\sum_i a_i\ne0.                   \tag{PC.2}
```

For odd `p` this is ordinary majority.  For even `p`, choose one value on
each antipodal pair in the zero layer; `tau(a)=a_1` on that layer is one
canonical choice.  Let

```math
\mathcal A_\tau=
\{S\subseteq[p]:\widehat\tau(S)\ne0\}.             \tag{PC.3}
```

Antipodal oddness implies that every set in `A_tau` has odd size.  Call the
port tuple **positive `tau`-product closed** when

```math
Hw_i=rw_i\quad(i\le p),
\qquad
Hw_S=rw_S\quad(S\in\mathcal A_\tau).              \tag{PC.4}
```

The first list is stated separately because a chosen selector can have a
zero singleton Fourier coefficient, while the spherical formula below
still needs every port in the active eigenspace.  Repeated conditions are
harmless.  There are at most `2^(p-1)` product identities; at fixed `p`
they neither contain the old Boolean energy landscape nor require
optimization over its `2^n` spins.

For an endpoint word `epsilon in {+-1}^p`, put

```math
z_\epsilon=\sum_i\epsilon_iw_i.                    \tag{PC.5}
```

Define labelled Boolean and spherical trust channels by

```math
\begin{aligned}
B_\epsilon
 &=\max_{x\in\{+-1\}^n,\ \sigma\in\{+-1\}}
 \left\{{\sigma\over2}x^THx+mz_\epsilon^Tx\right\},\\
S_\epsilon
 &=\max_{\|u\|_2^2=n,\ \sigma\in\{+-1\}}
 \left\{{\sigma\over2}u^THu+mz_\epsilon^Tu\right\}.
                                                               \tag{PC.6}
\end{aligned}
```

## 2. Exact trust closure

### Theorem PC.1 (`tau`-product-algebra closure)

Under (PC.4), every labelled endpoint channel is exact:

```math
\boxed{
B_\epsilon={rn\over2}+m\|z_\epsilon\|_1,
\qquad
S_\epsilon={rn\over2}+m\sqrt n\|z_\epsilon\|_2.}  \tag{PC.7}
```

Consequently the complete Boolean trust table factors through the
projective row histogram `mu_W` of PF.1, while the spherical table factors
through the port Gram matrix.  In particular,

```math
\max_\epsilon B_\epsilon
={rn\over2}+m\max_\epsilon\|W\epsilon\|_1.         \tag{PC.8}
```

For `m>0`, equality of all labelled trust channels for two closed systems
of the same `n,r,m` is equivalent to equality of their projective
histograms: subtract `rn/2` in (PC.7) and apply the invertible PF.1
convolution.  Thus the histogram is the exact contextual quotient on the
closed class, up to a bijective re-encoding.

#### Proof

The fixed selector has Fourier expansion

```math
\tau(a)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal A_\tau}\widehat\tau(S)
             \prod_{i\in S}a_i.                   \tag{PC.9}
```

For each endpoint word define a **specific** Boolean witness, not merely a
word maximizing aggregate support:

```math
x_\epsilon(j)=\tau(\epsilon_1w_1(j),\ldots,
                         \epsilon_pw_p(j)).         \tag{PC.10}
```

By (PC.4) and (PC.9), `x_epsilon` is a linear combination of positive top
eigenvectors, so

```math
Hx_\epsilon=rx_\epsilon.                           \tag{PC.11}
```

Where `z_epsilon(j)` is nonzero the selector is its sign; on a tie the
field contribution is zero regardless of the chosen antipodal tie value.
Therefore

```math
z_\epsilon^Tx_\epsilon=\|z_\epsilon\|_1.          \tag{PC.12}
```

The positive quadratic channel at this same witness attains both separate
upper bounds `rn/2` and `m||z_epsilon||_1`, proving the Boolean formula.
The singleton assumptions put `z_epsilon` in the positive top eigenspace,
so `sqrt(n)z_epsilon/||z_epsilon||_2` simultaneously attains the spherical
child and field bounds when the field is nonzero.  In the zero case any
positive top port attains `rn/2`.  This proves (PC.7). `square`

## 3. Intrinsic affine-coset form

There is a selector-independent stronger condition.  Fix `w_1` and let

```math
\mathcal C=\left\langle w_1\odot w_i:2\le i\le p\right\rangle
                                                               \tag{PC.13}
```

be the coordinatewise multiplicative group generated by the relative
ports.  Then

```math
\boxed{
\{w_S:|S|\text{ odd}\}=w_1\mathcal C.}            \tag{PC.14}
```

Indeed, for `T subseteq {2,...,p}`,

```math
w_1\prod_{i\in T}(w_1\odot w_i)
=w_1^{|T|+1}\prod_{i\in T}w_i,                    \tag{PC.15}
```

which is the odd product indexed by `{1} union T` when `|T|` is even and
by `T` when `|T|` is odd.  Thus **full odd-product closure** is exactly the
intrinsic containment

```math
w_1\mathcal C\subseteq
E_+(H)\cap\{+-1\}^n.                               \tag{PC.16}
```

This exposes the connection to affine multiplicative sets of Boolean top
poles.  It is stronger than necessary for one fixed selector, whose Fourier
support can omit some odd products, but it makes closure independent of tie
choices and especially transparent under tensor product.

## 4. Disjoint and tensor composition

Suppose two systems with the same `p,m,r` are `tau`-closed.  Their
block-diagonal child and concatenated corresponding ports are again closed.
Every active product is the concatenation of the two child products, and

```math
\mu_{W_1\sqcup W_2}=\mu_{W_1}+\mu_{W_2},
\qquad
B_\epsilon-{r(n_1+n_2)\over2}
=\sum_{a=1}^2\left(B_\epsilon^{(a)}-{rn_a\over2}\right).
                                                               \tag{PC.17}
```

There is also a genuine tensor law.

### Corollary PC.2 (tensor closure)

Let `(H;w_1,...,w_p)` and `(K;v_1,...,v_p)` be positive `tau`-closed, with
top eigenvalues `r` and `s`.  Then

```math
(H\otimes K;\ w_1\otimes v_1,\ldots,w_p\otimes v_p) \tag{PC.18}
```

is positive `tau`-closed with top eigenvalue `rs`.

Its exact histogram state has the intrinsic tensor law

```math
\boxed{
\mu_{W\boxtimes V}(g)
=\sum_{st=g}\mu_W(s)\mu_V(t)
=:(\mu_W*_G\mu_V)(g),}                            \tag{PC.18a}
```

the unnormalized group convolution on `G_p`.  Here `W boxtimes V` denotes
the tuple of corresponding tensor ports in (PC.18), not a Cartesian union
of all pairs of ports.

In particular, if `Kv=sv` for one Boolean pole and every port is amplified
as `w_i'=w_i\otimes v`, then for odd `S`

```math
w_S'=w_S\otimes v,                                 \tag{PC.19}
```

so full odd closure is preserved, the projective histogram is multiplied
by the order of `K`, and every normalized labelled field response is
unchanged.

#### Proof

For every active odd `S`, the product port in (PC.18) is
`w_S\otimes v_S`, a positive `rs` eigenvector.  Singleton ports obey the
same identity.  A row indexed by `(alpha,beta)` has port pattern

```math
(w_i(\alpha)v_i(\beta))_{i\le p}
=(w_i(\alpha))_{i\le p}\odot(v_i(\beta))_{i\le p}. \tag{PC.18b}
```

Projectivizing commutes with coordinatewise multiplication, so counting
row pairs proves (PC.18a).  The pole-amplification assertions follow from
`v^|S|=v` for odd `|S|`; multiplying every row pattern by the tail sign
does not change its projective type. `square`

## 5. Arity boundary

For `p=1,2`, full odd-product closure adds nothing beyond the top-port
conditions.  At `p=3`, the only new identity is

```math
H(w_1\odot w_2\odot w_3)=r(w_1\odot w_2\odot w_3), \tag{PC.20}
```

which is TC.2.  The degree-two identity for three-input absolute majority
then reduces the histogram response further to the Gram formula.

At `p=4`, four triple products enter.  The regular-Hadamard tuples in
EG.2/TC.3 fail this closure: their hidden fourth-order row statistic gives
equal `(G,R)` but support values `2n` and `7n/4`.  This demonstrates that
product closure, or a synchronization theorem implying it on exposed
channels, is genuine extra structure rather than a consequence of pairwise
Gram--Rayleigh data.

## 6. A dense growing-arity exact-sign family

The order-16 TC.2 seed has four Boolean positive top poles

```math
\mathcal A_0=\{a,b,c,a\odot b\odot c\}
=a\mathcal C_0,
\qquad
\mathcal C_0=\langle a\odot b,a\odot c\rangle,     \tag{PC.21}
```

inside the `+4` eigenspace of the regular Hadamard matrix `H_16`.  The group
`C_0` has rank two.  Tensor `j` copies.  With

```math
H_j=H_{16}^{\otimes j},
\qquad n_j=16^j,
\qquad r_j=4^j,                                    \tag{PC.22}
```

Each `H_j` is a symmetric entrywise-sign matrix with `H_j^2=n_jI` and
trace zero.  Deleting its diagonal therefore gives a hollow exact signing
with the same quadratic energy on the Boolean cube.

the tensor poles form an affine multiplicative set

```math
\mathcal A_j=a^{\otimes j}\mathcal C_j,
\qquad |\mathcal C_j|=4^j,                         \tag{PC.23}
```

where `C_j` has the two independent relative generators of `C_0` in each
tensor factor.  Every element of `A_j` is a Boolean `+r_j` eigenvector.

Take as ports the base pole `a_j=a^{\otimes j}` together with
`a_j\odot g` for each of the `2j` distinguished generators `g` of `C_j`.
Then

```math
p_j=2j+1={1\over2}\log_2 n_j+1,                   \tag{PC.24}
```

and (PC.14) says that all odd port products are precisely `A_j`.  Hence the
system is fully odd-product closed for every selector and PC.1 applies.

### Theorem PC.3 (sublinear exact carrier in a dense sign family)

For the family above, the complete labelled Boolean trust response has an
exact projective-histogram carrier with

```math
2^{p_j-1}=4^j=\sqrt{n_j}                           \tag{PC.25}
```

bins and an explicit description using

```math
O(\sqrt{n_j}\log n_j)                              \tag{PC.26}
```

bits.  The state composes by histogram addition and obeys the tensor law
PC.2.  Choose

```math
m_j=\left\lfloor{r_j\over p_j}\right\rfloor.       \tag{PC.27}
```

Then the total repeated-port mass satisfies `m_jp_j/r_j<=1`, while the
complete response is still given exactly by (PC.7).

If all pairs among the at most `p_jm_j<=r_j=sqrt(n_j)` auxiliary vertices
are filled by an arbitrary public hollow signing, the exact cap changes by
at most

```math
{p_jm_j\choose2}=O(n_j)=o(r_jn_j).                 \tag{PC.28}
```

Thus this is a dense exact-sign, growing-interface model with bounded total
port mass and a reusable state of sublinear description length.  It is not
the arbitrary dense-bridge problem: its interface is an affine
multiplicative top-pole family.  Its value is to demonstrate at a scale
approaching the original obstruction that algebraic synchronization can
turn exponentially many old spins into a strict macroscopic quotient.

#### Proof

Tensor eigenvector identities prove (PC.22)--(PC.23).  Independence of the
factor-embedded rank-two generators gives `|C_j|=4^j`; equivalently, a
product equal to one restricts in each tensor factor to the trivial base
group word.  Equation (PC.14) proves full closure of the `2j+1` ports.
PF.1 then supplies the exact histogram carrier.  Storing each of its
`sqrt(n_j)` counts with `ceil(log_2(n_j+1))` bits proves (PC.26).  The mass
and completion estimates are immediate from (PC.27) and `r_j^2=n_j`.
`square`

## 7. A finite non-Hadamard benchmark

Let coordinates be indexed by `{+-1}^p`, let `J` be the antipodal
permutation `(Jf)(a)=f(-a)`, and set `H=-J`, `r=1`.  Every odd Boolean
character is a positive eigenvector, so the coordinate ports are fully
odd-product closed at every arity.  The top eigenspace is the complete
odd-function space and PC.1 recovers all labelled responses exactly.  This
benchmark is sparse rather than an entrywise signing; its role is to show
that the abstract class is populated independently of the dense TC seed.

## 8. Scope

PC.1 is sufficient structure, not a claim that every useful system has a
large affine top-pole set.  Full closure has exponentially many identities
when `p` grows, and arbitrary dense bridges need not preserve it.  PC.3 is
special because its `p=Theta(log n)` algebra is generated by only `O(log n)`
relative poles and has `sqrt(n)` states.  The mathematical mechanism is
joint cancellation before absolute values: the endpoint-specific threshold
witness remains in one active eigenspace.
