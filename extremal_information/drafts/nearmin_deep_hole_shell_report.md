# Deep-hole and farthest-shell literature audit for the augmented cut code

**Scope.** This report tests published coding-theory results against FB.1/FB.3. It does not edit a canonical theorem file. Labels used below are **[PRIMARY]** (checked in the cited primary paper), **[DERIVED]** (an exact translation or consequence), **[NEW PROOF]** (proved here), **[COMPUTED]** (finite exhaustive check), and **[OBSTRUCTION]** (a failed hypothesis or a logical limitation).

## Bottom line

**[LITERATURE CONCLUSION]** I found no published theorem that turns FB.1 or FB.3, for this code, into uniform nearest-codeword multiplicity, a concentrated overlap law, or a sublinear composable state. The strongest superficially relevant theorem is real but inapplicable: if a code is uniformly packed in the wide sense (UPWS), equivalently if its covering radius equals its external distance, then every deepest coset has the same full weight distribution and hence the same number of nearest codewords. The augmented cut code fails the equality by a polynomial margin for all sufficiently large orders.

There are nevertheless two new code-geometric consequences.

1. **[NEW PROOF] FB.1 already gives a signed overlap moment and a macroscopic shell diameter.** Under (FB.6), two codewords in the same near-nearest shell are at mutual Hamming distance at least `(1-delta)N/2`, where `N=binom(n,2)` and `delta` is the FB.6 right side. For exact minimizers with `kappa=n^(-1/6)`, the shell of excess radius `n^(4/3)` therefore contains two codewords at distance `(1-O(n^(-1/6)))N/2`.
2. **[NEW PROOF] FB.3 can be upgraded from cardinality to a genuine, though logarithmically weaker, packing.** When `r/(n log n)->infinity`, its shell contains `exp(Omega(r/n))` codewords pairwise separated by `Omega(r/log n)` in Hamming distance. For `r=n^(3/2)/log n`, this is a packing of size `exp(Omega(sqrt(n)/log n))` and separation `Omega(n^(3/2)/log^2 n)`.

Neither conclusion is yet a composable response state. The first controls only one signed edge-overlap moment; the second loses a logarithm in separation and can still be generated by an extensive vertex-label description.

## 1. Exact code dictionary

Put `N=binom(n,2)` and map a signing `a in {+-1}^N` to

```math
y_e=(1-a_e)/2\in\mathbb F_2.
```

Define the augmented cut (augmented coboundary) code

```math
C_n=\{c^{\sigma,x}:c^{\sigma,x}_{ij}=(1-\sigma x_ix_j)/2,
       \ \sigma,x_i\in\{\pm1\}\}\subseteq\mathbb F_2^N.
```

It is the binary cut space of `K_n` plus the all-one word. For `n>=3`, it has dimension `n` and exactly `2^n` words. The sign set `Z_n^+` in FB.1 is in bijection with `C_n`.

**[DERIVED] Exact objective/radius identity.** For corresponding `z` and `c`,

```math
\langle a,z\rangle=N-2d_H(y,c),
\qquad Q(a)=N-2d_H(y,C_n).
```

Thus, writing `rho_n=rho(C_n)`,

```math
M_n=N-2\rho_n.
```

Exact minimizers are precisely deep holes (farthest-off points) of `C_n`. An `epsilon`-near-minimizer corresponds to

```math
d_H(y,C_n)\ge \rho_n-\tfrac12\epsilon n^{3/2}.
```

The shell `S_u(a)` is exactly the list of codewords satisfying

```math
d_H(y,c)\le d_H(y,C_n)+\tfrac12u n^{3/2}.
```

In particular, FB.3 counts codewords within radius `rho_n+r` of an exact deep hole; it does **not** count nearest codewords at radius exactly `rho_n`.

**[PRIMARY]** Solé and Zaslavsky prove the corresponding unaugmented statement: switching classes of signed graphs are cosets of the cutset/cocycle code, and maximum frustration is its covering radius (Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 in [Solé--Zaslavsky 1994](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480189174374), *SIAM J. Discrete Math.* 7, 544--553). **[OBSTRUCTION]** Their code is the ordinary cut code. Adding the all-one word folds a switching class together with its global sign reversal, so our radius is `min(ell(Sigma),ell(-Sigma))`, not the ordinary frustration index `ell(Sigma)`. Their covering-radius bounds do not state shell multiplicity or overlap information.

For later hypothesis checks, for `n>=5` the parameters begin

```math
C_n=[N,n,n-1]_2,
```

because nonzero cuts have weights `k(n-k)` and their complements have weights `N-k(n-k)`. Its dual is

```math
C_n^\perp
=\{\text{Eulerian subgraphs of }K_n\text{ having an even number of edges}\}.
```

In particular, `C_n^perp` contains a 4-cycle, so its minimum distance is at most 4 (equal to 4 for `n>=4`).

## 2. Published theorems and exact applicability

### 2.1 Multiple coverings of farthest-off points

**[PRIMARY]** Hämäläinen, Honkala, Litsyn, and Östergård define an `(n,M,r,mu)` multiple covering of the farthest-off points (MCF) by *assuming* that every point at distance `r` is covered by at least `mu` radius-`r` balls, and then bound the minimum code size and give constructions ([1995](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480193252100), *SIAM J. Discrete Math.* 8, 196--207). Bartoli et al. likewise construct short radius-1 and radius-2 MCF codes via saturating sets ([2016](https://doi.org/10.3934/amc.2016030), *Adv. Math. Commun.* 10, 613--632).

**[OBSTRUCTION]** MCF is a property to verify, not a consequence of linearity, a large covering radius, or many nearly nearest words. With our notation it assumes a lower bound on

```math
A_{\rho_n}(y+C_n)
=|\{c\in C_n:d_H(y,c)=\rho_n\}|
```

for every deep hole. FB.3 instead bounds `sum_{j=rho_n}^{rho_n+r} A_j`. No theorem in these papers removes the positive slack `r`, and their principal quantitative regimes have small fixed radius, whereas `rho_n=N/2-Theta(n^(3/2))`.

### 2.2 MDS deep cosets and coset weight distributions

**[PRIMARY]** Davydov, Marcugini, and Pambianco, Theorem 4.6, show that for an `[L,k,d]_q` MDS code with covering radius `R=d-1`, every weight-`d-1` coset has the same weight distribution and exactly `binom(L,d-1)` leaders of weight `d-1` ([2023](https://doi.org/10.3934/amc.2021042), *Adv. Math. Commun.* 17, 1115--1138). Consequently those codes are almost-perfect multiple coverings of their deep holes.

**[OBSTRUCTION]** `C_n` is very far from MDS: its minimum distance is `n-1`, while the Singleton value is `N-n+1`, and its radius is `N/2-Theta(n^(3/2))`, not `d-1=n-2`. The theorem has no puncturing/restriction clause that applies merely because `C_n` is the evaluation of affine functions on the weight-two slice of the cube.

### 2.3 Uniform packing and the external-distance route

This is the only published route found that would have supplied the desired multiplicity without explicitly assuming it.

**[PRIMARY]** Delsarte's external-distance bound is `rho(C)<=s(C^perp)`, where `s(C^perp)` is the number of distinct nonzero weights in the dual ([Delsarte 1973](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(73)80007-5), *Information and Control* 23, 407--438). In the modern formulation recorded as Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 5.2 by Davydov, Marcugini, and Pambianco, `C` is UPWS iff `rho(C)=s(C^perp)`; under that equality, all weight-`rho` cosets have the same full weight distribution and `C` is an almost-perfect multiple covering of its farthest-off points (see [their primary preprint/paper text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10223), Theorems 1.4 and 5.2).

**[NEW PROOF / OBSTRUCTION] The equality fails for `C_n` by `Omega(n^(3/2))`.** Let `W_n` be the maximum weight in `C_n^perp`. Elementary complementation gives

```math
W_n=
\begin{cases}
N-n/2,&n\text{ even},\\
N,&n\equiv1\pmod4,\\
N-3,&n\equiv3\pmod4.
\end{cases}
```

For even `n`, the complement of an even-degree graph has every degree odd, hence at least `n/2` edges; equality is a perfect matching. For odd `n`, `K_n` is Eulerian, and the additional even-edge constraint gives the last two cases.

Moreover,

```math
s(C_n^\perp)\ge W_n/2-2=N/2-O(n).
```

To verify the weight support rather than guess it, use Bryant--Horsley--Pettersson's complete solution of Alspach's cycle-decomposition problem: an odd-order `K_n`, or an even-order `K_n` minus a perfect matching, decomposes into cycles of any prescribed admissible lengths summing to its number of edges ([2014](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdt051), *Proc. London Math. Soc.* 108, 1153--1192). Every integer at least 3 is, for `n>=5`, a sum of integers in `[3,n]`. Partitioning an admissible cycle list into sublists therefore realizes every even weight from 4 through `W_n-4`, and also `W_n`, as an even-cardinality Eulerian subgraph. This yields the displayed lower bound (the possible missing top weight `W_n-2` is immaterial).

On the other hand, the retained lower bound `M_n>=c_0 n^(3/2)` gives

```math
\rho_n=(N-M_n)/2\le N/2-(c_0/2)n^{3/2}.
```

Hence `s(C_n^perp)>rho_n` for all sufficiently large `n`; quantitatively the slack is `Omega(n^(3/2))`. Thus `C_n` is not UPWS, and the published equal-deep-coset-distribution theorem does not apply.

**[COMPUTED, exact finite check]** The failure is visible before asymptotics. Exhaustive enumeration for `n=7` gives `N=21`, `rho=6`, `s(C_7^perp)=8`, and deep cosets with three different nearest-leader multiplicities `3,4,7`. Representatives (edge sets on vertices `0,...,6`) are respectively

```text
3: {01,03,05,13,14,23,24}
4: {01,02,05,12,14,23}
7: {01,03,05,12,14,23}.
```

The check enumerates the `2^(21-7)=16384` cosets and all `2^7` words per coset. This is not an asymptotic counterexample, but it rules out treating constant deep-hole multiplicity as an automatic symmetry of the augmented cut code.

### 2.4 Average coset stability and coset-leader algorithms

**[PRIMARY]** Bazzi bounds the average `L_infinity` and `L_1` distance between the weight distribution of a random coset and the binomial law when the dual has large bilateral minimum distance ([2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2015.2487348), *IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory* 61, 6493--6504). **[OBSTRUCTION]** Here `C_n^perp` contains a 4-cycle, so its bilateral distance is at most 4 (and is zero in congruence classes where the all-one word lies in the dual). More importantly, the theorem averages over all cosets; deep cosets are a rare, conditioned tail and no conclusion in the paper is uniform over them.

**[PRIMARY]** Borges-Quintana et al. compute all coset leaders and the number of leaders in each coset using a Gröbner representation ([2015](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219498815501285), *J. Algebra Appl.* 14, article 1550128). **[OBSTRUCTION]** This is an output-sensitive algorithm, with complexity proportional to the number of leaders; it does not bound deep-coset multiplicity, overlap, or description length from code parameters.

The phrase *multicovering radius* is also not the desired multiplicity invariant: it asks for one codeword simultaneously far/near relative to a prescribed tuple of received words (for example, Klapper's [Reed--Muller multicovering paper](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~klapper/pdf/rm.pdf)), not for the number of nearest codewords to one deep hole.

## 3. New consequence of FB.1: overlap moment and shell diameter

For signed codewords `z,z' in Z_n^+`, define the normalized edge overlap

```math
R(z,z')={1\over N}\sum_e z_ez'_e
=1-{2d_H(c,c')\over N}.
```

### Proposition DH.1

**[NEW PROOF]** Suppose a probability measure `mu` on one FB shell satisfies

```math
{1\over N}\sum_e|m_e(mu)|\le\delta,
\qquad m_e(mu)=\mathbb E_\mu[a_ez_e].
```

If `Z,Z'` are independent with law `mu`, then

```math
0\le \mathbb E R(Z,Z')
={1\over N}\sum_em_e(mu)^2\le\delta.                 \tag{DH.1}
```

Consequently:

```math
\operatorname{diam}_H(\operatorname{supp}\mu)
\ge {1-\delta\over2}N,                               \tag{DH.2}
```

and, for every `-1<t<1`,

```math
\Pr\{R(Z,Z')>t\}\le {1+\delta\over1+t}.             \tag{DH.3}
```

**Proof.** Since `a_e^2=1`, independence gives `E[Z_eZ'_e]=m_e^2`. Also `m_e^2<=|m_e|`, proving (DH.1). Some pair has overlap at most the mean, which is equivalent to (DH.2). Finally `R>=-1`, so if `p=Pr(R>t)`, then `E R >= pt-(1-p)=p(1+t)-1`; combine with (DH.1). `square`

Applying FB.6 gives

```math
\delta={2(\epsilon+\eta_n(\kappa))\over\kappa(1-1/n)}.
```

Thus fixed small `epsilon` with `kappa=sqrt(epsilon)` yields two codewords, each within `sqrt(epsilon)n^(3/2)` of the nearest distance, separated by `(1-O(sqrt(epsilon)))N/2`. For an exact minimizer and `kappa=n^(-1/6)`, the excess radius is `n^(4/3)` and the separation is `(1-O(n^(-1/6)))N/2`.

**[OBSTRUCTION]** (DH.1) is a *signed* overlap moment. It does not bound `E|R|`, the vertex-spin overlap, selector correlations, or a response metric under continuation. Algebraically, cancellation between nearly antipodal pairs can make `E R` small while `E|R|` is large. The common positive shell rules out exact antipodes when the shell floor is positive, but only gives the weak pairwise lower bound `R>=-1+2(Q(a)-2kappa n^(3/2))/N`, which still tends to `-1`. Therefore no absolute-overlap concentration follows from FB.1 by this route.

## 4. New consequence of FB.3: a separated near-nearest packing

### Proposition DH.2

**[NEW PROOF]** Let `a` be an exact minimizer and let `r` satisfy FB.3. Put `s=c_1r/n`, where `c_1` is its constant. If `s/log n -> infinity`, then the FB.3 shell contains a subset `P` such that

```math
|P|\ge\exp(s/2),
\qquad d_H(c,c')\ge {c_1r\over 40\log(en)}
\quad(c\ne c'\in P)                                  \tag{DH.4}
```

for all sufficiently large `n` (the constant 40 is deliberately nonoptimized).

**Proof.** Write each signed cut uniquely as `(sigma,[x])`, where `[x]` is a vertex sign vector modulo global negation. One `sigma`-fibre contains at least `exp(s)/2` shell words. Within a fixed fibre, if the projective vertex Hamming distance is `k<=n/2`, then the edge Hamming distance is exactly `k(n-k)`.

Choose `h=floor(s/(8log(en)))`. A projective Hamming ball of radius `h` contains at most `sum_{j<=h}binom(n,j)<=exp(s/8)`. Greedy ball deletion produces at least `exp(s)/(2exp(s/8))>=exp(s/2)` representatives at projective distance greater than `h`. Since `s=o(sqrt(n))` under FB.3, `h=o(n)`, and `k(n-k)>=hn/2>=c_1r/(40log(en))` eventually. `square`

For `r=floor(n^(3/2)/log n)`, (DH.4) gives the concrete packing quoted in the bottom line. The logarithmic loss is intrinsic to this cardinality-only extraction: a projective Hamming ball of radius `h` already has `exp(Theta(h log(n/h)))` words. FB.3 alone therefore cannot yield target-scale `Omega(r)` separation without additional information on how the shell sits inside the projective cube.

## 5. Consequences for the near-minimizer campaign

1. **Do not use MCF/APMCF terminology as a proved property.** FB.3 supplies many enlarged-radius covers of each deep hole, at radius `rho+r`; it says nothing quantitative about exact nearest-leader multiplicity and is not an MCF theorem at the actual covering radius `rho`.
2. **Strike the UPWS shortcut for the augmented cut code.** The exact external-distance hypothesis fails by `Omega(n^(3/2))`, so equal deep-coset distributions and automatic multiplicity are unavailable.
3. **Retain DH.1 as a Level-5 necessary overlap statement.** It is an honest upgrade of fractional balance: a near-minimizer shell has near-half-edge diameter and a controlled first signed overlap moment. It is still below `L_sync`, because selector/joint Fourier information is absent.
4. **Retain DH.2 only as a packing baseline.** It improves “many words” to “many separated words,” but only at `r/log n` separation and with no response-continuation lower bound.
5. **The precise missing theorem is now narrow.** One needs either (a) a near-deep-coset theorem special to the weight-two affine evaluation code that controls *absolute or selector overlap* from the common positive energy constraint, or (b) a counterexample showing a deep/near-deep coset can realize the DH.1 moment through two nearly antipodal clusters with extensive contextual information. General deep-hole, MDS, UPWS, MCF, and average-coset theorems do not supply this step.
