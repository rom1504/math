# Matroid contraction quotients and projective geometry

**Status.** Theorem-builder draft.  The proofs are self-contained.  This is
an application generated by the closed-summary congruence law, not by a
terminal Rees collapse.  The basic compression applies to every finite
matroid; projective geometry supplies an exact fiber formula and a sharp
state count.

## 1. General matroid model

Let `M=(E,r)` be a finite matroid of rank `R`, and let `L(M)` be its lattice
of flats.  Composition is join

```math
X\vee Y=\operatorname{cl}_M(X\cup Y),
```

and the observable is residual rank

```math
F_M(X)=R-r(X).                                   \tag{MQ.1}
```

Fix a flat `W` of rank `d`.  The interval `[W,E]` is the flat lattice of
the contraction `M/W`, and the canonical map

```math
h_W:L(M)\longrightarrow[W,E],
\qquad h_W(X)=X\vee W                             \tag{MQ.2}
```

forgets directions already supplied by `W`.

### Theorem MQ.1 (matroid contraction compression)

The map `h_W` is a surjective join homomorphism.  Every fiber over
`Y\in[W,E]` has residual-rank oscillation at most `d`:

```math
\operatorname{osc}_{F_M}(h_W^{-1}(Y))\le d.       \tag{MQ.3}
```

The decoder

```math
\widehat F_W(Y)=R-r(Y)+{d\over2}                  \tag{MQ.4}
```

therefore has uniform error at most `d/2` after an arbitrary number of
future joins.  This constant is optimal for this fixed quotient: the fiber
over `W` contains both the rank-zero bottom flat and `W`, so its oscillation
is exactly `d`.  The number of summary states is the number of flats of the
contraction `M/W`.

#### Proof

Associativity and commutativity of join give

```math
h_W(X\vee Z)
=(X\vee Z)\vee W
=(X\vee W)\vee(Z\vee W),
```

and every `Y>=W` is its own preimage, proving homomorphism and surjectivity.
If `X\vee W=Y`, then `X<=Y`, while subadditivity of matroid rank gives

```math
r(Y)=r(X\vee W)\le r(X)+r(W)=r(X)+d.             \tag{MQ.5}
```

Thus `r(Y)-d<=r(X)<=r(Y)`, which is exactly (MQ.3).  The midpoint in
(MQ.4) gives error at most `d/2`.  Because future composition is performed
exactly in `[W,E]`, the same fiber bound applies once at the end and does
not accumulate with depth.

Finally, the bottom flat `cl_M(varnothing)` has rank zero and joins with `W`
to `W`; the flat `W` also maps to `W`.  Their residual ranks differ by
`d`, proving optimality.  Flats above `W` are canonically the flats of
`M/W`, proving the state count. `square`

For `0<d<R`, this is not a terminal Rees collapse.  The entire bottom
interval `L(M|W)` maps to the identity `W` of the quotient join-semilattice,
not to its absorbing top.  Moreover, the identifications occur throughout
the lattice: `X` and `X\vee U` are merged for every flat `U<=W`.

## 2. Triggered contractions: a zero-separating quotient

The bottom contraction is only the first member of a larger family.  It can
be activated conditionally above a flat without collapsing any neighborhood
of zero.

Fix flats `X<Y` and put `d=r(Y)-r(X)`.  Define a relation on `L(M)` by

```math
U\equiv_{X,Y}V
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U=V\ \text{ or }
\bigl(X\le U,V\text{ and }U\vee Y=V\vee Y\bigr). \tag{TC.1}
```

### Theorem TC.1 (conditional contraction congruence)

The relation (TC.1) is a join congruence.  Its classes inside `[X,E]` are
indexed by flats `P>=Y` and have the form

```math
\mathcal C_P=\{U:X\le U,\ U\vee Y=P\}.          \tag{TC.2}
```

Every such fiber has residual-rank oscillation at most `d`, and the worst
fiber has oscillation exactly `d`.  Hence the quotient has an
arbitrary-depth decoder of optimal uniform error `d/2`.  Its exact number
of states is

```math
|L(M)|-|[X,E]|+|[Y,E]|.                         \tag{TC.3}
```

If `X` has positive rank, the congruence is zero-separating.  If also
`Y<E`, it is not a Rees congruence: the class containing `X` and `Y` is
not absorbing.

#### Proof

Equality outside the upper interval `[X,E]` and equality of `U\vee Y`
inside that interval plainly define an equivalence relation.  The only
nontrivial compatibility check starts with `U,V>=X` and
`U\vee Y=V\vee Y`.  For every flat `Z`,

```math
(U\vee Z)\vee Y
=(U\vee Y)\vee Z
=(V\vee Y)\vee Z
=(V\vee Z)\vee Y,                               \tag{TC.4}
```

and `U\vee Z,V\vee Z>=X`; hence the relation is a join congruence.

For `U\in\mathcal C_P`, submodularity in the flat lattice gives

```math
r(U)+r(Y)
\ge r(P)+r(U\wedge Y)
\ge r(P)+r(X).                                   \tag{TC.5}
```

Together with `U<=P`, this yields

```math
r(P)-d\le r(U)\le r(P).                          \tag{TC.6}
```

Thus the residual-rank oscillation is at most `d`.  The fiber
`\mathcal C_Y` contains both `X` and `Y`, so the global worst oscillation is
exactly `d`.  Decode a singleton by its exact residual rank and decode
`\mathcal C_P` by `R-r(P)+d/2`.  Since composition is exact in the quotient,
the error is paid only once, independently of depth.

There is one class for each `P>=Y`, while every flat outside `[X,E]` remains
a singleton, proving (TC.3).  When `r(X)>0`, the bottom class is a singleton.
When `Y<E`, joining the class `\mathcal C_Y` with a flat not contained in
`Y` moves it to another class, so it is not absorbing. `square`

This construction is the simplest explicit evidence that the
zero-separating remainder in PMQ.2 need not be the identity or a terminal
Rees truncation.  It conditionally forgets at most `d` ranks only after the
trigger `X` has appeared.

## 3. Projective specialization

Let `V=F_q^w`, let `L(V)` be its subspace join-semilattice, and compose by

```math
X\vee Y=X+Y.
```

The residual-rank observable is

```math
F_V(X)=w-\dim X.                                  \tag{PMQ.1}
```

As proved in HRC.3, this is the exact future-response quotient of the
projective matroid: every ground-set subset is represented by its span.

### A fixed-subspace quotient

Fix a subspace `W<=V` of dimension `d`, put `bar V=V/W`, and define

```math
h_W:L(V)\longrightarrow L(\bar V),
\qquad
h_W(X)=(X+W)/W.                                   \tag{PMQ.2}
```

### Corollary PMQ.1 (exact projective contraction compression)

The map `h_W` is a surjective join homomorphism.  On every fiber over
`Y<=bar V`, the exact oscillation of residual rank is `d`:

```math
\max_{h_W(X)=Y}F_V(X)-\min_{h_W(X)=Y}F_V(X)=d.  \tag{PMQ.3}
```

Consequently, decoding by

```math
\widehat F_W(Y)
=w-\dim Y-{d\over2}                             \tag{PMQ.4}
```

gives uniform error exactly `d/2`, and the summaries compose with no error
accumulation under arbitrarily many future joins.

The number of reachable states is

```math
|L(F_q^{w-d})|
=\sum_{j=0}^{w-d}{w-d\brack j}_q,               \tag{PMQ.5}
```

so, for fixed `q`,

```math
\log_2|L(F_q^{w-d})|
=\left\lfloor{(w-d)^2\over4}\right\rfloor
 \log_2q+O(\log w).                              \tag{PMQ.6}
```

In particular, for `0<=eta<w/2`, taking
`d=floor(2eta)` gives an exact closed summary with

```math
\left\lfloor{(w-\lfloor2\eta\rfloor)^2\over4}\right\rfloor
\log_2q+O(\log w)                               \tag{PMQ.7}
```

bits.  For `eta=epsilon*w` and fixed `0<epsilon<1/2`, the leading
coefficient is

```math
{(1-2\epsilon)^2\over4}\,w^2\log_2q.          \tag{PMQ.8}
```

#### Proof

Theorem MQ.1 already gives the homomorphism and the upper bound.  Projection
commutes with sums, so explicitly

```math
h_W(X+Z)=h_W(X)+h_W(Z).
```

Every quotient subspace has a linear section, proving surjectivity.  If
`h_W(X)=Y`, rank--nullity for the restriction of the quotient map to `X`
gives

```math
\dim X=\dim Y+\dim(X\cap W).                   \tag{PMQ.9}
```

The intersection dimension ranges from zero to `d`.  Both endpoints occur:
choose a linear section `U_Y` of `Y` and take respectively
`X=U_Y` and `X=U_Y+W`.  Equations (PMQ.1) and (PMQ.9) prove the exact
oscillation `d`, the midpoint guarantee, and its optimality for this fixed
congruence.

For `n=w-d`, the Gaussian coefficient bounds

```math
q^{j(n-j)}
\le {n\brack j}_q
\le C_q q^{j(n-j)},                              \tag{PMQ.10}
```

where `C_q=prod_(i>=1)(1-q^(-i))^(-1)<infinity`, show that the middle
dimension supplies the lower bound in (PMQ.6), while summing the upper bound
over `n+1` dimensions costs only `O(log n)` bits.  Equations
(PMQ.7)--(PMQ.8) follow. `square`

### Why this is not a Rees collapse

A Rees quotient identifies one semigroup ideal with an absorbing state.  The
zero fiber of `h_W` is

```math
h_W^{-1}(0)=L(W),                                 \tag{PMQ.11}
```

a downward subspace lattice whose image is the identity, not an absorbing
element.  Projection simultaneously merges `X` with `X+U` for every
`U<=W` at every rank, including states far from the spanning terminal
state.  It is therefore a genuinely different admissible congruence in
CSC.1.

### Projective triggered contractions

If `M` is projective geometry, every non-singleton fiber in (TC.2) has
oscillation exactly `d`: for `P>=Y`, choose a complement of `Y/X` in
`P/X`.  Its inverse image `U` satisfies `U\cap Y=X`, `U+Y=P`, and
`dim U=dim P-d`, while `P` itself supplies the other endpoint.

Writing `k=dim X` and `N_q(s)=|L(F_q^s)|`, the state count is

```math
N_q(w)-N_q(w-k)+N_q(w-k-d).                     \tag{PTC.1}
```

For every fixed `k>=1`, this still has
`(w^2/4)log_2 q+o(w^2)` bits: a single conditional contraction changes a
large but exponentially negligible part of the flat lattice.  Thus it is
structurally nontrivial but does not by itself improve the macroscopic
closed response rate.  A better zero-separating quotient would have to
coordinate many such triggers without allowing their rank errors to add.

## 4. Canonical kernel decomposition of projective join congruences

The fixed-subspace construction is forced inside every congruence, although
it need not be the whole congruence.

### Theorem PMQ.2 (linear kernel plus zero-separating remainder)

Let `theta` be any congruence of the join-semilattice `L(V)`.  There is a
unique subspace `W_theta` such that

```math
[0]_\theta=L(W_\theta).                         \tag{PMQ.12}
```

Every pair with the same image modulo `W_theta` is `theta`-equivalent:

```math
X+W_\theta=Y+W_\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\mathrel\theta Y. \tag{PMQ.13}
```

After factoring by these projection fibers, `theta` induces a congruence
`bar theta` on `L(V/W_theta)` whose zero class is the singleton `{0}`.
Conversely, a subspace `W` and a zero-separating congruence on `L(V/W)`
pull back to a unique congruence with zero class `L(W)`.

If a class of `bar theta` is `bar C`, the exact residual-rank oscillation
of its full preimage is

```math
\operatorname{osc}_{F_V}(h_W^{-1}(\bar C))
=\dim W+
 \operatorname{osc}_{F_{V/W}}(\bar C).           \tag{PMQ.14}
```

Therefore a closed `eta`-accurate projective-matroid summary is equivalent
to choosing

1. a linear kernel `W` of dimension at most `2eta`; and
2. a zero-separating quotient of `L(V/W)` for which **every** induced class
   has residual-rank oscillation at most `2eta-dim W`.

#### Proof

The zero class is closed under joins.  Let `W_theta` be the join of all its
members; finiteness puts `W_theta` in the zero class.  If `U<=W_theta`,
then

```math
W_\theta=W_\theta+U
\mathrel\theta 0+U=U,
```

so every subspace of `W_theta` is in the zero class, proving (PMQ.12).
Joining `W_theta \mathrel\theta 0` to an arbitrary `X` gives
`X+W_theta \mathrel\theta X`, which proves (PMQ.13).

Thus equivalence descends unambiguously to the quotient space.  Its zero
class is trivial by maximality of `W_theta`.  Pullback proves the converse.

For a quotient subspace of codimension `c`, (PMQ.9) shows that the original
residual rank runs through every value from `c` to `c+dim W`.  Taking the
union over a class `bar C` gives (PMQ.14).  The final characterization is
CSC.1 applied to this exact oscillation formula. `square`

This is a structural feature-algebra law rather than a count for one chosen
summary.  It says that every closed compression first forgets a linear
direction and then applies a congruence that is forbidden from forgetting
any further neighborhood of zero.  Terminal Rees truncation can occur only
in the second, zero-separating factor.

## 5. Comparison with the response-packing lower bound

For `q=2`, every exact closed summary is also a uniformly accurate response
summary.  The Grassmann packing behind HRC.5 gives, for fixed
`epsilon<1/4`,

```math
\log_2 K_{\rm closed}(\epsilon w)
\ge\big((1/2-2\epsilon)^2-o(1)\big)w^2.        \tag{PMQ.15}
```

The projection quotient gives

```math
\log_2 K_{\rm closed}(\epsilon w)
\le\big((1-2\epsilon)^2/4+o(1)\big)w^2.       \tag{PMQ.16}
```

The bounds agree at zero distortion but leave a genuine coefficient gap for
positive distortion.  PMQ.2 turns closing this gap into a precise algebraic
question: how much can a zero-separating congruence of a subspace
join-semilattice merge while keeping every residual-rank class narrow?

## 6. General lesson and falsifier

The construction separates three notions that previously looked similar.

1. A response net chooses nearby value profiles but need not compose.
2. A Rees quotient forgets an absorbing terminal ideal.
3. A projection quotient forgets a normal linear direction and remains
   exactly homomorphic.

The first theorem applies to another standard model independently of the
syndrome word metric and improves the closed-state count over the exact flat
lattice.  The second theorem explains what information every competing
closed quotient must discard first.

A decisive falsifier for the proposed optimality of projection would be a
zero-separating congruence on `L(F_q^n)` with small residual-rank
oscillation and substantially fewer classes than the identity.  A proof that
no such congruence exists would make (PMQ.7) optimal and completely determine
the closed response rate--distortion function of projective residual rank.
