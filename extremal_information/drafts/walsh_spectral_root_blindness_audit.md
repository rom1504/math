# Adversarial audit: Walsh spectral root-blindness

**Verdict: PASS AFTER REPAIR.**  Lemma SB.1 and Theorem SB.2 are correct for
`m>=1`, with the stated normalization, arbitrary real symmetric edge
weights, repeated/zero labels, and all tuple lengths.  Corollary SB.3 has a
valid strong interpretation, but its phrase "every unrooted weighted
Walsh-graph extension" is too broad.  It must be restricted to graph
compositions generated by synchronized copies of the compared child (and,
if desired, neutral zero-labelled blocks).  A common *arbitrary labelled*
context can distinguish the two spectra.

The audited draft now incorporates both required scope repairs. No canonical
theory file was changed in this audit.

## 1. Weyl reduction and phase

Put

```math
U_a=M_aT_a.
```

With the conventions in the draft,

```math
J_a=U_aF,qquad
U_aU_b=(-1)^{a\cdot b}U_{a+b},qquad
FU_aF=(-1)^{a\cdot a}U_a.                              \tag{A.SB.1}
```

These identities give a fully explicit induction behind SB.4.  If
`s_r=a_1+...+a_r` and

```math
J_{a_1}\cdots J_{a_r}=(-1)^{\theta_r}U_{s_r}F^{r\bmod2},
```

then

```math
\theta_{r+1}=\theta_r+s_r\cdot a_{r+1}
 +(r\bmod2)(a_{r+1}\cdot a_{r+1}).                    \tag{A.SB.2}
```

Thus the phase really is determined by the binary Gram matrix, including
its diagonal.  This also covers repeated and zero labels; no division or
linear-independence hypothesis is hidden in the induction.

For even length the trace of `M_sT_s` is zero unless `s=0`, and is then
`q`.  For odd length,

```math
\operatorname{tr}(M_sT_sF)
=q^{-1/2}\sum_x(-1)^{s\cdot x+(x+s)\cdot x}
=q^{-1/2}\sum_x(-1)^{\omega\cdot x}=0,                \tag{A.SB.3}
```

because the two copies of `s dot x` cancel and `omega=(1,...,1)` is
nonzero.  This proves SB.5 exactly for every `m>=1`.

The draft should state `m>=1` explicitly.  If the convention allowed
`m=0`, then `omega=0`, `F=J_0=(1)`, and an odd word has trace one, contrary
to SB.5.

## 2. Scaling check

The theorem uses normalized operators `F=R/sqrt(q)` and
`J_a=D_aFD_a`.  The verifier uses the unnormalized operators
`R` and `D_aRD_a`; hence a word of length `ell` is multiplied by
`q^(ell/2)`.  For an even zero-sum word the expected unnormalized trace is

```math
q^{\ell/2}\,q=q^{\ell/2+1},                            \tag{A.SB.4}
```

which is exactly the script's `q * q**(length // 2)`.  Odd and nonzero-sum
traces remain zero.  There is no missing factor of `sqrt(q)`.

Likewise the unnormalized carrier in Theorem 21.9 is `sqrt(q)` times the
draft's `K_(G,a)`, so its full matrix is `q(F tensor K_(G,a))`.  Equality of
spectra is invariant under that common scalar normalization.

## 3. Power traces determine all weighted-graph spectra

A term in `tr(K^ell)` is indexed by a closed block walk.  A stay at block
`i` contributes `J_(a_i)`; a move contributes its real scalar edge weight
and `J_0=F`.  Therefore its operator word has labels drawn from
`{0,a_1,...,a_k}`.  Its zero-sum condition is exactly membership of the
mod-two stay-count vector in `mathcal R(a)`, and (A.SB.2) shows that its sign
is determined by `G(a)`.  This justifies the two claims in the proof for
every closed walk, including walks with repeated vertices and zero labels.
The scalar weight product is common to both carriers.

Consequently all power traces agree.  Since the carriers are real symmetric
matrices of the same finite dimension, equality of the first `kq` power
sums already determines the eigenvalue multiset by Newton identities.  The
claim for `F tensor K` follows because its eigenvalues are products of those
of the fixed `F` and of `K`.

I found no counterexample to SB.2 for negative, zero, or unequal integer
edge weights.  The proof treats arbitrary real weights identically.

## 5. Necessary scope repair for SB.3

Let `a=omega=111` and `b=100` in `F_2^3`.  The singleton tuples `(a)` and
`(b)` have the same Gram and relation states and different characteristic
root fibres, as used in Theorem 21.13.

If "extension" means appending an arbitrary common labelled context, take
that context to be another block labelled `b` and join the two blocks by an
edge of weight one.  For the unnormalized carriers of the verifier, exact
integer multiplication gives

```math
\operatorname{tr}K_{((a,b))}^4=6144,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}K_{((b,b))}^4=4096.                  \tag{A.SB.5}
```

Equivalently, for normalized carriers the fourth moments are `96` and `64`
because `q=8`.  Thus arbitrary labelled extension spectra can see the
difference.  This is expected: the appended label changes both the enlarged
Gram matrix and the enlarged relation kernel.

There is nevertheless a clean and scalable valid corollary.  For every
`k>=1` and every weighted graph `G`, compare the two **constant tuples**

```math
(a,\ldots,a)\quad\hbox{and}\quad(b,\ldots,b).          \tag{A.SB.6}
```

They have the same Gram matrix (all entries are the common self-parity) and
the same relation kernel (the even-parity subspace), so SB.2 makes their
spectra identical for every `G`.  The same remains true if a fixed pattern
of neutral zero labels is inserted on both sides.  Yet the singleton rooted
future from Theorem 21.13 separates the projective responses by
`n^(3/2)/6`.

Therefore the justified conclusion is:

> all unrooted spectral moments of every synchronized-repetition Walsh-graph
> experiment fail to recover the characteristic-root response coordinate.

The conclusion should not be advertised as blindness under every possible
labelled spectral context, nor as a statement about all conceivable
unrooted spectral experiments.

## 6. Repairs incorporated before promotion

The audited draft now:

1. states `m>=1` in the setup;
2. replaces "every unrooted weighted Walsh-graph extension" in SB.3 by an
   explicit constant-tuple/synchronized-repetition statement such as
   (A.SB.6).
3. narrows "the complete unrooted spectral theory" to "the complete family
   of synchronized-repetition Walsh-graph spectra" (or define a declared
   spectral query class that means exactly this).

The remaining plural phrase "singleton pairs" is harmless in context: it
refers to the two compared singleton objects, not a two-block tuple.

With those repairs, the result is rigorous and worth promotion: it cleanly
separates an exact rooted orbit coordinate from a substantial, all-orders
unrooted spectral experiment without claiming more than the proof gives.
