# Mixed-circuit holonomy and exact feature growth

**Status.**  The statements in Sections 2--6 are proved below.  Their finite
instances are checked by
[`verify_phase3_mixed_circuit_hierarchy.py`](../experiments/verify_phase3_mixed_circuit_hierarchy.py),
and MC.4/MC.6 have an independent proof-level cross-audit in
[`phase3_transversal_composition_adversarial_audit.md`](phase3_transversal_composition_adversarial_audit.md).

This note isolates a precise mechanism by which composition creates
extremal information.  A binary lift of a quotient generator carries a
kernel offset.  Offsets on an independent family are gauge: a linear shear
removes them.  On a quotient cycle their sum is gauge-invariant
**holonomy**.  For the antipodal kernel target, the shortest-path saving is
exactly Hamming holonomy minus cycle length.  Consequently:

* nonexpansion need only be checked on quotient-matroid circuits;
* approximate circuit defects accumulate by at most the quotient nullity;
* composing fragments creates exactly one `W`-valued feature for each new
  mixed-cycle dimension; and
* an `r`-fold composition can create a macroscopic response although every
  proper subcomposition is gauge-trivial.

The elimination identity itself is syndrome decoding, and the gauge exact
sequence is elementary linear algebra.  The theory-level content is their
combination: it gives an exact, quantitative law for when composition forces
new response information rather than merely asserting that a larger dynamic
program is needed.

The antipodal elimination in MC.1 independently recovers Theorem TC.3 of
`phase3_transversal_composition_growth.md`; it is retained here only as the
base of the finer circuit, gauge, and information results.  For complete
linear-graph sources, that note's affine-hull state is a structured encoding
of the mixed holonomies below.  The present exact sequence explains why a
new independent map direction costs `D dim(Q)` raw holonomy bits, while
MC.6 shows that even one quotient direction can require `Omega(D)` response
bits.

## 1. Binary extension and word responses

Let

```math
G=W\oplus Q,\qquad W=\mathbb F_2^D,
```

and let `B={b_1,...,b_D}` be the coordinate basis of `W`.  Put

```math
t=b_1+\cdots+b_D.                              \tag{MC.1}
```

Let `E` be a finite indexed family.  A lifted quotient column is

```math
s_e=(a_e,q_e)\in W\oplus Q\qquad(e\in E).      \tag{MC.2}
```

Write

```math
q_R=\sum_{e\in R}q_e,\qquad
a_R=\sum_{e\in R}a_e.                          \tag{MC.3}
```

The indexed convention harmlessly permits repeated columns.  A shortest
binary word never uses the same indexed column twice.  Define

```math
\ell_{B\cup E}(x)
=\min\left\{|I|+|R|:
  \sum_{i\in I}b_i+\sum_{e\in R}s_e=x\right\}. \tag{MC.4}
```

This is simultaneously a Cayley-graph distance and the minimum weight of a
word with prescribed syndrome for the systematic parity-check matrix whose
columns are `B` and the `s_e`.

## 2. Exact cycle-excess identity

Let

```math
Z=\ker(q:\mathbb F_2^E\longrightarrow Q)
 =\{R\subseteq E:q_R=0\}                       \tag{MC.5}
```

be the binary quotient-cycle space.  The offset map induces a linear
holonomy map

```math
h:Z\longrightarrow W,\qquad h(R)=a_R.          \tag{MC.6}
```

Define its positive cycle excess by

```math
\Delta(E,a,q)
=\max_{R\in Z}\bigl(|h(R)|-|R|\bigr).
                                                               \tag{MC.7}
```

The empty cycle is allowed, so `Delta>=0`.

### Theorem MC.1 (exact antipodal elimination)

For every lifted family,

```math
\boxed{\ell_{B\cup E}(t)=D-\Delta(E,a,q).}       \tag{MC.8}
```

More generally, for `u in W` and `q_0 in Q`,

```math
\ell_{B\cup E}(u,q_0)
=\min_{R:q_R=q_0}\bigl\{|R|+|u+a_R|\bigr\}.    \tag{MC.9}
```

The minimum is `+infinity` when the quotient target is not generated by the
`q_e`.

#### Proof

Fix `R` with `q_R=q_0`.  There is a unique kernel-basis subset completing
it to `(u,q_0)`, namely the support of `u+a_R`, which proves (MC.9).  For
`u=t` and `q_0=0`, binary complementation gives

```math
|t+a_R|=D-|a_R|.
```

Taking the minimum over `R in Z` gives (MC.8). `square`

Thus the chosen length-`D` representation of `t` is shortest precisely when

```math
|h(R)|\le |R|\qquad(R\in Z).                   \tag{MC.10}
```

In this language, geodesicity says that the holonomy map is nonexpansive
from cycle support weight to kernel Hamming weight.

## 3. Circuits are the exact certificate

A **circuit** is a nonempty inclusion-minimal member of `Z`; loops and
parallel pairs are included.  For a circuit `C`, put

```math
d(C)=\bigl(|h(C)|-|C|\bigr)_+.                 \tag{MC.11}
```

Let

```math
\nu=\dim Z=|E|-\operatorname{rank}\{q_e:e\in E\}. \tag{MC.12}
```

### Theorem MC.2 (circuit criterion and approximate defect)

The following are equivalent:

1. `ell_{B cup E}(t)=D`;
2. every quotient cycle obeys (MC.10);
3. every circuit `C` obeys `|h(C)|<=|C|`.

Quantitatively,

```math
\max_C d(C)
\le \Delta(E,a,q)
\le \max_{\mathcal C}
       \sum_{C\in\mathcal C}d(C)
\le \nu\max_Cd(C),                             \tag{MC.13}
```

where the middle maximum is over pairwise disjoint circuit families.  More
generally, if every circuit satisfies, for nonnegative `eta(C)`,

```math
|h(C)|\le |C|+\eta(C),
```

then

```math
D-\ell_{B\cup E}(t)
\le\max_{\mathcal C}\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\eta(C). \tag{MC.14}
```

In particular a uniform circuit defect at most `eta` costs at most
`nu eta`, while a relative defect `eta(C)<=epsilon |C|` costs at most
`epsilon |E|`.

#### Proof

Every nonempty binary cycle is a disjoint union of circuits: choose a
minimal nonempty subcycle and iterate on its complement.  For a partition
`R=C_1 dotcup ... dotcup C_m`, Hamming subadditivity gives

```math
|h(R)|-|R|
\le\sum_{j=1}^m\bigl(|h(C_j)|-|C_j|\bigr)
\le\sum_{j=1}^m d(C_j).                        \tag{MC.15}
```

This proves the circuit criterion and the middle bound.  The indicators of
pairwise disjoint nonempty circuits are linearly independent in `Z`, so
`m<=nu`, proving the last bound.  The first bound follows by taking a
single circuit in (MC.7), and the same argument with `eta(C)` proves
(MC.14). `square`

The nullity factor is necessary.  Given integers `nu>=1` and `c>=0`, take
`nu` independent quotient directions, two columns over each direction, and
make the holonomy of the `j`th parallel-pair circuit the indicator of a
kernel block of size `c+2`, with the blocks disjoint.  Then `D=nu(c+2)`,
every circuit has defect `c`, and their union has defect exactly `nu c`.

## 4. Gauge classification and the mixed-cycle exact sequence

The word **holonomy** is justified by an exact gauge statement.  A linear
map `L:span{q_e}->W`, extended arbitrarily to all of `Q`, defines a
kernel-fixing shear

```math
\theta_L(w,q)=(w+Lq,q).                        \tag{MC.16}
```

It replaces every offset by `a_e+L(q_e)` and leaves `h(R)` unchanged on
all cycles.

### Theorem MC.3 (holonomy is the complete gauge invariant)

Two offset assignments `a,a':F_2^E->W` have the same holonomy on `Z` if
and only if

```math
a'_e=a_e+L(q_e)                                \tag{MC.17}
```

for one linear `L:span{q_e}->W`.  Hence `(q,h)` classifies the lifted
family up to a kernel-fixing linear shear.

#### Proof

The forward implication in (MC.17) is immediate on `Z`.  Conversely,
`a'-a` vanishes on `ker q`, so it factors through the image of `q`; the
factor is the required `L`. `square`

Now partition the columns into fragments

```math
E=E_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots\mathbin{\dot\cup}E_m.
```

Put `U_j=span q(E_j)`, `U=sum_j U_j`, and

```math
Z_{\rm loc}=\bigoplus_j\ker(q|_{F_2^{E_j}})\subseteq Z.
```

The **mixed-cycle rank** is

```math
\kappa=\dim(Z/Z_{\rm loc})
=\sum_j\dim U_j-\dim U.                        \tag{MC.18}
```

### Theorem MC.4 (exact gluing cost)

After the gauge class of every labeled individual fragment has been fixed,
the compatible global gauge classes form an affine space modeled on

```math
\operatorname{Hom}(Z/Z_{\rm loc},W).           \tag{MC.19}
```

Consequently there are exactly

```math
2^{D\kappa}                                    \tag{MC.20}
```

possible mixed-holonomy extensions.  For two fragments,

```math
\kappa=\dim(U_1\cap U_2).                      \tag{MC.21}
```

#### Proof

Fix representatives of the local gauge classes.  Every other compatible
choice differs on fragment `j` by `L_j q`, with
`L_j in Hom(U_j,W)`.  On a global cycle this produces

```math
(v_1,...,v_m)\longmapsto
\sum_jL_j(qv_j),
```

which vanishes on `Z_loc`.  Every map `Z/Z_loc->W` arises this way: extend
it linearly to

```math
F_2^E/Z_{\rm loc}\cong\bigoplus_jU_j.
```

Its kernel consists exactly of compatible families `L_j` obtained by
restricting one global `L:U->W`, by Theorem MC.3.  This proves (MC.19).
Rank--nullity gives (MC.18), and (MC.20)--(MC.21) follow. `square`

This statement is about exact gauge information, not automatically the
minimal information needed for a particular scalar response.  The next
section shows that even one new mixed channel can carry macroscopic
response entropy.

## 5. A strict composition-arity hierarchy

### Theorem MC.5 (a response invisible to every proper subcomposition)

For every `r>=2` and `D>r`, there are `r` singleton fragments

```math
E_j=\{s_j=(a_j,q_j)\}
```

such that:

1. every proper union is cycle-contracting and has
   `ell(t)=D`;
2. every proper union is in fact linearly shear-isometric, fixing `W`
   pointwise, to the same union with all offsets zero; but
3. the full union has

```math
\ell_{B\cup\{s_1,...,s_r\}}(t)=r,              \tag{MC.22}
```

so the newly created response gap is `D-r`.

#### Construction and proof

Take `Q=F_2^{r-1}` and let

```math
q_i=e_i\ (i<r),\qquad q_r=e_1+\cdots+e_{r-1}.  \tag{MC.23}
```

These columns form one circuit and have no proper dependence.  Partition
the `D` kernel coordinates into `r` nonempty parts and let `a_i` be their
indicators.  Then `sum_i a_i=t`.

On a proper union the quotient columns are independent, so the only cycle
is empty and (MC.8) gives length `D`.  More strongly, the assignment
`q_i mapsto a_i` on that independent set extends linearly; the associated
shear removes all its offsets while fixing `W`.  On the full union the only
nonempty cycle is all `r` columns, with holonomy `t` and excess `D-r`.
Equation (MC.22) follows from MC.1. `square`

Thus checking all interactions of arity at most `r-1` cannot certify the
`r`-fold response, even up to a fixed fraction of `D` when `r` is fixed.
The obstruction is not an internal defect of any input fragment.  It is a
new mixed circuit and the inconsistency of the locally valid shears around
that circuit.

### Theorem MC.6 (one mixed channel has linear response entropy)

For `Q=F_2` and `q` its nonzero vector, define

```math
S_v=B\cup\{(0,q),(v,q)\},\qquad v\in W,
```

and retain all kernel-endpoint queries.  Their response profile is

```math
F_v(u)=\ell_{S_v}(u,0)
=\min\{|u|,\ 2+|u+v|\}.                        \tag{MC.24}
```

There is a family `V_D subseteq W` of size

```math
|V_D|\ge
2^{(1-H_2(1/4)-o(1))D}                         \tag{MC.25}
```

such that

```math
\|F_v-F_{v'}\|_\infty\ge D/4-O(1)
\qquad(v\ne v'\in V_D).                       \tag{MC.26}
```

Hence any deterministic feature that answers all kernel-endpoint queries
to additive error `epsilon D`, for fixed `epsilon<1/8`, has
`Omega(D)` bits on this one-mixed-cycle family.

#### Proof

Formula (MC.24) is (MC.9): the only quotient-zero selections are the empty
set and the two-column circuit.  Greedily pack the at least `2^{D-1}`
vectors of weight at least `D/2` by Hamming balls of radius less than
`D/4`.  The standard volume bound

```math
\sum_{j<D/4}{D\choose j}
\le 2^{(H_2(1/4)+o(1))D}
```

gives (MC.25) with pairwise distance at least `D/4-O(1)`.  At the query
`u=v`, one has `F_v(v)=2`, whereas

```math
F_{v'}(v)
=\min\{|v|,2+|v+v'|\}\ge D/4-O(1).
```

This proves (MC.26).  If two such profiles shared one summary state, two
applications of an `epsilon D` decoder would bound their sup distance by
`2epsilon D`, contradicting (MC.26) for large `D`. `square`

Each singleton fragment above has no internal cycle, so its offset is pure
gauge.  Their union creates one mixed cycle (`kappa=1`) and an exponential
packing of genuinely different future responses.  This is an explicit
conversion of algebraic gluing rank into extremal response information.

### Corollary MC.7 (a cross-fragment information charge)

Take the packing `V_D` from MC.6 and regard

```math
P=B\cup\{(0,q)\},\qquad R_v=B\cup\{(v,q)\}
```

as two fragments.  All `R_v` have the same kernel-fixing shear class, as
does `P`; hence their separate gauge-invariant local states contain no
information about `v`.  Any deterministic compositional scheme which adds
a gluing message `J_v` and then answers every kernel-endpoint query of
`P union R_v` to error `epsilon D`, for fixed `epsilon<1/8`, must satisfy

```math
\log_2|\operatorname{range}J|
\ge (1-H_2(1/4)-o(1))D.                       \tag{MC.27}
```

Indeed, MC.6 shows that two different packing elements cannot share a
message.  Thus forgetting local gauge is a valid compression before
composition, but relative gauge alignment incurs a linear information
charge exactly when a mixed cycle is born.

## 6. Composition law and scope

Color each column by its source fragment.  If every source is internally
cycle-contracting, Theorem MC.2 implies:

> the union fails to contract if and only if it contains a positive-excess
> circuit using at least two colors.

More generally, contraction of every union of at most `k` sources is
equivalent to contraction on every circuit whose set of colors has size at
most `k`.  Theorem MC.5 proves that this hierarchy is strict at every
arity.

The approximate **antipodal defect bound** is also strong enough to falsify
proposed bounded-arity certificates.  If all remaining mixed circuits have defect at
most `eta`, then (MC.13) gives

```math
D-\ell(t)\le \nu\eta.                          \tag{MC.28}
```

Thus bounded local defect is harmless only when its product with the new
cycle-space dimension is submacroscopic.  Conversely, disjoint circuit
blocks attain the product bound.  Composition can therefore amplify a
microscopic per-circuit error precisely through cycle-space growth.

### Coding and shortest-path readings

For a systematic binary parity-check matrix, `q_e` records the quotient
syndrome of a nonsystematic column and `a_e` its systematic component.
Mixed holonomy is the systematic syndrome of a parity relation that exists
only after code fragments are appended together.  MC.1 is an exact
coset-leader identity, while MC.4 says how many new relation syndromes must
be glued.

Equivalently, in the Cayley graph of `W direct-sum Q`, local changes of
section are graph isomorphisms fixing the kernel layer.  A mixed circuit is
the first closed quotient walk on which the accumulated kernel voltage is
observable.  Its Hamming displacement competes with its walk length in
(MC.7).  These are two standard interpretations of the same theorem, not
claims of two unrelated applications.

### What is and is not new here

* Equation (MC.9) is ordinary syndrome elimination/dynamic programming.
* Theorems MC.3--MC.4 are an elementary gauge exact sequence, related to the usual
  voltage/holonomy description of lifted graphs and represented matroids.
* Theorems MC.4--MC.6 together with (MC.13) locate the exact new feature
  created by composition, prove an all-arity obstruction, give its robust
  nullity-amplification law, and show that one mixed feature already has
  linear response-metric entropy.

This does not prove that arbitrary syndrome supports admit no useful
approximate quotient.  It does rule out any quotient that stores only
fragment-internal cycle data or checks interactions to bounded arity.  A
successful composable state must either retain mixed-holonomy gluing data,
or impose a deterministic synchronization principle that predicts it from
strictly smaller information.  Nor is the exact state automatically small:
`D kappa` can itself be macroscopic or larger.  MC.4 is a growth law and a
diagnostic for compression, not a claim that exact holonomy storage solves
the general rate--distortion problem.
