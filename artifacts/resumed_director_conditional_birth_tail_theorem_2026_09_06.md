# Principal-angle tail control for an actual Gaussian response birth

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: exact theorem, independently reconstructed by both
the bound auditor and the response researcher.
This gives a quantitative certificate for a new mask that is correlated
with the old inverse. It does not replace that inverse by its univariate
conditional mean or assume that old and new masks are independent.

## 1. Gaussian geometry and the uniform tail theorem

Let the old Gaussian space be generated by an independent finite or
countable standard vector X. Let V be a finite standard orthogonal
subvector in its first chaos. Let Z be d additional orthonormal
first-chaos Gaussians, orthogonal to V but not necessarily independent
of all X. Suppose the projection of span(Z) into the old first chaos
orthogonal to V has operator norm at most theta<1.

For any old-measurable g in L2, set G(V,Z)=E[g|V,Z], and expand in the
Z variables using normalized multivariate Hermites:

    G(V,Z)=sum_alpha g_alpha(V) h_alpha(Z).

Let G_K retain |alpha|<=K. Then

    ||G-G_K||_2 <= theta^(K+1) ||g||_2.                  (1)

The bound is independent of the polynomial degree, dimension, or detailed
distribution of g. If theta=0 the conditional projection has no positive
Z-degree. At theta=1 no vanishing bound is asserted.

Proof: a singular-value decomposition of the old/new Gaussian covariance
gives independent old coordinates Y_j, independent fresh Gaussians W_j,
and numbers 0<=theta_j<=theta such that, after orthogonal changes within
the Z-space,

    Z_j=theta_j Y_j+sqrt(1-theta_j^2)W_j.

V is independent of these Y,W. First condition g on (V,Y), then apply
the Gaussian conditional expectation channel from Y to Z. Its action on
h_alpha is multiplication by product_j theta_j^(alpha_j). Parseval
therefore bounds the tail by theta^(2K+2) times the squared norm of the
first conditional expectation, which is at most ||g||_2^2. Orthogonal
changes preserve total Hermite degree, proving (1).

For one new coordinate, if b_j=E[Z X_j] in an orthonormal old frame
containing V, then theta^2=sum_j b_j^2, since E[ZV]=0. This is exactly
the parameter computed by the rich-core birth program. It is not the
variance of Z, which remains one.

## 2. Error in a weighted conditional extremal certificate

Let 0<=H(V,Z)<=1 be a measurable mask, k(V) a known L2 function, a a real
coefficient, and t>=0. Put

    M(V)=E[H|V], W(V)=E[H g|V],
    W_K(V)=E[H G_K(V,Z)|V].

Weighted conditional Jensen gives

    E H Psi(a*g+k(V),t)
      >= E M Psi(a*W/M+k(V),t),                         (2)

where the contribution is zero when M=0. Since Psi is 1-Lipschitz in
its first argument, the right side differs from the expression with
W_K in place of W by at most

    |a| E|W-W_K|
       <= |a| sqrt(EH) theta^(K+1)||g||_2.              (3)

Indeed H is (V,Z)-measurable, so W=E[H G|V], and conditional contraction
followed by Cauchy--Schwarz gives E|W-W_K|<=E H|G-G_K|.
Combining (2)--(3) yields the explicit lower certificate

    E H Psi(a*g+k(V),t)
      >= E M Psi(a*W_K/M+k(V),t)
                         -|a|sqrt(EH)theta^(K+1)||g||_2. (4)

Thus a new mask may depend strongly on the newly created Gaussian, yet
only finitely many conditional Hermite coefficients of the OLD inverse
are needed for a certified response value, provided the principal angle
is separated from one. The missing coefficients are paid before any
normalization by a possibly small M, so there is no inverse-mask-mass
blow-up in (4).

For H_tau=(1-tau)H_old(V)+tau H_new(V,Z), the bound improves to
|a|tau sqrt(EH_new) theta^(K+1)||g||_2, for K>=0. Every omitted positive
Z-degree has zero V-conditional mean, so the old-mask part contributes
no truncation error. The symbols tau (line parameter) and theta
(principal-angle bound) denote different quantities.

For several old inverse terms apply (1) to their linear combination,
or sum the individual bounds. For piecewise-constant Gaussian thresholds
in Z, the moments E[H h_alpha(Z)|V] are Gaussian endpoint integrals.
Finite V-bin Jensen then yields a completely finite arithmetic certificate.

## 3. The actual rich-core specialization

The current candidate uses an actual V=Ug, ||g||_2=1, and a scalar
even feedforward feature f(V). Set

    a1=<g,f(V)>, nu^2=||f||_2^2-a1^2>0,
    Z=(Uf(V)-a1 V)/nu.

Every coordinate and covariance is in the canonical creation space;
no distributional fixed point is substituted. The old rich inverse is
a finite polynomial in its independent anchor/innovation frame. The
covariances b of Z with that frame are exactly <U*X_j,f>, adjusted by
the V projection. Hence theta^2=||b||^2 is directly certifiable.

A feasible line of old and new conditional response pairs can be purified
using an independent even gate in the actual creation frame. This preserves
the old first-chaos coefficients and the averaged mask value, and makes
the residual variance p_line-||K_line||_2^2 valid. It is not legitimate to
replace that variance by one derived from a refreshed product-spin law.
The full nonlinear response theorem then turns (4), after its usual finite
approximations and spectral-deletion limit, into an ORIGINAL signing lower
bound. All geometric and truncation errors are explicit.

The numerical diagnostic has theta^2 about .303. A certificate must use
an outward upper bound, not that decimal. For example any proved
theta^2<=1/3 gives ||G-G_20||_2<=3^(-21/2), independently of the degree-200
old inverse. Formula (4) states exactly how that error affects the bound.

This theorem does not guarantee theta<1 at every subsequent birth, a
depth-uniform gain, a sharp response constant, or convergence of M_n.
Its content is a genuinely uniform, computable tail bound for this
correlated response update, not another independence approximation.
