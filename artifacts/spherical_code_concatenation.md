# Spherical cut-code concatenation: exact obstructions

## 1. Normalization

Let
\[
E_n=\binom{[n]}2,\qquad
P_A(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_n}a_{ij}x_ix_j,
\qquad a_{ij}\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
The test characters are unchanged by \(x\mapsto-x\), so they are the
characters of
\[
G_n=\{\pm1\}^n/\{\pm\mathbf 1\}\cong\mathbb F_2^{\,n-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
M_n=\min_{a\in\{\pm1\}^{E_n}}\|P_A\|_{L^\infty(G_n)}.
\]
Equivalently, this is the \(n^{3/2}\)-order deficit of the covering
radius of the augmented cut code.

The purpose of this note is to test whether the usual bent-function,
Reed--Muller, Johnson-scheme, or Schmidt concatenations can preserve
this norm under a change of vertex order.

## 2. Restricting ambient bent functions to the weight-two sphere is exactly an \(\mathbb F_2\)-rank condition

For a signing \(A\), put
\[
b_{ij}=\frac{1-a_{ij}}2\in\mathbb F_2
\]
and let \(B=(b_{ij})\) be the symmetric zero-diagonal binary matrix.
Define the quadratic Boolean function
\[
q_B(z)=\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}z_iz_j.
\]
For every weight-two point,
\[
(-1)^{q_B(e_i+e_j)}=(-1)^{b_{ij}}=a_{ij}.                 \tag{2.1}
\]
Consequently **every edge signing is the restriction to the
weight-two sphere of a quadratic Boolean function**.

The polar form of \(q_B\) is
\[
q_B(u+v)+q_B(u)+q_B(v)=u^TBv.
\]
The standard squaring argument for the Walsh transform gives:

> **Quadratic plateau theorem.**  If
> \(r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}B\), then the Walsh transform of
> \((-1)^{q_B}\) takes values in
> \(\{0,\pm2^{\,n-r/2}\}\).  In particular, for even \(n\), \(q_B\) is
> bent if and only if \(B\) is nonsingular.

So the ambient bent/plateaued classification, after restriction to
the edge sphere, remembers only
\(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}B\).  That statistic does not
control the real cut transform.

Indeed, let \(n\) be even and take \(B\) to be the adjacency matrix of
a perfect matching.  Then \(B\) is nonsingular over \(\mathbb F_2\),
so \(q_B\) is bent, while the restricted signing has minus signs only
on the matching.  Hence
\[
M(A)\ge P_A(\mathbf1)=\binom n2-n=\Theta(n^2).             \tag{2.2}
\]

At the opposite extreme there are nonsingular alternating \(B\) for
which \(M(A)=O(n^{3/2})\).  To see this without a construction, choose
the upper-triangular entries of \(B\) independently and uniformly.
Then the associated \(a_{ij}\)'s are independent signs, and
\[
\Pr\{M(A)>t\}\le
2^n\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2\binom n2}\right).             \tag{2.3}
\]
For \(n=2m\), a uniform alternating matrix is nonsingular with
probability
\[
\prod_{j=1}^m(1-2^{1-2j}),
\]
which is bounded below by a positive absolute constant.  Taking
\[
t=\sqrt{2\binom n2\bigl(n\log2+O(1)\bigr)}
\]
makes the event in (2.3) smaller than this constant.  Therefore the
two events intersect.

We have proved:

> **Bent-restriction no-go.**  Within the single class of restrictions
> of quadratic bent functions, the spherical cut norm ranges from
> \(O(n^{3/2})\) to \(\Theta(n^2)\).  Ambient bentness gives no
> scale-transfer information for \(M_n\).

This also identifies exactly what happens to the elementary bent
recursion.  The direct sum
\[
q'(z,u,v)=q_B(z)+uv
\]
has polar matrix \(B\oplus\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\).
On the weight-two sphere it preserves the old signing, makes the
new edge negative, and makes every old--new edge positive.  Iterating
this recursion gives precisely the matching-negative family in
(2.2).  Thus the recursion that preserves the full Walsh optimum
generates a worst-order spherical signing.

This is the precise point at which the
Maiorana--McFarland/Schmidt analogy fails: their construction controls
a full-group Walsh transform, whereas restriction to weight two turns
the decisive product into an uncontrolled complete cross block.
For the full-cube recursion and its role in proving asymptotic
optimality, see K.-U. Schmidt, *Asymptotically optimal Boolean
functions*, arXiv:1711.08215, especially the recursion
\(\mu_{n+2}\le\mu_n\) generated by \(F(y)+uv\).

## 3. Tensor products leave the weight-two layer

If \(A\) and \(D\) use disjoint variable sets, then
\[
P_A(x)P_D(y)
=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_n}\sum_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\in E_m}
 a_{ij}d_{\alpha\beta}x_ix_jy_\alpha y_\beta.             \tag{3.1}
\]
Every character in (3.1) has Hamming weight four.  Therefore its
orthogonal projection onto the weight-two Fourier layer is zero.
The exact product operation behind bent-function concatenation simply
does not define a nonzero product on edge signings.

If instead one uses a product vertex set and tests (3.1) only on
rank-one spin matrices \(X_{i\alpha}=x_i y_\alpha\), then the transform
does factor.  But the cut code of \(K_{nm}\) tests every
\(X\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times m}\), not just the rank-one matrices.  Passing
from the Segre set to the full cube is exactly the multichannel
bilinear/vector relaxation that destroys scalar amplification.

For a disjoint-union construction on \(n+m\) vertices, flatness forces
an \(n\times m\) cross signing \(C\), and
\[
P(x,y)=P_A(x)+P_D(y)+x^TCy.                               \tag{3.2}
\]
The mixed term cannot be lower order.  For every sign matrix \(C\),
\[
\|C\|_{\infty\to1}
=\max_{x,y}|x^TCy|
\ge n\,\mathbb E|\varepsilon_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_m|.     \tag{3.3}
\]
In particular, for \(n=m\),
\[
\|C\|_{\infty\to1}\ge
\bigl(\sqrt{2/\pi}+o(1)\bigr)n^{3/2}.
\]
Moreover, maximizing over the relative global sign of the blocks
gives the exact identity
\[
\max_{x,y}|P_A(x)+P_D(y)+x^TCy|
=\max_{x,y}\bigl(|P_A(x)+P_D(y)|+|x^TCy|\bigr).           \tag{3.4}
\]
Thus a concatenation estimate based only on the two child sup norms
necessarily pays a leading-scale mixed error.  Any viable
concatenation has to prove a new anti-alignment theorem between
energy layers and bilinear level sets; ordinary tensor or code
concatenation does not supply one.

Representation-theoretically, this is the unavoidable
\(\mathrm{Std}_n\otimes\mathrm{Std}_m\) summand in the restriction of
the weight-two \(S_{n+m}\)-module to \(S_n\times S_m\).

## 4. The complete ordinary Johnson scheme is blind

Regard the positive support of a signing as a subset \(Y\) of the
vertices of \(J(n,2)\).  The Johnson relations on two distinct edges
record only whether they share one endpoint or are disjoint.

If \(Y=E(H)\) for a \(d\)-regular graph \(H\), then
\[
|Y|=\frac{nd}{2},
\]
the number of ordered distinct pairs in \(Y\) sharing a vertex is
\[
n\,d(d-1),
\]
and the number of ordered disjoint pairs is
\[
|Y|(|Y|-1)-n\,d(d-1).                                    \tag{4.1}
\]
Hence every \(d\)-regular graph has exactly the same full inner
distribution in \(J(n,2)\), and therefore exactly the same values for
every matrix in the Bose--Mesner algebra and every ordinary Delsarte
transform.  This is an all-degree obstruction, not a fixed-degree
moment objection.

Nevertheless those graphs can have radically different cut
transforms.  For \(n=2m\), let \(H_{\rm bad}\) be the disjoint union of
two \(m\)-cliques.  It is \((m-1)\)-regular, and its signing is
\[
A_{\rm bad}=ss^T-I
\]
for the balanced vector \(s\) distinguishing the cliques.  Thus
\[
M(A_{\rm bad})=\binom n2=\Theta(n^2).                     \tag{4.2}
\]

On the other hand, an \((m-1)\)-regular graph with all nonprincipal
adjacency eigenvalues \(O(\sqrt n)\) exists (for example a typical
dense random regular graph; the required spectral estimate is a
standard theorem for dense random regular graphs).  Its signing
\[
A_{\rm good}=2\,\mathrm{Adj}(H)-(J-I)
\]
has eigenvalue \(-1\) on \(\mathbf1\), and on
\(\mathbf1^\perp\) it equals \(2\,\mathrm{Adj}(H)+I\).
Consequently
\[
M(A_{\rm good})
=\frac12\max_x|x^TA_{\rm good}x|
=O(n^{3/2}).                                               \tag{4.3}
\]
The signings in (4.2) and (4.3) have identical complete
\(J(n,2)\) Delsarte data.

One primary source for the dense random-regular spectral input is
K. Tikhomirov and P. Youssef, *The spectral gap of dense random regular
graphs*, arXiv:1610.01765.

## 5. Exact triangle-aware algebra has exponential dimension

Could one repair the Johnson scheme by retaining the defining cut
relations
\[
\chi_{ij}\chi_{jk}\chi_{ki}=1,
\qquad \chi_{ij}(x)=x_ix_j?
\]
There is an exact algebraic obstruction.

> **Full-closure lemma.**  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a unital pointwise
> subalgebra of functions on \(G_n\) containing every edge character
> \(\chi_{ij}\).  Then
> \[
> \mathcal A=\mathbb R^{G_n};
> \]
> in particular, \(\dim\mathcal A=2^{n-1}\).

Indeed, character multiplication is symmetric difference of supports.
The vectors \(e_i+e_j\) span the even-weight subspace of
\(\mathbb F_2^n\).  Products of the edge characters therefore give
every even character, and these form a basis of all functions on
\(G_n\).

More quantitatively, closure under products of at most \(t\) edge
characters already contains every even character of weight at most
\(2t\), hence has dimension at least
\[
\sum_{j=0}^t\binom n{2j}.                                 \tag{5.1}
\]
At \(t=\Theta(n)\), this is exponential.

Therefore a nonstandard harmonic projection has only two choices:

1. discard the higher even layers created by multiplication, in which
   case it is not an exact positive/sup-norm-preserving
   concatenation; or
2. retain exact cut/triangle multiplication, in which case it expands
   to the full \(2^{n-1}\)-dimensional energy histogram and is
   equivalent to the original problem.

There is no polynomial-dimensional exact algebra retraction from the
cut-character algebra to the level-two sphere for \(n\ge4\), since
the product of two disjoint edge characters has weight four.

## 6. Verdict

No scale-preserving concatenation inequality survives this audit.
The obstructions are stronger than the previously noted failure of
fixed-degree Delsarte moments:

* ambient bentness on the edge sphere is only binary rank and includes
  both \(O(n^{3/2})\) and \(\Theta(n^2)\) signings;
* the standard bent recursion produces the explicit
  matching-negative \(\Theta(n^2)\) family;
* tensor products move from weight two to weight four;
* the entire ordinary \(J(n,2)\) association scheme is unable to
  distinguish \(O(n^{3/2})\) from \(\Theta(n^2)\);
* exact triangle-aware multiplicative closure is already the full,
  exponentially large cut-character algebra.

The only remaining coding-style possibility would be a genuinely
nonmultiplicative, sign-aware projection that proves an energy-layer
anti-alignment estimate for the mixed bilinear block in (3.2).  No
such estimate was obtained here, and any construction that merely
controls rank-one product spins is insufficient.
