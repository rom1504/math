# Equal variance forces an affine-offset response tradeoff

2026-09-17. Director derivation during final composition audit.
The integrated stop-loss identity and convex-order interpretation are
classical. This quantitative corollary is recorded to prevent an invalid
transfer of the campaign's unshifted response improvements. It is not a
new impossibility theorem for the original signing problem.

## 1. Exact identity

Let X,Y be centered real random variables with finite second moments.
Set D_Z(s)=E|s+Z|-|s| and d(s)=D_X(s)-D_Y(s). Jensen gives D_Z>=0.
For s>=0, centering gives D_Z(s)=2E(-Z-s)_+; for s<=0 use
D_Z(s)=2E(Z+s)_+. Tonelli consequently proves

    integral_R D_Z(s) ds = E Z^2,
    integral_R d(s) ds = E X^2-E Y^2.                 (1)

Both nonnegative functions are integrable. In particular, if the
variances agree, no strict improvement at s=0 can extend to every offset.
This is also the elementary stop-loss characterization of convex order:
equal second moment precludes nontrivial convex domination.

## 2. Quantitative statement with a common tail budget

Assume E X^2=E Y^2=1 and both laws have linear subGaussian proxy K>=1:
Eexp(tZ)<=exp(Kt^2/2) for all real t. If

    E|X| <= E|Y|-delta,       0<delta<=1,

then some real s with |s|<=R satisfies

    E|s+X|-E|s+Y| >= delta^2/(8R),
    R=sqrt(2K log(32K/delta^2)).                       (2)

Proof. The function d is2-Lipschitz. Since d(0)<=-delta,
integral d_- >=delta^2/2, the area of the triangle
(delta-2|s|)_+. Equation(1) gives the same lower bound for
integral d_+.

For each Z, a second Tonelli calculation gives

    integral_(|s|>R) D_Z(s) ds = E(|Z|-R)_+^2
      = integral_R^infinity 2(t-R)P(|Z|>t)dt
      <=4K exp(-R^2/(2K)).

Thus the corresponding integral of |d| is at most
8K exp(-R^2/(2K))=delta^2/4. At least delta^2/4 of
the positive area lies in[-R,R]. Dividing by its length proves(2).
All assertions are finite-dimensional and nonasymptotic. No symmetry
of X or Y is required beyond their stated centering.

## 3. Exact relationship to the sign construction

For an exactly isotropic physical sign law, X=h.x/sqrt(n) has variance
one for every Boolean x. Taking Y standard Gaussian shows that every
positive normalized unshifted discount, with a fixed common subGaussian
proxy, entails a positive affine-offset loss at a quantitatively bounded
offset. The campaign's non-Gaussian variance mixtures therefore cannot
dominate independent Gaussian fields in every shifted absolute response.

This does NOT invalidate the all-offset APPROXIMATION theorem: that
theorem compares physical signs with their non-Gaussian variance mixture,
not with an ordinary Gaussian. Nor does it prove that the damaging
offset is generated by actual optimal child signings, or that an averaged
parent value cannot improve. Such quantifiers are not supplied by(2).

For one child field the exact optimization is
max{sqrt(n)u+B_+,-sqrt(n)u+B_-}=constant+sqrt(n)|u-s|.
Consequently the physical-to-mixture replacement can retain the child
exactly, but its value cannot be bounded favorably just by applying the
unshifted discount uniformly to these offsets. A proof must exploit their
actual joint distribution or another property of the full parent.

This clarifies a remaining proof obligation rather than resolving it.

## 4. Positive complement: unimodal offset averaging rescues the gain

There is an exact sufficient condition under which the necessary pointwise
loss disappears AFTER averaging the offsets. Let S=A U, where U is uniform
on[-1,1], A>=0 is independent of U, and E A<infinity. This includes an
atom at zero. We use this explicit uniform-scale-mixture representation;
no structural assumption about actual child offsets is inferred from it.
Let G be standard Gaussian independent of S and set

    F(v)=E|S+sqrt(v)G|,          v>0.

For fixed A=a, heat differentiation, or direct integration, gives

    F'_a(v)=[2 Phi(a/sqrt(v))-1]/(2a),
    F''_a(v)=-phi(a/sqrt(v))/(2v^(3/2)).              (3)

At a=0 use the limits F_0(v)=sqrt(2v/pi) and
F'_0(v)=phi(0)/sqrt(v). The derivatives are uniformly bounded on
every compact positive v-interval, so averaging(3) is justified.
In particular F is concave.

Consequently, if V is independent of S,G, has E V=1, and
0<l<=V<=u<infinity, then

    E|S+sqrt(V)G| <= E|S+G|,

and quantitatively

    E|S+G|-E|S+sqrt(V)G|
      >=[E exp(-A^2/(2l))] Var(V)/(4 sqrt(2pi) u^(3/2)). (4)

Indeed -F''(v)>=E exp(-A^2/(2l))/(2 sqrt(2pi) u^(3/2))
on[l,u]; subtracting this quadratic curvature and applying Jensen proves
(4). Thus fixed-offset improvement is impossible, but a checkable class
of independent random offsets reverses the conclusion. Gaussian offsets
are an especially simple example: directly F(v)=kappa sqrt(tau^2+v)
when S is centered Gaussian with variance tau^2.

For a fixed signed old branch of the child optimization, the cavity
offset from all OTHER bridge columns is independent of the replaced
column. If, under every successive hybrid replacement, its law has
the above representation, then each Gaussian-to-variance-mixture field
replacement decreases the expected optimized branch value. Formula(4)
quantifies the gain; at feature fraction t it uses Var(V_eff)=t^2 Var(V),
where V_eff=1+(V-1)t. A physical all-offset approximation pays exactly
the previously proved q sqrt(n) epsilon error. Old-code maximization
still pays its separate fluctuation cost.

The hypothesis is NOT automatic from symmetry, Gaussian input fields,
or exact child optimization. Clipping a centered continuous field at
two nonzero endpoints already creates a symmetric law with endpoint
atoms, outside this uniform-scale-mixture class. No assertion of
unimodality for actual optimal-child cavity fields is made here. This
is a precise possible mechanism and an elementary general inequality,
not an original-problem recurrence or an external novelty claim.

## 5. One actual sign edge already defeats unconditional concavity

The condition above is not merely a technical convenience. Take the
actual two-spin signing D_12=+1 and independent Gaussian fields
g_1~N(0,v), g_2~N(0,100). Its positive optimized branch is exactly

    max_(y_1,y_2) [y_1 y_2+g_1 y_1+g_2 y_2]
       =max(|g_2|,1)+|g_1+S|,
    S=clip(g_2,-1,1).

For the second term F(v), differentiation under the bounded offset law
gives

    F''(v)=E[phi(S/sqrt(v))(S^2-v)]/(2v^(5/2)).      (5)

At v=1/4 this is strictly POSITIVE. Indeed the interior probability
p=P(|g_2|<1)<.08, since its density is at most phi(0)/10<.04.
The endpoints contribute at least .92*.05*.75=.0345 to the numerator,
using phi(2)>.05. The entire interior contributes at least
-.08*.4*.25=-.008. Hence F''(1/4)>.0265/(1/16)=.424.
The elementary constants follow from pi>25/8, pi<32/9 and e^2<15/2;
no numerical quadrature is needed for this sign conclusion.

Thus independent Gaussian fields and an actual sign-valued child do not
force variance concavity. A sufficiently small mean-preserving variance
mixture about1/4 INCREASES this branch's expected value. The other field's
variance100 is material: this is not an unconditional isotropic bridge
law, an optimizing-child asymptotic example, or a full-parent no-go.
It falsifies only the unrestricted SIGNED-BRANCH child-field concavity
shortcut. In fact for this very example the full absolute cap is exactly
1+|g_1|+|g_2|, whose expected value IS concave in v. It must not be
advertised as a counterexample to concavity of the absolute parent.

## 6. Four actual spins falsify unrestricted absolute-cap concavity too

A distinct four-spin construction shows that the two-spin absolute
identity is not a general rescue. Let D be ANY hollow full signing on
four vertices, including an exact minimizer. Take independent Gaussian
fields g_1~N(0,v) and g_2,g_3,g_4~N(0,10000). Then

    F_D(v)=E max_y |H_D(y)+sum_i g_i y_i|

satisfies F_D''(1/4)>.3024.

Proof. Fix y_1=1, write z=(y_2,y_3,y_4), and put
w_z=|H_D(1,z)| in[0,6]. Global spin reversal preserves H_D, so
the conditional absolute maximum is exactly

    max_z [w_z+|g_1+g_other.z|]
       =(a_++a_-)/2+|g_1+S|,
    a_+=max_z(w_z+g_other.z),
    a_-=max_z(w_z-g_other.z),   S=(a_+-a_-)/2.

The quantities a_+,a_- depend only on the other three fields. On the
event E that each |g_i|>3, their maximizing words are respectively
z=sign(g_other) and -z: losing any aligned coordinate costs more than6,
the entire range of w. Write H_D(1,z)=h(z)+b(z), where h is the internal
three-spin energy and b is the three-edge row sum. Both are odd integers
in{+-1,+-3}. Thus on E,

    S=(|h+b|-|h-b|)/2,
    |S|=min(|h|,|b|) in{1,3}.

Signs of the three Gaussian fields are uniform and independent of E.
Exactly six of the eight patterns have |b|=1, so at least3/4 of the
conditional mass on E has |S|=1. The other good-event contributions to

probability less than3*6*phi(0)/100<.072. For every possible S its
curvature numerator is at least-v phi(0)>-.1. Therefore

    E[phi(2S)(S^2-1/4)]
       >(.75)(.928)(.05)(.75)-(.072)(.1)=.0189.

Division by2(1/4)^(5/2)=1/16 proves the claim. The offset is bounded
by3, so all derivatives and conditional expectations are justified.

This falsifier concerns the FULL absolute cap and even permits choosing
