# Dual cut cone, midpoint tangents, and the diffuse obstruction

## 1. Setup

For a signing \(A=(a_{ij})\), write

\[
H_A(x)=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}x_ix_j.
\]

Switch so that \(\mathbf1\) is a top state, and choose a bottom state
\(y\):

\[
P=H_A(\mathbf1)=\max H_A,\qquad
H_A(y)=-Q=\min H_A.
\]

Put

\[
W=\frac{P+Q}{2},\qquad d=\frac{P-Q}{2}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(\operatorname{CUT}_n\) be the cone generated by ordinary
\(0/1\) cut vectors \(\delta(S)\), and let

\[
K_n=\operatorname{CUT}_n^*
=\{w:w\cdot\delta(S)\ge0\text{ for every }S\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

A coefficient vector \(w\) lies in \(K_n\) exactly when
\(\mathbf1\) is a top state of its quadratic form.

## 2. Exact tangent decomposition

Orient the bottom state as a new top signing:

\[
\widetilde a_{ij}=-a_{ij}y_iy_j.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
H_{\widetilde A}(\mathbf1)=Q=\max H_{\widetilde A},
\]

so both

\[
A,\widetilde A\in K_n.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Let

\[
U=\{i:y_i=-1\},\qquad V=U^c,
\]

and define

\[
c=\frac{A+\widetilde A}{2},\qquad
h=\frac{A-\widetilde A}{2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then:

* \(c\) is supported exactly on \(U\times V\), with
  \(c_{ij}=a_{ij}\);
* \(h\) is supported exactly on \(E(U)\cup E(V)\), with
  \(h_{ij}=a_{ij}\);
* \(c+h=A\) and \(c-h=\widetilde A\) both lie in \(K_n\);
* their total masses are

  \[
  \boxed{c\cdot\mathbf1=W,\qquad h\cdot\mathbf1=d.}
  \tag{2.4}
  \]

The last identities follow from

\[
H_A(\mathbf1)-H_A(y)
=2\sum_{i\in U,j\in V}a_{ij}=P+Q.
\]

Because \(c\pm h\in K_n\), every cut obeys the exact order-interval
inequality

\[
\boxed{
|h\cdot\delta(S)|\le c\cdot\delta(S).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus midpoint imbalance is precisely a tangent direction \(h\) inside
the dual cut cone, dominated on every cut by the cross-supported point
\(c\).

## 3. Cross-kernel form

Write

\[
C=(a_{ij})_{i\in U,j\in V},\qquad
B=A[U],\qquad D=A[V].
\tag{3.1}
\]

For \(p\in\{\pm1\}^{U}\), \(q\in\{\pm1\}^{V}\), let \(S\) be the cut
encoded by the combined spin \((p,q)\).  Then

\[
c\cdot\delta(S)=\frac{W-p^\mathsf TCq}{2}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
h\cdot\delta(S)
=\frac{d-H_B(p)-H_D(q)}2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently (2.5) is exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\left|d-H_B(p)-H_D(q)\right|
\le W-p^\mathsf TCq
\qquad(p,q).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
\|C\|_{\infty\to1}=W,
\tag{3.5}
\]

and every \(r\)-near maximizing cross orientation satisfies

\[
p^\mathsf TCq\ge W-r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left|H_B(p)+H_D(q)-d\right|\le r.
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the exact bridge between dual-cone geometry and low-slack
traffic entropy: the near-maximizer layer of the rectangular form must
lie in an equally thin internal-energy tube around the midpoint.

## 4. Minimal-face theorem

Let \(F(c)\) be the minimal face of \(K_n\) containing \(c\).

### Proposition 4.1

\[
c+h,\ c-h\in F(c).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every active cut at \(c\) annihilates the midpoint direction:

\[
\boxed{
c\cdot\delta(S)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h\cdot\delta(S)=0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

If \(c\) spans an extreme ray of \(K_n\), then \(h=0\), and hence

\[
d=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

The identity

\[
c=\frac12(c+h)+\frac12(c-h)
\]

expresses \(c\) as a strict convex combination of two points of
\(K_n\).  Every face containing \(c\) must therefore contain both
endpoints, proving (4.1).  Equation (4.2) follows either from the face
description or directly from (2.5).

If \(F(c)\) is a ray, then \(h\) is proportional to \(c\).  Their
supports are disjoint, so \(h=0\). \(\square\)

In cross-matrix language, an active cut is exactly a bilinear
maximizer:

\[
c\cdot\delta(S)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p^\mathsf TCq=W.
\]

Thus all exact maximizing orientations obey

\[
\boxed{
H_B(p)+H_D(q)=d.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The useful issue is not the catalog of triangle or hypermetric rays by
itself, but the size and conditioning of the minimal face exposed by
the maximizing orientations of \(C\).

## 5. Near-maximizer graph criterion

For \(r\ge0\), form a bipartite graph \(\mathcal G_r(C)\):

* its left vertices are \(p\in\{\pm1\}^{U}\);
* its right vertices are \(q\in\{\pm1\}^{V}\);
* \(p\sim q\) when \(p^\mathsf TCq\ge W-r\).

Put

\[
u(p)=H_B(p)-H_B(\mathbf1),\qquad
v(q)=H_D(q)-H_D(\mathbf1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since

\[
H_B(\mathbf1)+H_D(\mathbf1)=d,
\]

equation (3.6) becomes

\[
\boxed{
|u(p)+v(q)|\le r
\qquad(p\sim q).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At \(r=0\), \(u\) is constant on the left side of each connected
component and \(v\) is its negative on the right side.  The component
containing \((\mathbf1,\mathbf1)\) has \(u=v=0\).

This gives a concrete sufficient rigidity statement.  If the
\(p\)-vertices in that component have feature differences

\[
\bigl(p_ip_j-1\bigr)_{i<j\in U}
\]

spanning \(\mathbb R^{E(U)}\), then \(B=0\), impossible when
\(|U|\ge2\).  The analogous statement holds on \(V\).  Quantitatively,
the least singular value of these feature differences converts (5.2)
into a bound on the corresponding projection of \(B,D\).

Hence an imbalanced signing forces the exact or near-maximizer graph
to be algebraically thin in the internal degree-two features.  This
is the face-rank version of the desired entropy-versus-replacement
dichotomy.

## 6. Covariance and restricted-invertibility consequences

Let

\[
\mathcal L_r
=\{S:c\cdot\delta(S)\le r\}
\tag{6.1}
\]

and let \(\mu\) be any probability measure supported on
\(\mathcal L_r\).  Restrict the cut vectors to the internal edge set
\(E(U)\cup E(V)\), and define

\[
X=\delta(S)|_{E(U)\cup E(V)},\qquad
M_\mu=\mathbb E_\mu[XX^\mathsf T].
\tag{6.2}
\]

From (2.5),

\[
|h\cdot X|\le r
\]

pointwise.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
h^\mathsf TM_\mu h\le r^2.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If a subspace \(L\) satisfies

\[
M_\mu\succeq\lambda\Pi_L,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_Lh\|_2\le\frac r{\sqrt\lambda}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Let \(e\) be the all-one vector on the internal edges, so
\(d=h\cdot e\).  If \(e\) belongs to the range of \(M_\mu\), generalized
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the effective-resistance bound

\[
\boxed{
d^2\le r^2\,e^\mathsf TM_\mu^\dagger e.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Equations (6.3)--(6.5) are the exact covariance implications requested
by the restricted-invertibility strategy.  They show:

> If a low-slack layer admits a measure whose cut-feature covariance is
> well conditioned in the internal all-one direction, then a large
> midpoint is impossible.

Conversely, a midpoint \(d\) forces every measure on that layer either
to have large effective resistance in direction \(e\), or to have
\(e\) substantially outside its covariance range.

There is also a degree-two barycenter form.  Let

\[
\phi(p,q)=
\bigl((p_ip_j)_{i<j\in U},(q_iq_j)_{i<j\in V}\bigr)
\]

and \(m_\mu=\mathbb E_\mu\phi\).  From (3.6),

\[
\boxed{
|d-h\cdot m_\mu|\le r,
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\|m_\mu\|_2
\ge
\frac{(|d|-r)_+}{\sqrt{\binom{|U|}{2}+\binom{|V|}{2}}}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

For two independent samples
\((p,q),(p',q')\sim\mu\), Parseval rewrites the squared barycenter as

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|m_\mu\|_2^2
=\frac12\mathbb E\big[
&(p\cdot p')^2-|U|\\
&+(q\cdot q')^2-|V|
\big].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus a large midpoint forces a measurable excess in the two-replica
squared-overlap profile of every sufficiently low-slack cross layer.

## 7. Exact second-moment bounds and their scale

Squaring (3.4) and averaging over independent uniform \(p,q\) gives

\[
\boxed{
d^2+\binom{|U|}{2}+\binom{|V|}{2}
\le
W^2+|U||V|.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For fixed \(q\), averaging only over \(p\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(d-H_D(q)\bigr)^2+\binom{|U|}{2}
\le
W^2+\|Cq\|_2^2.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

There is a symmetric formula with \(U,V\) interchanged.

For a balanced partition, the difference between the edge-count terms
in (7.1) is only \(O(n)\).  Since \(W^2\) is of order \(n^3\), this
does not imply \(d=o(n^{3/2})\).  It rigorously confirms that bare
second moments of the cone interval miss the desired scale.

## 8. Why covariance does not automatically give an entropy-rate bound

A single ill-conditioned direction does not force a subset of the
cube to have small exponential entropy.  More quantitatively, a
degree-two barycenter of precisely the size forced by a diffuse
midpoint can be purchased at sublinear entropy cost.

Take independent signs with common mean

\[
\rho=\gamma n^{-1/4}.
\]

Then every pair correlation is

\[
\mathbb E p_ip_j=\rho^2=\gamma^2n^{-1/2},
\]

and over \(\Theta(n^2)\) internal pairs the degree-two barycenter has

\[
\|m\|_2^2=\Theta(\gamma^4n).
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is exactly the \(\Theta(n)\) squared norm required by (6.7) when
\(d=\Theta(n^{3/2})\).  But the entropy loss of the product measure
from the uniform cube is only

\[
\Theta(n\rho^2)=\Theta(\sqrt n),
\tag{8.2}
\]

which is \(o(n)\).  Therefore degree-two covariance or barycenter
information alone cannot force a strict entropy-rate loss.

The same limitation is visible geometrically.  Top-switching maps
*every* signing into \(K_n\); the \(\{\pm1\}\)-points of the dual cut
cone, with a distinguished top state, still parameterize arbitrary
switching classes.  A theorem using only cone membership would simply
be a restatement of the original problem.

Triangle facets do not fix this.  The all-positive vector has the
explicit triangle-cone decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf1_E
=\frac1{n-2}
\sum_{\{i,j,k\}}
\left(
t_{ij|k}+t_{ik|j}+t_{jk|i}
\right),
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

where, for example, \(t_{ij|k}\) is the triangle-inequality normal
having coefficients \(+1,+1,-1\) on the three edges, with \(ij\) the
negative edge.  The three oriented normals on one triangle sum to the
all-one vector on that triangle.  Thus the worst \(\Theta(n^2)\)
midpoint local trap already lies in the cone generated by triangle
facets.

Full-support hypermetric rays give the opposite warning.  If
\(\beta_i\in\{\pm1\}\), \(\sum_i\beta_i=1\), then

\[
a_{ij}=-\beta_i\beta_j
\]

is the normal of a hypermetric inequality and is a
\(\{\pm1\}\)-valued element of \(K_n\).  Its remote cuts have quadratic
size, so it fails the competitive two-sided width bound.  Hence
cataloging triangle and hypermetric generators does not by itself
separate the competitive interval.

## 9. Surviving quantitative target

The cone route has produced an exact conditional dichotomy, but not a
proof of centering:

1. if the low-slack cross layer is well conditioned in its internal
   degree-two features, (6.5)--(6.7) bound the midpoint;
2. if it is not, it lies near one or more quadratic-feature
   hyperplanes, providing candidate global replacement directions;
3. a single such hyperplane costs only sublinear entropy, so one needs
   either linearly many independent directions or a replacement
   theorem exploiting the specific low-slack graph \(\mathcal G_r(C)\).

The precise next lemma is:

> For a global width minimizer, either the near-maximizer graph
> \(\mathcal G_r(C)\) has enough restricted invertibility in the
> degree-two features to make (6.5) imply
> \(d=o(n^{3/2})\), or its feature-kernel supplies a correlated
> multiblock edge replacement satisfying the exact criterion in
> `global_block_replacement_midpoint.md`.

The first branch is now a linear-algebra calculation.  The second
branch remains the genuinely new combinatorial step.
