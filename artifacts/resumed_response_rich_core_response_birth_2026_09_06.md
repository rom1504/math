## 3. Exact two-variable projection of the rich inverse

For every core coordinate E_j let t_j=E[h_j(E)f(V)], where h_j=U*E_j.
For the canonical anchors, if d_j is the child degree and
v_j=E[h_j h_(d_j)(V)], then

    t_j=v_j E[f(V)h_(d_j)(V)].

For the innovation coordinate use

    t_*=(A-sum_j rho_j*t_j)/s.

The covariance of E with Z is b_core=(t-A*(rho,s))/nu. It satisfies
b_core.(rho,s)=0 and |b_core|<=1. This covariance is computed from the
actual inverse isometry, not by asserting that Z is external to the core.

Write a_res>0 for the core resolvent parameter and N for the norm of its
unnormalized, anchor-stripped inverse. With beta_d the original center
Hermite coefficients, c_j=beta_(d_j)*v_j, the exact polynomial inverse is

    g=sum_j [rho_j-s*c_j/(a_res*N)]h_j(E)
          +(s/N) sum_(d even<=200) beta_d R_(a_res)h_d(V).

Here R_a integrates the partial OU semigroup in Z_* against r^(a-1)dr
on [0,1], so innovation Hermite degree ell has eigenvalue 1/(a+ell).
The d=0 term cancels the removed constant, leaving Eg=rho_0.

Under that partial OU operation,

    V_r=rho.A+s*r*Z_*+s*sqrt(1-r^2)*N_external,
    Cov(V_r,V)=R^2+s^2*r=:A_r,
    Cov(V_r,Z)=s*(r-1)*b_core,*=:B_r.

Therefore, exactly,

    E[h_d(V_r)|V,Z]
       =sum_(k=0)^d sqrt(binomial(d,k)) A_r^(d-k) B_r^k
                                  h_(d-k)(V)h_k(Z).       (2)

Integrating r^(a_res-1) gives every coefficient of E[g|V,Z] by finite
polynomial arithmetic. Each anchor monomial projects similarly: expand
product_j(rho_j*x+b_core,j*y)^(multiplicity_j); the coefficient of
x^(d-k)y^k is multiplied by
sqrt((d-k)!k!/product_j multiplicity_j!). These are normalized-Hermite
coefficients, so the squared coefficient sum is the exact L2 norm.

## 4. Rectangle version suitable for exact certification

under (V,Z)->(-V,-Z). On each positive interval set F_1=+1 for Z>r_i,
F_1=-1 for Z<l_i, and F_1=0 in between, with rational l_i<=r_i. Beyond a

Every support, first-chaos moment, and rectangle integral of g is a
finite product of Gaussian endpoint integrals. In normalized Hermites,

    integral_l^r h_k(x)phi(x)dx
       =[phi(l)h_(k-1)(l)-phi(r)h_(k-1)(r)]/sqrt(k), k>=1,

and k=0 is Phi(r)-Phi(l). The same formula integrates m, u, and a
piecewise-polynomial surrogate f on each V bin. Applying (1) per bin
therefore yields a finite rational interval certificate with no nonlinear
validated quadrature and no mask/inverse independence assumption.

A useful rigorous truncation is available. Let r_b=|b_core|<1. Resolve
the core coordinates into V, B=(b_core.E)/r_b, and their orthogonal
complement. The new Z has correlation r_b with B and is independent of
V. Hence the k-th Z-Hermite coefficient in E[g|V,Z] is attenuated by
r_b^k from the corresponding coefficient of E[g|V,B]. Consequently

    ||E[g|V,Z]-P_(Z-degree<K)E[g|V,Z]||_2^2 <= r_b^(2K)||g||_2^2.

The aggregate weighted-mean error in (1) is at most
|a_theta|*theta*r_b^K: only the new-center part uses the truncated
projection, |Psi(k,t)-Psi(k',t)|<=|k-k'|, and Cauchy--Schwarz bounds
E H_1|error| by its L2 norm. Thus a modest Z-degree cutoff is safe even
though the V degree remains 200. This bound does not confuse the small
conditional projection norm with the full inverse norm, which is one.
