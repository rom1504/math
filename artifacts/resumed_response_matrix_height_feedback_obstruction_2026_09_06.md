# Matrix tree-height recursion and a sharp discontinuous-feedback obstruction

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: exact creation-space statements and explicit
two-innovation example proved below, submitted for independent audit.
This is a structural discriminator for actual same-space Gaussian
feedback. It does not infer impossibility from noncontractivity or
infinite derivative energy alone.

## 1. Exact multivariate height recursion

Let U h_T=G_T be the canonical Gaussian creation isometry. The height
of a created tree is 1 plus the maximum height of its child trees;
the edge, U1, has height one. Let F_h be the Gaussian sigma-field
generated by all canonical coordinates of height at most h, and let
Pi_h be the corresponding first-chaos projection. Set F_0 trivial and
Pi_0=0. A fixed height can contain infinitely many tree types; no
finite-cardinality assertion is needed.

For every even L2 function f,

    Pi_(h+1) U f = U E[f|F_h].                        (1)

Indeed conditional expectation retains exactly the Hermite monomials
whose child coordinates all have height at most h. Their creation
coordinates are exactly the parent trees of height at most h+1.
The identity follows first for finite Hermite sums, then by L2
continuity and isometry.

Suppose G=(G_1,...,G_d) is an orthonormal finite first-chaos frame in
this SAME canonical space, and g=(g_1,...,g_d) is an even measurable
function of G such that

    G_j=U g_j(G),  j=1,...,d.                        (2)

The inverse features are automatically orthonormal: E[g g^T]=I_d.
Define the positive semidefinite height covariance matrices

    P_h=E[(Pi_h G)(Pi_h G)^T],  P_0=0.

For 0<=P<=I, define the covariance kernel

    K(P)=E[g(X)g(Y)^T],

where X,Y are standard Gaussian d-vectors with cross covariance P.
Equivalently X=Z+N_1,Y=Z+N_2 with Z of covariance P and independent
N_1,N_2 of covariance I-P. Thus K(P) is a SECOND-MOMENT Gram matrix
of conditional expectations, not their centered covariance. In
particular K(0)=Eg(Eg)^T and K(I)=I.

The conditional law of G given F_h has mean Pi_h G and deterministic
covariance I-P_h. Applying (1), followed by isometry, gives EXACTLY

    P_(h+1)=K(P_h).                                  (3)

Since the height subspaces increase and their union contains every
canonical tree coordinate,

    0=P_0<=P_1<=...<=I,  P_h -> I.                    (4)

This is the endogenous same-space requirement. A formal Gaussian
distributional solution of (2) in an enlarged space need not satisfy
the canonical height exhaustion (4).

## 2. Loewner monotonicity and the subfixed-matrix obstruction

K is monotone on 0<=P<=I in positive-semidefinite order. To see this
for P<=Q, decompose a standard Gaussian into independent components
with covariance P, Q-P, and I-Q. Conditional expectation of g given
the first component is a further conditional expectation of g given
the first two. The difference of their second-moment Gram matrices
is positive semidefinite by conditional variance. These two Gram
matrices are K(P),K(Q), respectively.

Consequently, if there is a matrix

    0<=P<=I,  P!=I,  K(P)<=P,                        (5)

then (2) CANNOT hold in the canonical creation space. Starting with
P_0=0<=P, monotonicity and (3) imply P_h<=P for every h; taking the
limit in (4) would give I<=P, a contradiction.

No strict positive-definite gap is required in (5): some matrix
directions may be exact equalities. This matters for the rank-deficient
jump example in Section 5.

## 3. Exact anchored reduction

Let a finite canonical ancestor-closed anchor frame A be fixed, and
suppose Z=(Z_1,...,Z_k) is an orthonormal Gaussian innovation frame
orthogonal to A. Assume its inverse h(A,Z) is even, orthonormal,
and orthogonal to the anchor inverse features. The latter are
functions of A alone.

For a test matrix diag(I_anchor,Q), the two anchor copies agree
exactly. Therefore the complete kernel has block form

    K(diag(I,Q))=diag(I,K_A(Q)),
    K_A(Q)=E[h(A,X)h(A,Y)^T],                         (6)

where X,Y are standard innovation vectors with cross covariance Q,
independent of the common anchor. Anchor/innovation cross blocks
vanish by the stated exact orthogonality, not an approximation.

Thus ANY proper innovation matrix 0<=Q<=I with K_A(Q)<=Q rules out
the proposed endogenous innovation frame. One need not assume that
the actual finite-height anchor covariances have already become I:
the full-frame comparison from P_0=0 proves the obstruction directly.

For one innovation, expanding only in its Gaussian coordinate gives

    K_A(q)=sum_l w_l q^l,  w_l>=0,  sum_l w_l=1.

Actual endogenous closure requires K_A(q)>q for every 0<=q<1.
In particular derivative energy sum_l l w_l>1, including infinity,
would force K_A(q)<q close enough to one and is impossible in this
specific scalar feedback setting. This is not a statement about the
derivative energy of arbitrary feedforward output functions.

The canonical finite-resolvent innovation has derivative energy D<1.
Convexity gives K_A(q)>=1+D(q-1)>q for q<1, so it correctly passes
this necessary test despite genuinely depending on its own Gaussian
innovation through the finite polynomial inverse.

## 4. A local matrix condition, with its limitations explicit

For polynomial inverse features, K_A is differentiable at I. Its
derivative is the positive linear map

    L(D)=E[J_h D J_h^T],                              (7)

where J_h differentiates only the innovation coordinates. Gaussian
covariance differentiation, or direct Hermite expansion, proves (7).
If there exists D>0 and eta>0 such that

    L(D)>=(1+eta)D,

then Q=I-epsilon D is a subfixed matrix for sufficiently small
epsilon>0. Indeed

    K_A(I-epsilon D)=I-epsilon L(D)+O(epsilon^2),

and the positive-definite margin eta D dominates the remainder.
Section 3 then excludes endogenous closure.

This sufficient condition must not be replaced by an unqualified
claim that any large derivative entry, any infinite derivative
energy, or any expansive rank-deficient direction excludes a frame.
Degenerate output directions and feedforward dependencies require
the actual matrix inequality (5) or an additional justified argument.

## 5. One jump plus an arbitrarily weak smooth return path is impossible

Here is an exact example where the jump Gram matrix has rank one,
yet a two-coordinate feedback loop is ruled out. Let the anchor be
G_0=U1. Fix 0<r<=1 and suppose there were independent standard
Gaussian innovations Z_1,Z_2, also independent of G_0, with

    U*Z_1=sqrt(r) G_0 Z_2+sqrt(1-r) h_2(G_0),
    U*Z_2=G_0 sign(Z_1),                             (8)

where h_2(x)=(x^2-1)/sqrt(2). Both inverse features are jointly even,
have unit L2 norm, and are orthogonal to each other and to the anchor
inverse 1. These claims follow directly from independence, parity,
and normalized Hermite orthogonality.

For a diagonal innovation cross covariance diag(q_1,q_2), condition
on the shared G_0 in (6). Direct Gaussian expectation gives

    K_A(diag(q_1,q_2))
      =diag(1-r+r q_2, kappa(q_1)),
    kappa(q)=(2/pi)arcsin(q).                         (9)

The off-diagonal terms are zero because the two innovation coordinates
are independent across this diagonal coupling. The elementary sign
correlation formula in (9) follows by writing a correlated Gaussian
pair as projections of an isotropic two-dimensional Gaussian: the
opposite-sign angular sectors have total angle 2 arccos(q).

Choose the explicit proper positive-definite matrix

    Q=diag(1-r^2/2, 1-r/2).

The first entry of K_A(Q) is EXACTLY the corresponding entry of Q.
The second is strictly smaller. Indeed

    1-cos(pi r/4) <= pi^2 r^2/32 < r^2/2,

using 1-cos(x)<=x^2/2 and pi<4. Hence

    arccos(1-r^2/2)>pi r/4,
    kappa(1-r^2/2)<1-r/2.

Thus K_A(Q)<=Q<I, and the exact height obstruction rules out (8)
for EVERY positive r, however weak the smooth return coefficient.
There is no limiting argument in r and no appeal to infinite
derivative energy as an independent criterion.

At r=0, the analogous system is genuinely FEEDFORWARD and DOES exist:
first create Z_1=U h_2(G_0), then create Z_2=U[G_0 sign(Z_1)]. The
same norm and orthogonality checks make these independent Gaussian
innovations in the actual creation space. The discontinuity by itself
is therefore not the obstruction. Closing the return path with any
r>0 changes the exact matrix-height inequalities and destroys
endogeneity.

## 6. Relation to full-response stationary feedback

The stationary full-gradient inverse has the form
H s(K)-2B K, where the center mask H is defined by a threshold involving
the gradient Gaussian itself. This may create discontinuous SELF-
feedback. Sections 1--5 give an actual-space method for testing such
proposals, not a conclusion that every stationary threshold is excluded.

In particular, full-rank innovation jump covariance can support a
subfixed-matrix argument, but rank-deficient jumps need a proved return
path or another matrix comparison. The example above supplies one
exact return-path condition. Conversely W=U H(V) in the canonical
certificate is a discontinuous FEEDFORWARD addition to the V core:
the inverse of the existing core does not depend on W. It must not
be rejected using the derivative size of H.

The remaining targeted question is whether a high-value full-response
stationary threshold necessarily creates an analogous active return
path after all smooth and degenerate directions are accounted for.
That necessity is not proved here.

## 7. A rigorous one-rough-feature SELF-feedback obstruction

There is an additional exact sufficient condition which isolates a
single rough self-feedback direction without treating all infinite
derivative energies as obstructions. Let G_1,...,G_d be an orthonormal
finite creation-closed Gaussian frame with inverse vector h. Rotate
the frame if desired, then write z=G_d. Expand each inverse feature
in normalized z-Hermites, with coefficient functions of the other
coordinates:

    h_i=sum_{ell>=0} h_i,ell(G_<d) h_ell(z).

Assume

    sum_ell ell ||h_d,ell||_2^2 = infinity,          (10)
    sum_ell ell^2 ||h_i,ell||_2^2 < infinity,
                                      i=1,...,d-1. (11)

Thus the distinguished inverse feature has infinite Dirichlet energy
in its OWN Gaussian coordinate. Every other inverse feature belongs
to the partial Ornstein--Uhlenbeck generator domain in that coordinate.
Polynomials satisfy (11). The own-coordinate qualification and the
smooth-complement assumption are both material.

Then this frame cannot exist endogenously in the creation space.

To prove it, set q=1-epsilon and

    Q_epsilon=I-epsilon e_d e_d^T.

Let T_q denote the partial z-OU operator, multiplying z-Hermite
degree ell by q^ell. The defect matrix is EXACTLY

    E_epsilon=I-K(Q_epsilon),
    E_epsilon(i,j)=<h_i,(I-T_q)h_j>.                  (12)

It is positive semidefinite. Write o={1,...,d-1}. By the Hermite
series and (11),

    E_oo/epsilon -> A,
    A_ij=sum_ell ell <h_i,ell,h_j,ell>,              (13)

in matrix norm. If u belongs to ker(A), then sum_i u_i h_i has no
positive z-Hermite degree. Therefore its row and column in E_epsilon
are EXACTLY zero for every epsilon, including cross terms with h_d.
It suffices to work on range(A), where A is positive definite.

Self-adjointness moves I-T_q onto a smooth complement feature. Since
1-q^ell<=epsilon ell,

    |E_epsilon(i,d)|
      <=||(I-T_q)h_i||_2 ||h_d||_2
      <=epsilon [sum_ell ell^2 ||h_i,ell||_2^2]^(1/2).

Thus E_od=O(epsilon). On range(A), (13) makes the inverse of E_oo
O(1/epsilon), so

    E_do E_oo^(-1) E_od = O(epsilon).                (14)

In contrast, monotone convergence in the nonnegative Hermite series
and (10) give

    E_dd/epsilon
      =sum_ell [(1-q^ell)/(1-q)] ||h_d,ell||_2^2
       -> infinity.                               (15)

The Schur complement of E_epsilon-epsilon e_d e_d^T is consequently
positive for sufficiently small epsilon. The inactive ker(A) block
is exactly zero, so there is no hidden singular-block assumption.
Equivalently

    E_epsilon>=epsilon e_d e_d^T,
    K(Q_epsilon)<=Q_epsilon<=I,  Q_epsilon!=I.

Section 2 gives the contradiction. If A=0, all cross terms vanish
exactly and (15) proves the same assertion without an inverse.

This supplies a direct self-feedback test for stationary thresholds:
if one inverse feature has a genuine jump in its own Gaussian input
and all other inverse features are generator-smooth in that input,
(10)--(11) exclude endogenous closure. A genuine jump across a finite
one-dimensional threshold, on a positive-measure set of the other
coordinates with nonzero jump size, is not in the corresponding
Gaussian W^(1,2) space and has the infinite Hermite energy in (10).
One may use (10) itself as the exact assumption, avoiding any
unproved regularity assertion for complicated threshold boundaries.

The discontinuous FEEDFORWARD inverse W=U H(V) has zero energy in
its own independent W innovation, so (10) fails and it is correctly
not excluded. If other inverse features share rough dependence on
the same input, (11) can fail; the theorem then makes no claim.
General full-response stationary frames still require a proof that
their rough directions meet this condition or another actual
subfixed-matrix criterion.

## 8. Strongest finite-energy-combination criterion

The generator-domain assumption in Section 7 can be removed by a
different argument. The following statement explicitly uses FINITE
frame dimension; its compactness step does not extend automatically
to an infinite-dimensional frame.

Let D be any nonzero positive semidefinite d-by-d matrix. Define the
extended Gaussian Dirichlet form on output coefficient vectors v by

    E_D(v)=E[(gradient(v^T h))^T D gradient(v^T h)],   (16)

with value infinity if the combination is outside the corresponding
Gaussian Sobolev domain. Equivalently, diagonalize D, expand in the
noised Gaussian coordinates, and use the exact nonnegative Hermite
series below. If G=U h(G) exists in the same canonical creation space,
then there MUST be a vector v such that

    v^T D v > 0,  E_D(v) <= v^T D v.                (17)

This includes the critical equality case. Only strict expansion of
every relevant finite-energy combination yields a contradiction.

### Proof

For 0<epsilon<1/||D||op, put

    A_epsilon=[I-K(I-epsilon D)]/epsilon.

After orthogonally diagonalizing D with eigenvalues lambda_j>=0,
expand the inverse features in multivariate Hermites of the noised
coordinates, keeping coefficient functions of the unnoised ones.
Let Gamma_alpha be the positive semidefinite Gram matrix of the
coefficient vector at multiindex alpha. Then

    A_epsilon=sum_alpha w_alpha(epsilon) Gamma_alpha,
    w_alpha(epsilon)
      =[1-product_j(1-epsilon lambda_j)^(alpha_j)]/epsilon.

All weights are nonnegative. The numerator is concave in epsilon:
its second derivative is the negative of a sum of nonnegative
products on the indicated interval. Since it vanishes at zero,
w_alpha(epsilon) increases as epsilon decreases to zero. Its limit
is sum_j alpha_j lambda_j. Consequently A_epsilon is Loewner-
increasing as epsilon decreases, and

    v^T A_epsilon v increases to E_D(v).             (18)

This defines (16) even when derivatives are not initially available.

Let N={v:E_D(v)=0}. It is a linear subspace, and its vectors annihilate
EVERY A_epsilon exactly by the nonnegative Hermite expansion. Suppose
(17) were false. Then N is contained in ker(D): otherwise a vector
in N with positive D norm would already satisfy (17). For every
nonzero vector v in N-perp one therefore has

    lim_epsilon->0 v^T(A_epsilon-D)v > 0,

allowing infinity as the limit. Indeed if v has positive D norm this
is the negation of (17); if its D norm is zero, its positive extended
energy follows from v notin N.

Choose any decreasing sequence epsilon_k tending to zero. The open
sets

    {v in the unit sphere of N-perp:
                    v^T(A_epsilon_k-D)v>0}

are nested and cover that compact finite-dimensional sphere, by
(18). A finite subcover therefore reduces to one k. Hence
A_epsilon_k-D is positive definite on N-perp and zero on N. It is
positive semidefinite on the full space. This gives

    K(I-epsilon_k D)<=I-epsilon_k D,

a proper subfixed matrix, contradicting Section 2. This proves (17).

### Own-coordinate consequence

For D=e_j e_j^T, scale the vector in (17) to v_j=1. Every actual
finite endogenous frame must admit coefficients b_i such that

    h_j+sum_{i!=j} b_i h_i

has FINITE Gaussian Dirichlet energy in its own input coordinate
G_j, with that energy at most 1. In particular if h_j is rough in
G_j and every other inverse feature is merely in Gaussian W^(1,2)
in G_j, no such cancellation is possible and the frame is impossible.
This strengthens Section 7 without claiming the O(epsilon) cross
estimate under the weaker hypotheses. The proof instead uses exact
Loewner monotonicity and finite-dimensional compactness.

### Rank-deficient jumps and smooth return paths

For piecewise regular inverse features, finite-energy combinations
must cancel all jump vectors across interfaces crossed by the chosen
input noise D. Once those cancellations are explicitly checked,
(17) tests the remaining smooth combinations against their D norm.
If every such nonzero relevant combination has strictly larger
weighted energy, the formal feedback frame is unrealizable.

For the two-cycle in Section 5, take innovation D=diag(r/2,1), keeping
the anchor unnoised. Any finite-energy combination must have zero
coefficient on G_0 sign(Z_1), because the other inverse feature is
smooth. The remaining combination has energy r v_1^2, whereas its
D norm is (r/2)v_1^2. Thus (17) fails for r>0, recovering the exact
subfixed-matrix contradiction. At r=0, the remaining energy is zero
and no contradiction is obtained, as required for the actual
feedforward construction.

This criterion does not assume that other inverse features cannot
cancel a stationary threshold's jumps. That cancellation question
has to be solved in the actual finite feature span before using
(17) to exclude a proposed stationary frame.
