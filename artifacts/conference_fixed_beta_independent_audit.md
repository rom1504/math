# Independent audit of the fixed-temperature conference obstruction

Status: **the fixed-`beta` conference theorem checks out; the transfer to exact
pressure minimizers remains open**.  This note independently checks the two
delicate points in
[`conference_reverse_kl_fixed_temperature_obstruction.md`](conference_reverse_kl_fixed_temperature_obstruction.md):
the random-bridge parent law and the maximum-entry power condition.  It also
isolates two sufficient, but currently unproved, minimizer-transfer statements.

## 1. Child normalization and the new deterministic theorem

Let `A_r` be a symmetric conference signing and put

```math
U_r={A_r\over\sqrt{r-1}},\qquad U_r^2=I,\qquad
(U_r)_{ii}=0,\qquad |(U_r)_{ij}|=(r-1)^{-1/2}.
\tag{1.1}
```

For `s=beta/sqrt(r)`, the ordinary exponential in the Ising partition
function is

```math
sH_{A_r}(x)={1\over2}x^{\mathsf T}(sA_r)x.
\tag{1.2}
```

Thus the interaction matrix in Corollary 2.10(c) of Fan--Misiakiewicz--Wang--Wen,
[*Dynamical mean-field limit and replica-symmetric free energy for the
orthogonally-invariant SK model*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10102), is

```math
X_r=sA_r=\beta\sqrt{1-1/r}\,U_r.
\tag{1.3}
```

Its empirical spectral law tends to the symmetric Bernoulli law
`mu_beta=(delta_(-beta)+delta_beta)/2`.  Assumption 2.9 of that paper is exact
here: an even power of `X_r` is scalar, while an odd power is a scalar multiple
of `U_r`.  Hence its diagonal is exactly the normalized trace and its
off-diagonal entries are `O(r^(-1/2))`.  The high-temperature hypothesis is
`||X_r||_op<1/2`, so every fixed `0<beta<1/2` is allowed for the child.

At zero external field the finite-order one-spin means vanish by global spin
flip, so the paper's replica overlap `q_*` is exactly zero.  Its formula gives

```math
{1\over r}\log\left(2^{-r}\sum_x e^{sH_{A_r}(x)}\right)
\longrightarrow
\psi(\beta),
\tag{1.4}
```

where

```math
\psi(c)={1\over4}\left[
 \sqrt{1+4c^2}-1
 -\log\left({1+\sqrt{1+4c^2}\over2}\right)
 \right].
\tag{1.5}
```

The same statement holds with `A_r` replaced by `-A_r`.  Since

```math
\overline Z_r(A_r,s)
={1\over2}\left[
 2^{-r}\sum_xe^{sH_{A_r}(x)}+
 2^{-r}\sum_xe^{-sH_{A_r}(x)}
 \right],
\tag{1.6}
```

the `cosh` pressure has the same limit.  This checks every factor of two in
the child mapping.

## 2. Why the bipartite bridge is scalar-free from the child

Put `N=2r` and, before multiplication by `beta`, write

```math
K_{r,\epsilon}=D_{r,\epsilon}+W_r,
\quad
D_{r,\epsilon}={1\over\sqrt{2r}}
 \begin{pmatrix}A_r&0\\0&\epsilon A_r\end{pmatrix},
\quad
W_r={1\over\sqrt{2r}}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&B_r\\B_r^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.1}
```

Here `B_r` has iid Rademacher entries.  The block variance profile means that
one may not silently invoke ordinary Wigner freeness.  The two-block
operator-valued calculation nevertheless reduces to scalar freeness in this
special case.

Let `B_2` be the algebra generated by the two block projections and let
`E_(B_2)` be block-normalized trace.  In the limit,

```math
E_{B_2}(D^{2k})=2^{-k}I_2,
\qquad E_{B_2}(D^{2k+1})=0
\tag{2.2}
```

for both orientations.  The bipartite random block tends to a
`B_2`-semicircular element with covariance map

```math
\eta(\operatorname{diag}(a,b))
={1\over2}\operatorname{diag}(b,a).
\tag{2.3}
```

In particular, `eta(cI_2)=(c/2)I_2`.  Every block-valued moment of `D` is
scalar, and every contraction generated by (2.3) therefore remains scalar.
Alternating scalar-centered polynomials in `D` and `W` have zero scalar trace.
Consequently the two variables are scalar-free, despite the nonconstant block
variance profile, and

```math
\operatorname{ESD}(K_{r,\epsilon})
\Longrightarrow
\mu_{1/\sqrt2}\boxplus {\rm SC}(1/2).
\tag{2.4}
```

This is also obtained from the two-block Pastur system.  The two diagonal
inputs have the same symmetric spectral law, so uniqueness forces the two
block Stieltjes transforms to agree, and their common value satisfies

```math
g(z)=G_{\mu_{1/\sqrt2}}(z-g(z)/2),
\tag{2.5}
```

the scalar Pastur equation for (2.4).  In particular, the orientation does not
change the limiting law.  A fixed-walk pairing proof transfers (2.4) from a
Gaussian bridge to a Rademacher bridge: bridge-edge blocks of size at least
four lose a free vertex and are lower order.

## 3. Audit of maximum-entry power delocalization

Empirical spectral convergence alone is not enough for Assumption 2.9.  For
each fixed `j`, one needs, almost surely,

```math
\max_a\left|(K_{r,\epsilon}^j)_{aa}
 -{1\over N}\operatorname{Tr}K_{r,\epsilon}^j\right|
+\max_{a\ne b}|(K_{r,\epsilon}^j)_{ab}|
=O_{j,\eta}(N^{-1/2+\eta}).
\tag{3.1}
```

There are two separate ingredients.

First, the open-walk expectation expansion pairs every bridge edge.  Leading
pair contractions leave only scalar traces and within-block powers of `A_r`.
The latter reduce by `A_r^2=(r-1)I` to either `I` or `A_r`; collision diagrams
lose a free index.  Uniformly in the endpoints,

```math
\max_a\left|\mathbb E(K^j)_{aa}
 -{1\over N}\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}K^j\right|=O_j(N^{-1}),
\qquad
\max_{a\ne b}|\mathbb E(K^j)_{ab}|=O_j(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{3.2}
```

Second, the required centered `L^2` estimate admits a check independent of
diagram bookkeeping.  Flip one bridge entry.  This changes `K` by a rank-two
matrix `Delta` with `||Delta||_op=2/sqrt(N)`, and

```math
K'^j-K^j=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}K'^\ell\Delta K^{j-1-\ell}.
\tag{3.3}
```

For a fixed output entry, summing the squares of (3.3) over all bridge
coordinates factorizes into squared Euclidean row and column norms.  Since
`j` is fixed and the normalized Rademacher operator norm has moments bounded
uniformly in `r`, Efron--Stein gives

```math
\sup_{a,b}\operatorname{Var}[(K^j)_{ab}]\le {C_j\over N}.
\tag{3.4}
```

After reducing repeated Rademacher variables, the centered entry is a
multilinear Rademacher polynomial of degree at most `j`.  Bonami's inequality
and (3.4) imply

```math
\Pr\{|(K^j)_{ab}-\mathbb E(K^j)_{ab}|>N^{-1/2+\eta}\}
\le 2\exp(-c_{j,\eta}N^{2\eta/j}).
\tag{3.5}
```

A union bound and Borel--Cantelli prove (3.1).  This validates the stronger
power condition, not only the global law.

Finally,

```math
\limsup_r\|K_{r,\epsilon}\|_{\rm op}
\le {1\over\sqrt2}+\sqrt2={3\over\sqrt2}
\tag{3.6}
```

by the rectangular Bai--Yin theorem.  Therefore `beta K_(r,epsilon)` meets
the paper's strict operator-norm hypothesis for

```math
0<\beta<{\sqrt2\over6}.
\tag{3.7}
```

## 4. Fixed-temperature limit and its strict sign

The R-transform of `mu_c` is

```math
R_{\mu_c}(u)={\sqrt{1+4c^2u^2}-1\over2u}.
\tag{4.1}
```

Since a semicircle of variance `beta^2/2` has R-transform `beta^2u/2`,
the preceding theorem and (2.4) give, for either orientation,

```math
\begin{aligned}
{1\over r}\log\overline Z_r(A_r,\beta/\sqrt r)
 &\longrightarrow\psi(\beta),\\
{1\over2r}\mathbb E_B\log\overline Z_{2r}
 \left(S_{\epsilon,B},{\beta\over\sqrt{2r}}\right)
 &\longrightarrow\psi(\beta/\sqrt2)+{\beta^2\over8}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
```

The expectation passage is legitimate: pathwise convergence holds on the
probability-one event above, while

```math
0\le {1\over N}\log\overline Z_N
\le {1\over2}\|\beta K_{r,\epsilon}\|_{\rm op},
\tag{4.3}
```

and the right side has a uniformly bounded moment of order greater than one.

It follows that the conference compensation margin satisfies

```math
{\mathcal M_r(\beta)\over r}\longrightarrow
L(\beta)=2\{\psi(\beta)-\psi(\beta/\sqrt2)\}-{\beta^2\over4}.
\tag{4.4}
```

Put `u=sqrt(1+4 beta^2)` and `v=sqrt(1+2 beta^2)`.  Direct differentiation
gives

```math
{L'(\beta)\over\beta}
={1\over u+v}-{1\over2}<0
\tag{4.5}
```

for every `beta>0`, while `L(0)=0`.  Hence the limit is strictly negative.
Equivalently, with `gamma(beta)=-L(beta)>0`,

```math
\mathcal M_r(\beta)=-\gamma(\beta)r+o(r),
\qquad
D_{\rm KL}(U\Vert\Pi)=o(r).
\tag{4.6}
```

This is the requested fixed-`beta` scalable theorem for conference children.

## 5. The minimizer transfer is a separate open theorem

The result above does not yet falsify the minimizer-optimized condition.
Equality or `o(r)` proximity of the **scalar child pressures alone is
insufficient**: the parent average also depends on the full Gibbs/output law,
and the reverse-KL identity gives no continuity in pressure gap alone.

Two precise transfer statements would suffice.

### 5.1 Geometric transfer

Suppose an exact pressure minimizer `C_r`, after switching and permutation,
differs from `A_r` on `h_r=o(r^(3/2))` edges.  Flipping `h` edges changes every
child energy by at most `2h`, hence

```math
|\log\overline Z_r(C_r,u)-\log\overline Z_r(A_r,u)|\le2u h_r.
\tag{5.1}
```

The doubled parent has two such internal blocks, so its log pressure changes
by at most `4t h_r`.  Consequently the compensation margins differ by

```math
O\left({\beta h_r\over\sqrt r}\right)=o(r),
\tag{5.2}
```

and (4.6) transfers.  No such Hamming-nearness theorem is known.

### 5.2 Spectral/delocalization transfer

There is also a mechanism that does not require Hamming closeness.  Let `mu`
be a symmetric compactly supported law with variance one.  For real `z`
outside its support,

```math
G_\mu(z)=\mathbb E{z\over z^2-X^2}
\ge {z\over z^2-\mathbb EX^2}
={z\over z^2-1}=G_{\mu_1}(z),
\tag{5.3}
```

by Jensen's inequality.  Both Cauchy transforms decrease on this branch, so
inversion gives

```math
R_\mu(w)\ge R_{\mu_1}(w)
\tag{5.4}
```

throughout their common small positive domain, with equality only when
`X^2=1` almost surely.  Thus the symmetric Bernoulli spectral law uniquely
minimizes the replica-symmetric high-temperature pressure among symmetric
variance-one laws in this domain.

It would therefore be enough to prove that every sequence of exact child
pressure minimizers at one fixed sufficiently small `beta` has uniformly
bounded normalized operator norm, satisfies Assumption 2.9, and has a
symmetric limiting spectral law (with a strong enough norm bound also for
the random-bridge parent).  The conference competitor and (5.3)--(5.4) would
then force the Bernoulli law, and the same random-bridge calculation would
transfer (4.6).

This regularity theorem is not currently known.  It is not supplied by the
ground-state cap, by the first `C_4` coefficient, or by scalar pressure
closeness, and none of those facts controls maximum entries of every fixed
matrix power.  Hence the new primary theorem rigorously closes the
fixed-temperature remainder issue for conference children, while leaving a
well-identified minimizer-regularity bridge rather than silently assuming
that conference signings are the relevant exact children.
