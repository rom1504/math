# An actual two-spin child's signed branch is not variance-concave

2026-09-17. Independent reconstruction of the director's final finite
falsifier. This is a SIGNED-BRANCH statement, not a counterexample to
variance concavity of the full absolute parent cap.

## 1. Exact actual-child identity

Take the actual unit-sign child D_12=+1 and independent Gaussian fields
g_2~N(0,100), g_1~N(0,v). For every real pair of fields,

    max_(y1,y2 in{+-1})[y1 y2+g1 y1+g2 y2]
       =max(1,|g2|)+|g1+S|,
    S=clip(g2,-1,1).                                  (1)

Indeed, first maximize y2 to obtain max_(y1)[g1 y1+|g2+y1|],
then use the elementary maximum-of-two-lines identity. The half-sum
of the two intercepts is max(1,|g2|), and their half-difference is S.

The cavity offset S is independent of g1 and symmetric, but has atoms
at both nonzero endpoints. Thus it is not a mixture of centered uniform
intervals. This is generated by an actual child, not an invented scalar
offset unrelated to Boolean optimization.

## 2. Strict positive variance curvature with rigorous constants

Let F(v)=E|sqrt(v)G+S|. Gaussian heat differentiation gives

    F''(v)=E[phi(S/sqrt(v))(S^2-v)]/(2v^(5/2)),       (2)

where phi is the standard Gaussian density. Differentiation is justified
on any positive compact v-interval since |S|<=1.

Put v=1/4 and p=P(|g2|<1). The density bound phi(0)<2/5 gives
p<2phi(0)/10<2/25. On the endpoint event, whose probability exceeds
23/25, the numerator in(2) is phi(2)*3/4>3/80, using phi(2)>1/20.
On the interior event it is bounded below by -(2/5)(1/4)=-1/10.
Consequently the averaged numerator is strictly larger than

    (23/25)(3/80)-(2/25)(1/10)=53/2000.

Since 2(1/4)^(5/2)=1/16,

    F''(1/4)>106/250=.424.                          (3)

For fully elementary density constants one may use pi>25/8 for
phi(0)<2/5, and pi<22/7 together with e<11/4 for phi(2)>1/20.
The latter follows, for example, from sqrt(2pi)<51/20 and
e^2<(11/4)^2, whose product is less than20.

Thus sufficiently small independent two-point variance mixing around
v=1/4 INCREASES this optimized branch expectation. Symmetry, Gaussian
input fields and exact child optimization do not automatically give
the concavity needed by the positive offset-averaging theorem.

## 3. Necessary scope distinction

The fields in this example have unequal variances100 and1/4; they are
not an unconditional isotropic bridge. More importantly, if BOTH
absolute polarities are optimized with no old offset, then

    max_(y1,y2)|y1 y2+g1 y1+g2 y2|
       =1+max(|g1+g2|,|g1-g2|)
       =1+|g1|+|g2|.                               (4)

Its expectation is1+kappa(sqrt(v)+10), which IS concave in v.
Therefore(3) disproves automatic signed-branch/cavity concavity, not
absolute two-spin-cap concavity, and does not give an asymptotic
obstruction for actual minimizing signings. The distinction matters
because the complete parent comparison retains signed indices before
its final old-code/polarity maximum.
