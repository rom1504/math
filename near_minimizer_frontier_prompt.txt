# Ultimate objective

The eventual goal remains proving whether

    M_n / n^(3/2)

converges, where

    M_n = min_A max_x |sum_(i<j) a_ij x_i x_j|.

Do NOT launch a generic direct attack on this theorem.

The current contextual-response theory has now crossed an important threshold:

- it works on classical benchmarks;
- it has exact compositional laws;
- it has strong leading-scale falsifiers;
- it has structured dense exact-sign families with strict sub-landscape states;
- and, crucially, the sparse-flip/microcanonical construction now gives a
  fully physical exact-sign parent contextual collision at the target
  n^(3/2) scale after every spin optimizes.

The main remaining deficiency is no longer physical realization.

It is:

> We do not know whether any of the structural quantities discovered by the
> theory are forced, constrained, or simplified by near-minimality of the
> quadratic cap.

This campaign should deliberately transition from general theory building to
**near-minimizer structural discovery**.

The campaign has two phases.

======================================================================
PHASE I — FINISH THE CURRENT MULTI-SELECTOR INFORMATION QUESTION
======================================================================

Spend at most ~2–3 serious hours on this unless a major theorem is clearly
within reach.

We currently have one physically realized hidden coherence bit:
active product poles remain asymptotically good, but an exact-sign parent can
still expose a fixed n^(3/2)-scale response difference.

Determine whether this phenomenon amplifies.

Let k_n be the largest number of approximately independent diffuse selector
channels that can be simultaneously encoded while:

1. preserving every currently retained active-product statistic to o(1);
2. using exact-sign sparse edits;
3. admitting balanced/microcanonical physical compilers;
4. producing unconstrained exact-sign parents whose caps/responses are
   separated by c n^(3/2) in each encoded direction.

Try to prove one of:

A. k_n -> infinity;

B. preferably k_n >= n^alpha or k_n >= c log n;

C. a quantitative upper bound such as k_n=O(1), O(log n), or another sharp
   packing ceiling.

Either direction is useful.

If k_n grows:
    this becomes a genuine information-rate obstruction.

If k_n is small:
    the missing coherence may itself be augmentable as a manageable macrostate.

Do not spend the full campaign optimizing constants in this construction.

Once its qualitative rate is understood, move on.

======================================================================
PHASE II — FORCE CONTACT WITH ACTUAL NEAR-MINIMIZERS
======================================================================

This is the main campaign.

Define a near-minimizer class without assuming convergence:

    N_n(epsilon)

      Q(A) <= M_n + epsilon n^(3/2) }

or, where exact M_n is unavailable, use the best certified upper/lower
surrogates carefully and label the distinction.

The central question is:

> Which structural properties are actually constrained across
> N_n(epsilon)?

Do not assume Walsh/Hadamard/product-algebra structure.

The theory has generated many candidate observables. Test which, if any, are
special on true minimizers or near-minimizers.

Candidate quantities include:

- selector/coherence defects;
- higher-product spectral defects;
- projective row-pattern entropy;
- Gram versus higher Fourier information;
- contextual response packing dimension;
- numerical bridge rank at the sqrt(n) threshold;

- holonomy / relational-memory growth;
- near-top response entropy;

- any newly discovered collective quantity suggested by the data.

Do not privilege these merely because they already exist in the repository.

Use actual computed minimizers and serious near-minimizers from the project.

If needed, extend exact/heuristic computations modestly, but the goal is
structural hypothesis generation, not a large new numerical search.

For every candidate P(A), ask:

    Does
        Q(A) <= M_n + epsilon n^(3/2)
    appear to force
        P(A) <= f(epsilon,n)

Look especially for behavior that becomes stronger as epsilon -> 0.

The ideal discovery is a candidate implication

    near-minimal cap

where P is demonstrably weaker than solving the whole optimization problem.

THE MAIN THEOREM TARGET
======================================================================

By the end of the campaign, select at most 2 serious candidate lemmas of the
form

    Q(A) <= M_n + epsilon n^(3/2)
        =>
    P(A) <= f(epsilon) + o_n(1),

or another quantitatively comparable near-minimizer statement.

A useful P must satisfy all of:

1. P is not merely another encoding of Q(A);
2. P can plausibly be proved by tools weaker than solving the original
   minimization;
3. P plugs into a proved response/composition theorem;
4. together with the current theory it yields a genuine quantitative gain,
   ideally o(n^(3/2)) cross-scale loss or subextensive reusable state;
5. existing counterexamples do not already kill it.

Do NOT select more than 2 such lemmas.

Then spend substantial time actually attacking the strongest one.
