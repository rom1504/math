# The sharp subGaussian price of isotropic response suppression

2026-09-17. Proved by elementary tail and moment-generating-function
estimates. The physical realization uses the independently audited
fixed-magnetization slice lemma in
`paper_discrepancy_kernel_slice_repair_2026_09_17.md`, Section 2.
This is a quantitative scope theorem for non-Gaussian variance allocation,
not a claim about the geometry of actual minimizing ground codes.

We use the convention that a centered real random variable X is
K-subGaussian if E exp(tX)<=exp(K t^2/2) for every real t. Logs are natural.

## 1. Necessary scalar cost

Suppose E X^2=1, E|X|=epsilon, and X is K-subGaussian. If
0<epsilon<=1/2, then

    K >= 1/[8 epsilon^2 log(2/epsilon^2)].                 (1)

More sharply, for ANY sequence of such variables with epsilon->0,

    liminf K epsilon^2 log(1/epsilon) >= 1/4.              (2)

The variable need not have a three-point law or be generated by Gaussian
signs. Exact isotropy is used here only through the scalar variance one.

For every threshold L>0, the subGaussian tail estimate and layer-cake
integration give

    E X^2 1_{|X|>L}
      <=2(L^2+2K) exp[-L^2/(2K)],
    1<=L epsilon+2(L^2+2K) exp[-L^2/(2K)].                (3)

To obtain (1), put L=1/(2epsilon) and u=1/(8K epsilon^2).
If u<=1, (1) follows immediately. Otherwise (3) implies

    exp(u)<=(u+1)/(u epsilon^2)<=2/epsilon^2,

which is exactly (1). For (2), suppose along a subsequence
K epsilon^2 log(1/epsilon)<=c<1/4. Choose a in (0,1) with a^2>4c,
and put L=a/epsilon in (3). The tail term is

    O(epsilon^(-2)) exp[-a^2 log(1/epsilon)/(2c)]=o(1).

This gives 1<=a+o(1), a contradiction. No lower bound on a density or
support cardinality is needed.

## 2. A three-point law attains the leading constant

Let J_p equal 0 with probability 1-p and +1,-1 each with probability p/2,
where 0<p<=1. Set X_p=J_p/sqrt(p). Then

    E X_p=0,   E X_p^2=1,   E|X_p|=sqrt(p).

Write ell=log(2/p), and define

    v(p)=min{1, max{2 exp(-ell/2),
                        1/(2ell)+8 log(2)/ell^2}}.

The exact optimal subGaussian proxy K_*(p) for X_p satisfies

    1/[2p ell] <= K_*(p) <= v(p)/p,
    K_*(p) = (1+o(1))/[2p log(2/p)]  as p->0.             (4)

Indeed the unscaled MGF is

    M_p(s)=1+p(cosh(s)-1)<=1+exp(|s|-ell).

For |s|<=ell/2, use cosh(s)-1<=s^2 cosh(s)/2 to get
2 log M_p(s)/s^2<=p cosh(s)<=2 exp(-ell/2).
For |s|>=ell/2,

    2 log M_p(s)/s^2
      <=2(|s|-ell)_+/s^2+2 log(2)/s^2
      <=1/(2ell)+8 log(2)/ell^2.

Symmetry and |J_p|<=1 also give the proxy 1, explaining the minimum
in v(p). Conversely, at s=2ell,
M_p(s)>=p exp(s)/2=exp(ell), proving the lower bound in (4).
Consequently

    K_*(p) [E|X_p|]^2 log(1/E|X_p|) -> 1/4.              (5)

Thus the leading constant in (2) is sharp, not merely its order.

## 3. Exact physical-sign realization with the same leading price

Fix p in (0,1), and take even n tending to infinity. Choose an even
integer M_n with M_n/sqrt(n)->p^(-1/2), and set

    p_n=n/M_n^2.

For all sufficiently large n, sqrt(n)<=M_n<=n and p_n lies in (0,1].
Choose a sign vector h uniformly from the magnetization-zero slice with
probability 1-p_n, and uniformly from each of the slices
sum_i h_i=+M_n and -M_n with probability p_n/2. The law is centered and
permutation invariant. Since E(sum_i h_i)^2=p_n M_n^2=n, its pairwise
off-diagonal correlations vanish exactly: Cov(h)=I_n.

Let P=11^T/n. The fixed-slice exchangeable-pair estimate gives, uniformly
over the selected magnetization and all t perpendicular to 1,

    E[exp(t.h) | sum_i h_i] <= exp(||t||^2).

Combining this conditional bound with Section 2 shows that the physical
law has the matrix subGaussian proxy

    2(I-P)+[v(p_n)/p_n]P.                                 (6)

Its response in the physical all-ones query is exactly

    E|1.h|/sqrt(n)=sqrt(p_n)->sqrt(p).                     (7)

The common scalar proxy is max{2,v(p_n)/p_n}. Mixing with weight n^(-1)
of the independent uniform cube gives full support, preserves exact
isotropy and the same scalar proxy, and changes (7) by at most n^(-1).
Coordinate switching gives the same construction for any prescribed
Boolean query u. It is not an all-query cheap-response assertion.

First let n->infinity for each fixed p, and then p->0. Equations (4),
(6), and (7) attain the necessary leading price (2) among actual,
centered, exactly isotropic, full-support physical sign laws.
The same conclusion can be diagonalized, but no quantitative simultaneous
order limit is needed here.

## 4. Consequence for response-based parent constructions

For any centered isotropic physical law and any Boolean query u,
X=u.h/sqrt(n) has variance one. Hence a uniform response bound epsilon
on a nonempty code forces (1), and asymptotically the sharp lower price
(2). Arbitrarily cheap non-Gaussian response is compatible with a fixed
subGaussian constant when epsilon is fixed first; it is incompatible
with keeping that constant bounded as epsilon->0.

This does not prove that any particular energy-shell bound must incur
the full scalar proxy: anisotropic directions and the joint process can
still matter. It does prevent replacing a Gaussian-band law by an
arbitrarily cheap isotropic law while silently retaining a universal
subGaussian constant. Unlike the bounded-band Gaussian response floor,
this restriction applies to ALL isotropic physical sign laws.
