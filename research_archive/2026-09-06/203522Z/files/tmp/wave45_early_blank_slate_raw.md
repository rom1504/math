# Early Wave 45 blank-slate abstraction audit: raw generation

```math
M_n=\min_{a_{ij}\in\{\pm1\}}\max_{x_i\in\{\pm1\}}
\left|\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}x_ix_j\right|
```

and the question whether `M_n/n^(3/2)` converges.  This file deliberately

## Candidate A: finite-temperature zero-sum interpolation

For a signing `A`, define the normalized soft maximum

```math
F_{n,\gamma}(A)
=\frac1{\gamma n}\log\left(
2^{-n}\sum_{x\in\{\pm1\}^n}
2\cosh\frac{\gamma x^TAx}{2\sqrt n}
\right),
\qquad
v_n(\gamma)=\min_A F_{n,\gamma}(A).
```

The factor `1/2` converts the matrix quadratic form to the original
unordered-edge energy.  Log-sum-exp gives the exact uniform comparison

```math
\left|v_n(\gamma)-\frac{M_n}{n^{3/2}}\right|
\le\frac{\log2+o(1)}\gamma.
```

A concrete sufficient theorem is: for every fixed `gamma`, there are
`epsilon_N(gamma)->0` such that, whenever `n,m>=epsilon_N N` and `N=n+m`,

```math
\left|N v_N(\gamma)-n v_n(\gamma)-m v_m(\gamma)\right|
\le N\epsilon_N(\gamma),
```

and the convergence this implies is locally uniform in `gamma`.  Then
`v_n(gamma)` has a limit for every `gamma`; first send `n` to infinity and
then `gamma` to infinity in the soft-max comparison to prove convergence of
`M_n/n^(3/2)`.  The mathematical mechanism sought is an interpolation of the
optimized log partition function in which a signed bipartite bridge has
sublinear free-energy cost.

## Candidate B: summable cavity derivative

Let `A` attain `M_n`, adjoin a new vertex with an edge-sign vector
`b in {+1,-1}^n`, and call the enlarged signing `A_b`.  A concrete sufficient
theorem is the uniform extension estimate

```math
\min_b\max_{x,t}
\left|H_A(x)+t\sum_i b_ix_i\right|
\le M_n+\frac{3M_n}{2n}+O(n^{1/2-\delta})
```

for some fixed `delta>0`.  Equivalently,

```math
M_{n+1}\le M_n\left(1+\frac3{2n}\right)
+O(n^{1/2-\delta}).
```

After division by `(n+1)^(3/2)`, the positive increments are summable;
boundedness below then forces `M_n/n^(3/2)` to converge.  A route to the
inequality would be a minimax/vector-balancing theorem for one edge row
against the near-maximal spin configurations, with the leading
`3M_n/(2n)` term supplied by the derivative of `n^(3/2)`.

## Candidate C: second-order signed-kernel Gamma compactness

Represent `A` by the step operator `T_A=A/sqrt(n)` on the uniform
`n`-point probability space.  Then

```math
\frac{M(A)}{n^{3/2}}
=\frac12\sup_{f:\,f_i\in\{\pm1\}}
\left|\langle f,T_Af\rangle_{L^2}\right|,
\qquad
\lVert T_A\rVert_{HS}^2=1+o(1).
```

Ordinary graphon convergence sends oscillatory signings to zero and is too
coarse.  The proposed object is instead a two-scale signed-kernel Young
measure retaining both the weak kernel and its quadratic fluctuation law.
A concrete sufficient theorem has three parts:

1. every sequence of signings has a subsequence converging in a compact
   two-scale metric;
2. the displayed Boolean quadratic supremum is continuous in that metric;
3. every limit object has recovery sequences at every sufficiently large
   order, with `o(1)` energy error.

These three statements are an equicoercive Gamma-convergence theorem for the
finite minima.  They imply that all subsequential minima converge to the
minimum of one order-independent limiting functional, hence establish the
desired limit.  The test is whether a nontrivial two-scale topology can make
the Boolean supremum continuous without encoding the entire finite energy
histogram.
