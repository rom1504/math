On the Fourier spectrum of functions
on Boolean cubes

A. Defant, M. Mastyło and A. Pérez
Abstract.

Let ff be a real-valued, degree-dd Boolean function defined on the nn-dimensional Boolean cube {±1}n\{\pm 1\}^{n}, and f⁡(x)=∑S⊂{1,…,d}f^​(S)​∏k∈Sxkf(x)=\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}}\widehat{f}(S)\prod_{k\in S}x_{k} its Fourier-Walsh expansion. The main result states that there is an absolute constant C>0C>0 such that the ℓ2​d/(d+1)\ell_{2d/(d+1)}-sum of the Fourier coefficients of f:{±1}n→[−1,1]f:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\rightarrow[-1,1] is bounded by ≤Cd​log⁡d\leq C^{\sqrt{d\log d}}. It was recently proved that a similar result holds for complex-valued polynomials on the nn-dimensional poly torus 𝕋n\mathbb{T}^{n}, but that in contrast to this a replacement of the nn-dimensional torus 𝕋n\mathbb{T}^{n} by nn-dimensional cube [−1,1]n[-1,1]^{n} leads to a substantially weaker estimate. This in the Boolean case forces us to invent novel techniques which differ from the ones used in the complex or real case. We indicate how our result is linked with several questions in quantum information theory.

1. Introduction

The analysis of maps f:{±1}n→ℝf:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} defined on the Boolean cube {±1}n\{\pm 1\}^{n} is of interest in theoretical computer sciences, signal processing, social choice, combinatorics, graph theory, as well as others. For detailed explanation we refer to the surveys [22] and [26], or the more extensive book [21] and the references therein. The study of these functions is carried out through their polynomial expansion, a canonical representation of ff in terms of basic functions known as Walsh functions, which are defined for every S⊂[n]:={1,…,n}S\subset[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\} as

χS:{±1}n→{±1},χS​(x)=xS:=∏k∈Sxk,x∈{±1}n,\chi_{S}:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}\,,\hskip 8.53581pt\chi_{S}(x)=x^{S}:=\prod_{k\in S}{x_{k}},\hskip 8.53581ptx\in\{\pm 1\}^{n},

where χ∅​(x):=1\chi_{\emptyset}(x):=1 for each x∈{±1}nx\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}. Using elementary linear algebra it can be checked that every ff as above can be written as a unique linear combination of these χS\chi_{S}. Another more profitable point of view to prove this fact is by means of Fourier analysis on groups: we can look at {±1}n\{\pm 1\}^{n} as a compact abelian group endowed with the coordinatewise product and the discrete topology (the Haar measure is then given by the normalized counting measure). This way, for each function f:{±1}n→ℝf:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} we have an integral or expectation given by

𝔼⁡[f]:=12n​∑x∈{±1}nf⁡(x)\mathbb{E}\big[f\big]:=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{x\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}}{f(x)}

The dual group of {±1}n\{\pm 1\}^{n} actually consists of the set of all Walsh functions χS\chi_{S} for S⊂[n]S\subset[n], which let us associate to each such f:{±1}n→ℝf:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} its Fourier-Walsh expansion

(1) f⁡(x)=∑S⊂[n]f^​(S)​xS,x∈{±1}n,f(x)=\sum_{S\subset[n]}{\widehat{f}(S)\,x^{S}}\,,\hskip 8.53581ptx\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}\,,

where the coefficients are explicitly given by f^​(S)=𝔼⁡[f⋅χS]\widehat{f}(S)=\mathbb{E}\big[f\cdot\chi_{S}\big]. Thereby, a nonzero function ff of degree dd satisfies that f^​(S)=0\widehat{f}(S)=0 whenever |S|>d|S|>d, i.e., its Fourier spectrum concentrates on levels less than or equal to dd. We say that ff is dd-homogeneous if moreover f^​(S)=0\widehat{f}(S)=0 provided |S|≠d|S|\neq d, i.e., the Fourier spectrum concentrates at level dd.

1.1. Main result

Blei in [6] proved that for each dd there is a constant C⁡(d)>0C(d)>0 such that for each degree-dd Boolean function f:{±1}n→ℝf\colon\{\pm 1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

(2) (∑S⊂[n]|S|≤d|f^​(S)|2​dd+1)d+12​d≤C⁡(d)​‖f‖{±1}n,\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset[n]\\ |S|\leq d\end{subarray}}{|\widehat{f}(S)|^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2d}}\leq C(d)\,\|f\|_{\{\pm 1\}^{n}},

and here the exponent 2​dd+1\tfrac{2d}{d+1} is best possible (to denote the supremum norm of a scalar-valued function on KK we will often write ‖f‖K\|f\|_{K} instead of ‖f‖∞\|f\|_{\infty}). The best possible constant C⁡(d)C(d) is denoted by

BH{±1}≤d.\operatorname{BH}_{\{\pm 1\}}^{\leq d}\,.

The inequality (2) is a Boolean analog of an important inequality for complex polynomials due to Bohnenblust and Hille [7]. This inequality today forms a fundamental tool of the theory of Dirichlet series ∑nan​n−s\sum_{n}a_{n}n^{-s}, and it states that for every dd there is a (best) constant

BH𝕋≤d\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d}

such for each complex valued degree-dd polynomial P⁡(z)=∑α∈ℕ0n|α|≤daα​zαP(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\\ |\alpha|\leq d\end{subarray}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha} in nn complex variables z1,…,znz_{1},\ldots,z_{n}

(3) (∑α∈ℕ0n|α|≤d|aα|2​dd+1)d+12​d≤BH𝕋≤d​‖f‖𝕋n;\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\\ |\alpha|\leq d\end{subarray}}|a_{\alpha}|^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2d}}\leq\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d}\,\|f\|_{\mathbb{T}^{n}};

again the exponent 2​dd+1\tfrac{2d}{d+1} can not be improved. Originally, this result was proved for dd-homogeneous polynomials only. If we define

BH𝕋=d,\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{=d}\,,

in the by now obvious way, a straight forward trick shows that

(4) BH𝕋=d=BH𝕋≤d;\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{=d}=\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d}\,;

indeed, for a degree-dd polynomial P⁡(z)=∑|α|≤daα​zαP(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq d}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha} on 𝕋n\mathbb{T}^{n} the polynomial Q⁡(z,w)=∑|α|≤daα​zα​wd−|α|Q(z,w)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq d}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}w^{d-|\alpha|} is dd-homogeneous on 𝕋n+1\mathbb{T}^{n+1} with the same set of coefficients and the same sup-norm.

In recent years the Bohnenblust-Hille constants BH𝕋≤d\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d} were undertaken an intensive study. The original proof of (3) and its later improvements give upper estimates which are essentially of the order dd\sqrt{d}^{d}, but in [11] it was proved that BH𝕋≤d\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d} grows at most like 2d\sqrt{2}^{d}, and this was later substantially improved in [5], where the authors show that there is an absolute constant C>0C>0 such that

(5) BH𝕋≤d≤Cd​log⁡d.\displaystyle\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d}\leq C^{\sqrt{d\log d}}.

In this case we say that the growth of BH𝕋≤d\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d} is subexponential. In particular this implies that

(6) lim supd→∞BH𝕋≤dd=1.\displaystyle\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[d]{\operatorname{BH}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\leq d}}=1\,.

A natural question appears related to the asymptotic decay of BH{±1}≤d\operatorname{BH}_{\{\pm 1\}}^{\leq d} as a function in dd in the case when for each n∈ℕn\in\mathbb{N} the group 𝕋n\mathbb{T}^{n} is replaced by the group {±1}n\{\pm 1\}^{n}. Our main result is the following analog of (5).

Theorem 1.1.

There is an absolute constant C>0C>0 such that for each d∈ℕd\in\mathbb{N}

BH{±1}≤d≤Cd​log⁡d.\operatorname{BH}_{\{\pm 1\}}^{\leq d}\leq C^{\sqrt{d\log{d}}}.

In particular, lim supd→∞BH{±1}≤dd=1\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[d]{\operatorname{BH}_{\{\pm 1\}}^{\leq d}}=1.

In section 4.1 we indicate that this result is closely related with several questions in quantum information theory.

Comparing Bohnenblust-Hille constants for complex polynomials on the polytorus 𝕋n\mathbb{T}^{n} with Bohnenblust-Hille constants for real polynomials on the nn-dimensional cube [−1,1]n[-1,1]^{n} indicates that both behave substantially different. Therefore the preceding result for the Boolean cube {±1}n\{\pm 1\}^{n} was not necessarily expected, and in fact its proof has to overcome new technical difficulties. We intend to explain all this in the rest of this introduction.

2. The dd-homogeneous case

4. Remarks and open problems

4.1. Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture

Theorem 1.1 proves that BH{±1}≤d\operatorname{BH}^{\leq d}_{\{\pm 1\}} grows at most subexponetially in the degree dd, but it might even be true that it at most grows polynomially, i.e., BH{±1}≤d≤poly⁡d\operatorname{BH}^{\leq d}_{\{\pm 1\}}\leq\operatorname{poly}{d}.
