# Wave 53C: linear-core entropy and the global-cluster threshold

## Status

This attack does **not** prove the large-core excess lemma or convergence.
It gives a verified exact reformulation that sharply identifies what a
linear-core proof must supply:

1. The diagonal part of the intersection histogram can be removed exactly.
   The desired excess is equivalent to a lower bound on the average weighted
   number of **distinct** favorable selector partners.
2. At `ell=alpha n`, the second eigenvalue tends to a positive constant while
   the diagonal retention and `rho_ell` are exponential.  Thus the target asks
   for a constant-scale normalized retention generated by an exponentially
   large global cluster, with an additive saved margin; it is not a finite
   self-loop effect.
3. There is a concrete high-intersection-tail sufficient lemma.  In a range
   of linear parameters its direct degree consequence is only
   `exp{-Theta(n)}`, whereas the spectral-excess theorem amplifies it to the
   required `exp{-O(L)}` degree.  It is therefore not circular through the
   direct term `rho_ell P_ell`.
4. The known row-cap and exact-ground facts do not provide this cluster.
   The port theorem certifies only polynomially many partners, and every
   one-seed sublinear exchange ball remains exponentially too small.  The
   exact A10 row-optimal family is below `lambda_2(ell)` at each
   `ell=3,4,5`.  This is a finite scoped obstruction, not an asymptotic
   counterexample and not an obstruction to later cap enlargement.

All finite identities and asymptotic numerical checks are reproduced by
`tmp/linear_core_entropy_r53_check.py`; its output is saved in
`tmp/linear_core_entropy_r53_check.out`.  No external theorem is imported.

## 1. Exact removal of the diagonal

Fix `n,m`, a selector-independent center class `C`, and a nonempty favorable
family

```math
F_z\subseteq\binom{[n]}m,\qquad r_z=|F_z|,\qquad
a_z=\frac{r_z}{N},\qquad N=\binom nm.
```

For `2<=ell<m`, let

```math
H_z(j)=|\{(S,T)\in F_z^2:|S\cap T|=j\}|,
```

where pairs are ordered.  Put

```math
d_\ell=\binom m\ell\binom{n-\ell}{m-\ell},\qquad
b_\ell=\binom{m-2}{\ell-2}\binom{n-\ell-2}{m-\ell},
```

and

```math
h_\ell=\frac{\binom m\ell}{d_\ell}
=\binom{n-\ell}{m-\ell}^{-1}.
```

The diagonal of `H_z` is exactly `H_z(m)=r_z`.  Define the average weighted
number of distinct favorable partners by

```math
\Delta_{\ell,z}
=\frac1{r_z}\sum_{j=\ell}^{m-1}H_z(j)\binom j\ell
=\frac1{r_z}\sum_{S\in F_z}\sum_{\substack{T\in F_z\\T\ne S}}
 \binom{|S\cap T|}{\ell}.
\tag{R53C.1}
```

Splitting the exact histogram transform into diagonal and off-diagonal parts
gives the **Verified identity**

```math
\boxed{p_\ell(z)=h_\ell+\frac{\Delta_{\ell,z}}{d_\ell}.}
\tag{R53C.2}
```

Let

```math
\omega_C(z)=\frac{r_z^2}{\sum_{x\in C}r_x^2}.
```

This is exactly the `a_z^2` center law in `P_ell`.  Since
`lambda_2(ell)=b_ell/d_ell`, averaging (R53C.2) gives the **Verified exact
weighted-neighbor criterion**

```math
\boxed{
P_\ell-\lambda_2(\ell)
=\frac{\mathbb E_{\omega_C}\Delta_{\ell,z}
       -\{b_\ell-\binom m\ell\}}{d_\ell}.}
\tag{R53C.3}
```

Consequently, for every `epsilon>0`,

```math
\boxed{
P_\ell-\lambda_2(\ell)\ge\epsilon
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb E_{\omega_C}\Delta_{\ell,z}
\ge b_\ell-\binom m\ell+d_\ell\epsilon.}
\tag{R53C.4}
```

The global multiplicity is not merely an artifact of the sufficient
conditions below.  Let

```math
N_z(\ell)=\frac1{r_z}
 |\{(S,T)\in F_z^2:S\ne T,\ |S\cap T|\ge\ell\}|.
```

Because every contributing pair has weight at most `binom(m,ell)`, one has
`Delta_{ell,z}<=binom(m,ell)N_z(ell)`.  Hence (R53C.4) implies the
**Verified necessary partner bound**

```math
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega_C}N_z(\ell)
\ge\frac{\lambda_2(\ell)-h_\ell+\epsilon}{h_\ell}.}
\tag{R53C.4a}
```

At a linear core the right side is `exp{Theta(n)}`.  Thus any proof of the
spectral target necessarily establishes exponentially many distinct
large-overlap partners on this size-biased average; a polynomial local star
cannot be enough.

This is more informative than leaving the diagonal inside the binomial
transform.  An `ell`-packing, meaning that no two distinct members of a fiber
share `ell` points, has `Delta_{ell,z}=0` and retention exactly `h_ell`.
Thus nonemptiness and self-loops alone cannot meet (R53C.4) once
`h_ell<lambda_2(ell)`.

## 2. Collision entropy and a concrete core dictionary

For an `ell`-core `R`, write

```math
c_z(R)=|\{S\in F_z:R\subseteq S\}|,
\qquad
q_z(R)=\frac{c_z(R)}{r_z\binom m\ell}.
```

The law `q_z` is obtained by choosing a uniform favorable selector and then a
uniform `ell`-subset of it.  Let `U_ell` be uniform on the
`Q_ell=binom(n,ell)` cores and define the effective collision support

```math
K_{2,z}^{\rm eff}=\left(\sum_Rq_z(R)^2\right)^{-1}.
```

The core-load identity becomes the **Verified collision-entropy identity**

```math
\boxed{
p_\ell(z)=\frac{a_zQ_\ell}{K_{2,z}^{\rm eff}}
=a_z\exp\{D_2(q_z\Vert U_\ell)\}.}
\tag{R53C.5}
```

Here `D_2(q||U)=log(Q_ell sum_R q(R)^2)`.  This supplies an exact compression
interpretation: retention is family density times the Renyi-2 compression of
the induced core law.

There is also a directly testable sufficient certificate.  If a core
dictionary `B_z` carries `q_z(B_z)>=1-delta_z`, then Cauchy--Schwarz gives

```math
p_\ell(z)\ge
\frac{a_zQ_\ell(1-\delta_z)^2}{|B_z|}.
\tag{R53C.6}
```

Therefore the aggregate inequality

```math
\mathbb E_{\omega_C}
\frac{a_zQ_\ell(1-\delta_z)^2}{|B_z|}
\ge\lambda_2(\ell)+\epsilon
\tag{R53C.7}
```

would prove the desired excess.  This is not supplied by a vocabulary change:
one must construct dictionaries that compress almost all favorable-generated
large cores at the precise scale in (R53C.7).

Nor is (R53C.7) automatically the saved degree in disguise through the
elementary core-load bound.  Since every atom of `q_z` is at most
`1/binom(m,ell)`, a dictionary carrying `1-delta_z` mass has at least
`(1-delta_z)binom(m,ell)` members.  The immediate density scale visible from
such a certificate is therefore `rho_ell`, which is exponentially smaller
than the project target at a linear core.  The gain from a positive saved
excess still comes from (10.1261), not from `rho_ell P_ell`.

## 3. A high-intersection cluster lemma that would suffice

For `ell<=s<m`, define the off-diagonal upper-intersection tail

```math
\theta_z^\circ(s)
=\frac1{r_z^2}
 |\{(S,T)\in F_z^2:S\ne T,\ |S\cap T|\ge s\}|,
```

and

```math
\Gamma_{\ell,s}=\frac{\binom s\ell}{\binom m\ell}.
```

Every pair in this tail contributes at least `binom(s,ell)` to (R53C.1), so

```math
p_\ell(z)
\ge h_\ell+\frac{a_z\Gamma_{\ell,s}}{\rho_\ell}
\theta_z^\circ(s).
\tag{R53C.8}
```

This proves the following **Verified sufficient global-cluster lemma**:

```math
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega_C}[a_z\theta_z^\circ(s)]
\ge\frac{\rho_\ell}{\Gamma_{\ell,s}}
\{\lambda_2(\ell)-h_\ell+\epsilon\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_\ell-\lambda_2(\ell)\ge\epsilon.}
\tag{R53C.9}
```

Equivalently, let

```math
N_z(s)=\frac1{r_z}
 |\{(S,T)\in F_z^2:S\ne T,\ |S\cap T|\ge s\}|
```

be the average number of high-overlap partners of one favorable selector.
Then the concrete condition

```math
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega_C}N_z(s)
\ge
\frac{b_\ell-\binom m\ell+d_\ell\epsilon}
     {\binom s\ell}}
\tag{R53C.10}
```

implies the target.  Formula (R53C.4) is the exact weighted condition;
(R53C.10) is a stronger but simpler one-threshold cluster condition.

This identifies a genuinely signing-specific possible theorem: favorable
child grounds at a controlled row cap must occur in a global cluster with
the exponentially large partner count (R53C.10).  A forbidden-intersection,
compression, or exchange theorem would materially advance the problem only
if it proves this kind of quantitative cluster statement; merely renaming
`P_ell` as entropy or stability does not.

There is an important hypothesis audit for any future literature import.
A forbidden-intersection or stability theorem that assumes in advance
`|F_z|/N>=exp{-O(L)}` already assumes the center degree sought here.  A theorem
starting only after that density threshold is therefore circular for this
application, even if its conclusion gives strong intersection structure.
No such external theorem is used in this memo.

## 4. Linear-core asymptotics and normalization audit

Let

```math
\frac mn\longrightarrow p\in(1/2,1),\qquad
\frac\ell n\longrightarrow\alpha\in(0,p).
```

The exact eigenvalue is

```math
\lambda_2(\ell)
=\frac{\ell(\ell-1)(n-m)(n-m-1)}
       {m(m-1)(n-\ell)(n-\ell-1)},
```

and hence the **Verified limit** is

```math
\boxed{
\lambda_2(\ell)\longrightarrow
\vartheta_{p,\alpha}^2,
\qquad
\vartheta_{p,\alpha}
=\frac{\alpha(1-p)}{p(1-\alpha)}\in(0,1).}
\tag{R53C.11}
```

Likewise `lambda_1(ell)->vartheta_{p,alpha}`, so the denominator in the
spectral-excess extraction has the audited scale

```math
(1-\lambda_1)+n(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)
=n\vartheta_{p,\alpha}(1-\vartheta_{p,\alpha})+o(n).
\tag{R53C.11a}
```

It therefore costs only a polynomial factor after a saved excess is proved.

Writing `H(x)=-x log x-(1-x)log(1-x)`, Stirling's formula gives

```math
\boxed{
-\log\rho_\ell
=nI_\rho(p,\alpha)+o(n),
\qquad
I_\rho(p,\alpha)
=H(\alpha)-pH(\alpha/p)>0,}
\tag{R53C.12}
```

and

```math
\boxed{
-\log h_\ell
=n(1-\alpha)
 H\!\left(\frac{p-\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)+o(n).}
\tag{R53C.13}
```

Both remainders improve to `O(log n)` when `m=pn+O(1)` and
`ell=alpha n+O(1)`.

Thus `rho_ell` and the diagonal `h_ell` are exponential, whereas
`lambda_2(ell)` stays at a positive constant.  For
`epsilon=exp{-O(L)}` with `L=n^(3/4-c)=o(n)`, (R53C.4) becomes

```math
\frac{\mathbb E_{\omega_C}\Delta_{\ell,z}}{d_\ell}
\ge\lambda_2(\ell)+e^{-O(L)}-h_\ell
=\vartheta_{p,\alpha}^2+o(1)+e^{-O(L)}-e^{-\Theta(n)}.
\tag{R53C.14}
```

This is constant-scale off-diagonal retention with a saved one-sided margin.
At the same time the direct hybrid term is only
`rho_ell P_ell=exp{-Theta(n)}` when `P_ell` is constant.
An entropy-rate statement that does not determine the sign at the constant
threshold is insufficient, although any robust constant surplus would of
course be more than enough.

The cluster criterion is genuinely spectral in a nonempty linear parameter
range.  Let `s/n -> beta` with `alpha<beta<p`.  Then

```math
-\log\Gamma_{\ell,s}
=nI_\Gamma(p,\alpha,\beta)+o(n),
\qquad
I_\Gamma=pH(\alpha/p)-\beta H(\alpha/\beta).
\tag{R53C.15}
```

Here the error is `O(log n)` under bounded integer rounding of all three
linear parameters.

Choose `beta` sufficiently close to `p` that
`I_Gamma<I_rho`.  The right side of (R53C.9) is then

```math
\exp\{-n(I_\rho-I_\Gamma)+o(n)\}.
```

Since `a_z theta_z^circ<=a_z`, the direct density consequence is only
`M>=exp{-Theta(n)}`, far below `exp{-O(L)}`.  Nevertheless (R53C.9) and
(10.1261) give `M>=exp{-O(L)}/poly(n)`.  This explicitly checks that the
proposed cluster input does not already prove the desired degree through
`rho_ell P_ell`.

For example, at

```math
p=3/5,\qquad \alpha=3/10,\qquad\beta=9/20,
```

the audited constants are

```math
\vartheta^2=0.0816326530\ldots,\qquad
I_\rho=0.1949759937\ldots,\qquad
I_\Gamma=0.1294569326\ldots,
```

so the direct incidence scale in (R53C.9) is
`exp{-(0.0655190611...+o(1))n}`.  In partner-count language,
(R53C.10) still demands exponentially many partners per typical selector:

```math
\frac1n\log\frac{b_\ell}{\binom s\ell}
\longrightarrow
pH(\alpha/p)+(1-\alpha)
H\!\left(\frac{p-\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)
-\beta H(\alpha/\beta)>0.
\tag{R53C.16}
```

## 5. What the known signing structure does and does not supply

The maximal-selector port theorem from Wave 52 certifies only `O(n)`
one-exchange selector labels at one reached center.  Such selectors do have
intersection `m-1`, but (R53C.16) requires `exp{Theta(n)}` high-overlap
partners per typical selector at a linear core.  Even an entire one-seed
`o(n)`-radius Johnson ball has only `exp{o(n)}` labels and cannot meet this
threshold.  Hence the exact port inequalities are qualitatively at the right
intersection location but exponentially short in multiplicity.

The row cap controls the center `z`, not the intersection histogram within
`F_z`.  Exact child-ground status supplies local one-spin and port
inequalities, but no known identity lower-bounds the global off-diagonal term
in (R53C.3).  The existing abstract singleton-fiber construction is therefore
still a valid obstruction to proofs using only cap nesting, nonemptiness, and
local smoothness: it has `Delta_{ell,z}=0` at every scale.

There is also an exact-minimizer finite warning.  For the stored order-ten
minimizer at `m=6`, the two centers at the optimal row cap `R=10` have the
same five-selector exact-ground family, with ordered intersection histogram

```math
H(2)=10,\qquad H(4)=10,\qquad H(6)=5.
```

The exact large-core data are

| `ell` | `Delta_ell` | `b_ell-C(m,ell)` | `p_ell` | `lambda_2(ell)` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 8 | 20 | `1/25` | `2/35` |
| 4 | 2 | 21 | `17/225` | `4/25` |
| 5 | 0 | 6 | `1/5` | `2/5` |

Thus the attained row-optimal exact-ground family has negative excess at all
three audited genuinely larger cores.  At `ell=2` it has positive excess,
but there `h_2=lambda_2(2)=1/70`, so it is not evidence for the asymptotic
linear-core mechanism.  This finite example **falsifies only** a universal
same-cap implication from row optimality plus exact-ground status to
large-core nonnegative excess.  It does not falsify an asymptotic aggregate
lemma, the existence of another core scale, or controlled cap enlargement.

## 6. Exact remaining lemma and research judgment

The minimal exact signing-specific target within this route is (R53C.4) at a
controlled project row cap, with a scale satisfying
`-log(rho_ell)=omega(L)` and `epsilon=exp{-O(L)}`.  A more geometric but
stronger target is (R53C.10) for some linear `ell=alpha n` and
`s=beta n` with `I_Gamma<I_rho`.

Either target would prove the Wave 52 saved spectral excess, after which
(10.1261) supplies a saved-degree center and the existing restriction chain
proves convergence.  Present exact-ground and row-cap structure does not
supply either target.  Any viable continuation must prove global exponential
multiplicity of coherently overlapping child-ground selectors—perhaps through
cancellation-stable linear-radius exchange, a genuine compression theorem, or
an all-seed incidence count.  Local ports, one-seed balls, finite self-loops,
and entropy terminology alone are insufficient.

The route remains **Open**, with a sharper assessment: linear-core spectral
excess is a global clustering theorem at constant retention scale, not merely
a saved rare-collision theorem.  No evidence in this audit makes it stronger
than the direct K-functional alternative; its appeal is the exact extraction
once such clustering is available, while its main obstruction is now the
required exponential partner multiplicity.
