# Wave 18 candidate routes

The primary target is Ledger (10.617): at one temperature, select deletions
whose soft cavity reward follows the root `r^(3/2)` profile up to a universal
constant.  A9 proves zero slack impossible but does not obstruct constant
slack.  Independent spatial work should use the switching-orbit and paired
raw-shore results without pretending that raw resource is already allocated.

Scores are promise / immediate falsifiability / independence, on five-point
scales.

1. **Near-ground entropy/decrement dichotomy (5/5/4).**  Express each cavity
   reward through oriented deficit polynomials.  Prove that some deletion has
   either a large zero-temperature decrement or a sufficiently favorable
   parent/child near-ground entropy ratio.  Use projection/Shearer inequalities
   for nested deficit layers, allowing the A9-mandated constant loss.

2. **Windowed pressure descent (5/4/5).**  Replace the stronger stepwise
   condition (10.617) by a cumulative pressure-drop condition across a
   fixed-proportion order window.  Freeze one beta over the whole window and
   use random restrictions, averaging, or a shortest-path argument to seek an
   `O(n-m)` unnormalized error, which already yields the adaptive tail.

3. **Root-frozen mesoscopic temperature (4/5/4).**  Take
   `beta_n=b/sqrt(n)` (or `b_n/sqrt(n)`) fixed throughout
   `[rho n,n]`.  Re-derive the entropy error and use a high-/intermediate-
   temperature expansion to estimate cavity rewards.  This avoids changing
   beta at every deletion while permitting the entropy error to vanish after
   optimizing `b`.

4. **Gibbs row-field inequality (4/5/3).**  Starting from
   `kappa_i=beta^(-1) log E cosh(2 beta b_i.y)`, relate `max_i kappa_i` to
   Gibbs internal energy, entropy, and the signed local fields.  Seek a
   deterministic inequality with leading coefficient `3 alpha/2` and a
   constant remainder; exhaustively test any candidate on A8/A9.

5. **Deterministic free-energy universality (4/3/5).**  Search primary
   spin-glass/free-energy literature for comparison theorems applying to
   deterministic low-operator-norm sign matrices at `beta=Theta(1/sqrt n)`.
   Audit whether competitive signings meet the hypotheses and whether the
   resulting cavity increment has the required constant.

6. **Cavity worst-case finite program (4/5/3).**  Enumerate or optimize the
   maximum cavity shortfall over all small minimizers and their restrictions,
   at a grid and symbolically where possible.  Identify the likely universal
   K, structural extremizers, and a candidate inequality; do not promote
   numerical evidence to proof.

7. **Switching-subgroup construction (4/4/5).**  Build a group with about
   `sqrt(n)` controlled character cells from correlated near grounds.  Use
   additive energy/Fourier identities to force leading incompatible block
   norms while keeping the antipodal orbit gap subleading, directly targeting
   (10.600).

8. **Orbit-variance dichotomy (4/4/4).**  At a cross ground, either the
   switching-orbit variance is nearly maximal and (10.610)--(10.611) close
   the active defect, or its Fourier mass is spread in a way that produces a
   different large-Q witness or a refinable partition.  Quantify the exact
   deficit rather than use average-state variance.

9. **Allocate the non-endpoint paired shore (4/4/5).**  Generalize the
   endpoint path-cover polytope to a cut selected by two near-cross states.
   Determine whether the raw four-bucket total from (10.603) can receive a
   conserved allocation and satisfy proper causal Hall cuts, or find a finite
   obstruction beyond A8/A9.

10. **Conference-channel closure (3/5/5).**  Attack the exact 256-state
    separable A9-conference model left by (10.619), seeking either a Boolean
    witness above constant one or a counterexample.  This is now mainly a
    falsification route, not the primary convergence route.

Selected routes: 1, 2, and 7.  Route 1 attacks one-step cavity selection,
Route 2 attacks the strictly weaker cumulative comparison actually needed
for convergence, and Route 7 independently attacks the spatial partition
problem.
