Abstract.

It was recently proved by Bayart et al. that the complex polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille inequality is subexponential. We show that, for real scalars, this does no longer hold. Moreover, we show that, if Dℝ,mD_{\mathbb{R},m} stands for the real Bohnenblust–Hille constant for mm-homogeneous polynomials, then limsupmDℝ,m1/m=2.\displaystyle\lim\sup_{m}D_{\mathbb{R},m}^{1/m}=2.

Key words and phrases: 
Bohnenblust–Hille inequality, Absolutely summing operators.

1. Introduction

If EE is a Banach space, real or complex, we say that PP is a homogeneous polynomial on EE of degree m∈ℕm\in{\mathbb{N}} if there exists an mm-linear form LL on EmE^{m} such that P⁡(x)=L⁡(x,…,x)P(x)=L(x,\ldots,x) for all x∈Ex\in E. It is customary to denote by L^\widehat{L} the restriction of LL to the diagonal of EmE^{m}. An old and widely known algebraic result establishes that for every homogeneous polynomial PP of degree mm on EE there exists a unique symmetric mm-linear form LL on EmE^{m}, called the polar of PP, such that P=L^P=\widehat{L}. We denote by 𝒫(mE){\mathcal{P}}(^{m}E), ℒ(mE){\mathcal{L}}(^{m}E) and ℒs(mE){\mathcal{L}}^{s}(^{m}E) the spaces of continuous mm-homogeneous polynomials, continuous mm-linear forms and continuous symmetric mm-linear forms on EE respectively. It is well known that homogeneous polynomials or mm-multilinear forms are continuous on EE if and only if they are bounded, respectively, over the unit ball 𝖡E{\mathsf{B}}_{E} of EE or 𝖡Em{\mathsf{B}}_{E}^{m}. In that case

‖P‖:\displaystyle\|P\|: =sup{|P⁡(x)|:x∈𝖡E},\displaystyle=\sup\{|P(x)|:x\in{\mathsf{B}}_{E}\},
‖L‖:\displaystyle\|L\|: =sup{|L(x1,…,xm)|:x1,…xm∈𝖡E},\displaystyle=\sup\{|L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})|:x_{1},\ldots x_{m}\in{\mathsf{B}}_{E}\},

define a norm in 𝒫(mE){\mathcal{P}}(^{m}E) and ℒ(mE){\mathcal{L}}(^{m}E) respectively. If P∈𝒫(mE)P\in{\mathcal{P}}(^{m}E), we shall refer to ‖P‖\|P\| as the polynomial norm of PP in EE. This norm is very difficult to compute in most cases, for which reason it would be interesting to obtain reasonably good estimates on it. The ℓp\ell_{p} norm of the coefficients of a given polynomial on 𝕂n{\mathbb{K}}^{n} (𝕂=ℝ{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{R}} or ℂ{\mathbb{C}}) has also been widely used in mathematics and is much easier to handle. Observe that an mm-homogeneous polynomial in 𝕂n{\mathbb{K}}^{n} can be written as

P⁡(x)=∑|α|=maα​xα,P(x)={\sum\limits_{\left|\alpha\right|=m}}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha},

where x=(x1,…,xn)∈𝕂nx=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{K}}^{n}, α=(α1,…,αn)∈(ℕ∪{0})n\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{n}, |α|=α1+⋯+αn|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n} and xα=x1α1⋯xnαnx^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}. Thus we define the ℓp\ell_{p} norm of PP, with p≥1p\geq 1, as If EE has finite dimension nn, then the polynomial norm ∥⋅∥\|\cdot\| and the ℓp\ell_{p} norm |⋅|p|\cdot|_{p} (p≥1p\geq 1) are equivalent, and therefore there exist constants k⁡(m,n),K⁡(m,n)>0k(m,n),K(m,n)>0 such that

(1.1) k⁡(m,n)​|P|p≤‖P‖≤K⁡(m,n)|P|p,k(m,n)|P|_{p}\leq\|P\|\leq K(m,n)|P|_{p},

for all P∈𝒫(mE)P\in{\mathcal{P}}(^{m}E). The latter inequalities may provide a good estimate on ‖P‖\|P\| as long as we know the exact value of the best possible constants k⁡(m,n)k(m,n) and K⁡(m,n)K(m,n) appearing in (1.1).

The problem presented above is an extension of the the well known polynomial Bohnenblust-Hille inequality (polynomial BH inequality for short). It was proved in [4] that there exists a constant Dm≥1D_{m}\geq 1 such that for every P∈𝒫(mℓ∞n)P\in{\mathcal{P}}(^{m}\ell_{\infty}^{n}) we have

(1.2) |P|2​mm+1≤Dm​‖P‖.|P|_{\frac{2m}{m+1}}\leq D_{m}\|P\|.

Observe that (1.2) coincides with the first inequality in (1.1) for p=2​mm+1p=\frac{2m}{m+1} except for the fact that DmD_{m} in (1.2) can be chosen in such a way that it is independent from the dimension nn. Actually Bohnenblust and Hille showed that 2​mm+1\frac{2m}{m+1} is optimal in (1.2) in the sense that for p<2​mm+1p<\frac{2m}{m+1}, any constant DD fitting in the inequality

|P|p≤D​‖P‖,|P|_{p}\leq D\|P\|,

for all P∈𝒫(mℓ∞n)P\in{\mathcal{P}}(^{m}\ell_{\infty}^{n}) depends necessarily on nn.

The polynomial and multilinear Bohnenblust–Hille inequalities were overlooked for a long period (see [Boas]) and were only rediscovered in the last few years; now these inequalities can be seen as an extension of the successful theory of absolutely summing operators (see [6]) and have fundamental importance in different fields of Mathematics and Physics, such as Operator Theory, Fourier and Harmonic Analysis, Complex Analysis, Analytic Number Theory and Quantum Information Theory (see [1, Boas, 3, 5, 8, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12] and references therein).

The best constants in (1.2) may depend on whether we consider the real or the complex version of ℓ∞n\ell_{\infty}^{n}, which motivates the following definition

D𝕂,m:=inf{D>0:|P|2​mm+1≤D​‖P‖, for all n∈ℕ and P∈𝒫(mℓ∞n)}.D_{{\mathbb{K}},m}:=\inf\left\{D>0:\text{$|P|_{\frac{2m}{m+1}}\leq D\|P\|$, for all $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and $P\in{\mathcal{P}}(^{m}\ell_{\infty}^{n})$}\right\}.

If we restrict attention to 𝒫(mℓ∞n){\mathcal{P}}(^{m}\ell_{\infty}^{n}) for some n∈ℕn\in{\mathbb{N}}, then we define

D𝕂,m(n):=inf{D>0:|P|2​mm+1≤D​‖P‖ for all P∈𝒫(mℓ∞n)}.D_{{\mathbb{K}},m}(n):=\inf\left\{D>0:\text{$|P|_{\frac{2m}{m+1}}\leq D\|P\|$ for all $P\in$}{\mathcal{P}}(^{m}\ell_{\infty}^{n})\right\}.

Note that D𝕂,m​(n)≤D𝕂,mD_{{\mathbb{K}},m}(n)\leq D_{{\mathbb{K}},m} for all n∈ℕn\in{\mathbb{N}}.

It was recently shown in [1] that the complex polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille inequality is, at most, subexponential, i.e., for any ε>0\varepsilon>0, there is a constant Cε>0C_{\varepsilon}>0 such that Dℂ,m≤Cε​(1+ε)mD_{{\mathbb{C}},m}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{m} for all positive integers mm. The main motivation of this paper are the following problems:

  • (I)

    Is the real polynomial BH inequality subexponential?

  • (II)

    What is the optimal growth of the real polynomial BH inequality?

We provide the final answer to these previous problems by showing that

limsupmDℝ,m1/m=2.\lim\sup_{m}D_{\mathbb{R},m}^{1/m}=2.

2. The upper estimate

The proof of the subexponentiality of the complex BH inequality given in [1] lies heavily in arguments restricted to complex scalars (it uses, for instance estimates from [2] for complex scalars); so a simple adaptation for the real case does not work. The calculation of the upper estimate of the BH inequality is quite simplified by the use of complexifications of polynomials. In particular we are interested in the following deep result due to Visser [13], which generalizes and old result of Chebyshev:

Theorem 2.1 (Visser, [13], 1946).

Let

P(y1,…,yn)=∑|α|≤maαy1α1⋯ynαn,P(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}},

with α=(α1,…,αn)\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}), |α|=α1+⋯+αn|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}, be a polynomial of total degree at most m∈ℕm\in\mathbb{N} in the variables y1,…,yny_{1},\ldots,y_{n} and with real coefficients aαa_{\alpha}. Suppose 0≤k≤m0\leq k\leq m and PkP_{k} is the homogeneous polynomial of degree kk defined by

Pk(y1,…,yn)=∑|α|=kaαy1α1⋯ynαn.P_{k}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

Then we have

maxz1,…,zn∈𝔻⁡|Pm​(z1,…,zn)|≤2m−1⋅maxx1,…,xn∈[−1,1]⁡|P⁡(x1,…,xn)|,\max_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}}|P_{m}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})|\leq 2^{m-1}\cdot\max_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in[-1,1]}|P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|,

where 𝔻{\mathbb{D}} stands for the closed unit disk in ℂ{\mathbb{C}}. In particular, if PP is homogeneous, then

maxz1,…,zn∈𝔻⁡|P⁡(z1,…,zn)|≤2m−1⋅maxx1,…,xn∈[−1,1]⁡|P⁡(x1,…,xn)|.\max_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}}|P(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})|\leq 2^{m-1}\cdot\max_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in[-1,1]}|P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|.

Moreover, the constant 2m−12^{m-1} cannot be replaced by any smaller one.

Let P:ℓ∞n​(ℝ)→ℝP:\ell_{\infty}^{n}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R} be an mm-homogeneous polynomial

and consider the complexification Pℂ:ℓ∞n​(ℂ)→ℂP_{\mathbb{C}}:\ell_{\infty}^{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{C} of PP given by

Pℂ​(z)=∑|α|=maα​zα.P_{\mathbb{C}}(z)={\sum\limits_{\left|\alpha\right|=m}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}.

From Theorem 2.1 above we know that

(2.1) ‖Pℂ‖≤2m−1​‖P‖.\left\|P_{\mathbb{C}}\right\|\leq 2^{m-1}\left\|P\right\|.

Thus, since the complex polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille inequality is subexponential, for all ε>0\varepsilon>0 there exists Cε>1C_{\varepsilon}>1 such that

(2.2) |P|2​mm+1=|Pℂ|2​mm+1≤Cε​(1+ε)m​‖Pℂ‖|P|_{\frac{2m}{m+1}}=|P_{\mathbb{C}}|_{\frac{2m}{m+1}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{m}\left\|P_{\mathbb{C}}\right\|

and combining (2.1) and (2.2) we conclude that

limsupmDℝ,m1/m≤2.\lim\sup_{m}D_{\mathbb{R},m}^{1/m}\leq 2.

As we mentioned earlier, Bayart et al. proved, recently, that the complex polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille inequality is subexponential (see [1]). The following result shows that the exponential growth of the real polynomial BH inequality is sharp in a very strong way: the exponential bound can not be reduced in any sense, i.e., there is an exponential lower bound for Dℝ,mD_{\mathbb{R},m} which holds for every m∈ℕm\in\mathbb{N}.

3. The lower estimate

Using our previous results, in order to show that limsupmDℝ,m1/m=2\lim\sup_{m}D_{\mathbb{R},m}^{1/m}=2, we just need the following theorem:

The next result shows that the real version of Proposition 4.1 is not valid; we stress that Theorem 2.2 cannot be used here since it uses polynomials in a growing number of variables.
