# Retrieval-panel report schema

This temporary schema standardizes reports without supplying archive
vocabulary to independent researchers.

## Domain researcher report

1. **Native translation.** Define the problem in the field's own objects and
   check every factor of two and normalization.
2. **Architectures.** Give at most three mechanisms that are mathematically
   distinct, not vocabulary variants.
3. **Best implication.** State

   ```math
   \text{named imported theorem(s)}+\boxed{L}\Longrightarrow
   \text{convergence or genuine nonconvergence}.
   ```

4. **Exact lemma `L`.** Include quantifiers, scales, uniformity, and error
   rates.
5. **Strictness.** Identify information needed for `L` and prove it is less
   than full Boolean/coset optimization; description length alone is not
   enough.
6. **Falsifier.** Give an exact finite test, structural counterexample, or
   asymptotic obstruction that would stop the route.
7. **Imported leverage.** Name the closest theorem and list every hypothesis
   not yet verified.
8. **Circularity audit.** List hidden assumptions that would reconstruct the
   desired optimum, full coset histogram, or an equivalent recurrence.
9. **Confidence.** Give separate estimates for truth of `L`, tractability of
   `L`, and the whole implication.

## Archive-verifier report

For each frozen proposal record:

- exact mathematical mechanism, stripped of terminology;
- closest ledger sections and artifact files;
- whether a proved no-go actually contains all of its hypotheses;
- whether it reconstructs parent maximization or the full coset histogram;
- whether channels are separately paid or a fixed leading loss occurs;
- whether it is uniform on arbitrary near-minimizers and all large orders;
- whether the advertised state becomes exponential at required accuracy;
- classification: `A`, `B`, `C`, or `D`;
- one decisive reason and one exact surviving issue;
- for `A/B`, the minimum revision request.

Classification meanings:

- `A`: genuinely new and survives existing obstructions;
- `B`: new mechanism with one clearly isolated unresolved issue;
- `C`: equivalent to an archived obligation;
- `D`: rigorously obstructed or falsified.

## Candidate card

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Domain | Home field and foreign packet |
| Imported theorem(s) | Exact names, hypotheses, primary URLs |
| Problem translation | Checked equation, including normalization |
| Proposed mechanism | One paragraph |
| Exact missing lemma | Fully quantified theorem statement |
| Why strictly weaker | Information/recovery argument |
| Ledger collisions | Sections, artifacts, implication comparison |
| Falsification test | Decisive finite or structural test |
| Specialist confidence | Truth / tractability / implication |
| Verifier confidence | Novelty / survival |
| Director judgment | Execute, hold, or reject with reason |

## Cross-domain critique

Answer only:

1. What is the strongest mathematical reason the architecture might fail?
2. What is the strongest theorem from the critic's field that might rescue it?
3. Does the rescue preserve zero-temperature/extreme behavior, all-order
   realizability, joint cancellation, and a coefficient with no fixed loss?
4. What one test settles the disagreement fastest?
